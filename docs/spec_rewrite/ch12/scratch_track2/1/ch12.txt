8588 [] ## 12. Runtime periphery: lifecycle and orchestration
8589 [] 
8590 [] This chapter specifies the machinery that drives the loop itself, taking the
8591 [] loop of [§10][s10] and the data plane of [§11][s11] as given. [§12.1][s12-1] covers the control plane;
8592 [] [§12.2][s12-2] loop scheduling, in turn its wait primitive, its yields, the thread budget
8593 [] and the liveness heartbeat; [§12.3][s12-3] the next-snapshot wait; [§12.4][s12-4] the shutdown
8594 [] protocol; [§12.5][s12-5] scripts and the mid-run mutation doctrine; [§12.6][s12-6] the run
8595 [] lifecycle from `init!`, and partial advance; and [§12.7][s12-7] replay, where the trace
8596 [] re-drives the ordinary loop.

8598 [12.1] ### 12.1 Control plane
8599 [12.1] 
8600 [12.1] Pause, un-pause, pace changes, `margin` changes and stop are a few fields on a
8601 [12.1] separate atomic surface, the [control plane](#g-control-plane). It is not staging, structurally
8602 [12.1] ([D-022][d-022]). Staged writes apply at [drains](#g-drain) (the frame-top swaps that apply staged
8603 [12.1] device writes), and a paused loop drains nothing, so un-pause via staging would
8604 [12.1] deadlock by construction. Riding outside the path through the drain and the
8605 [12.1] [trace](#g-trace), the primary record of a session, is safe for determinism precisely
8606 [12.1] because [§10.7][s10-7] put [pacing](#g-pacing) (the pacer's wall-clock waits) outside the semantics
8607 [12.1] ([D-021][d-021]). Control changes *when* [frames](#g-frame) (iterations of the loop) execute, never
8608 [12.1] what they compute, and stop merely truncates the trajectory. This section
8609 [12.1] covers, in order, when the loop reads the plane, the stop and its issuer, the
8610 [12.1] pause, pace and `margin`, and the fields of `Control`, the periphery's own type
8611 [12.1] that holds the control plane ([D-256][d-256]).
8612 [12.1] 
8613 [12.1] The loop consults the control plane at frame top, the start of each frame. A
8614 [12.1] change issued while a frame runs or waits is observed at the next frame top, at
8615 [12.1] most one frame budget (`h/p`, the step over the pace) later ([D-269][d-269]). The pause
8616 [12.1] block, described with the pause verbs below, is the one wait that wakes at once.
8617 [12.1] 
8618 [12.1] The stop's issuers are the operator's channels and, in an interactive session,
8619 [12.1] Ctrl-C. The operator's channels are a front end's button, the handle of a
8620 [12.1] [device](#g-device) (any attached participant in the periphery) and calling
8621 [12.1] code ([D-310][d-310]). An [operator interrupt](#g-operator-interrupt) (Ctrl-C
8622 [12.1] read as a control-plane stop) sets exactly this stop, with no separate entry
8623 [12.1] point involved ([§12.4][s12-4], [D-132][d-132]). The loop catches it at one of its unmask
8624 [12.1] points, where a deferred Ctrl-C is raised ([§12.4][s12-4]).
8625 [12.1] 
8626 [12.1] **The [stop word](#g-stop-word) carries its issuer** ([D-203][d-203]). The stop word is the control
8627 [12.1] plane's stop command, one atomic word holding its issuer. Each issuing site
8628 [12.1] writes its identity (the device's name, `:code`, or `:interrupt`) by
8629 [12.1] compare-and-swap from empty, and the first writer wins. The loop's frame-top
8630 [12.1] read consults the word for non-empty, so the recorded issuer is the request that
8631 [12.1] actually initiated the tail, the ordered shutdown sequence of [§12.4][s12-4]. The issuer
8632 [12.1] lands in the [termination record](#g-termination-record) (the stopped-sim value naming how the run ended)
8633 [12.1] as its `ControlRequestedStop`'s payload ([§13.5][s13-5], [D-203][d-203]).
8634 [12.1] 
8635 [12.1] **`ControlRequestedStop` sits outside the stop policy** ([D-255][d-255]). The
8636 [12.1] policy is what the caller declares for one advance ([§13.5][s13-5]). The stop
8637 [12.1] word is what anyone can issue at any moment.
8638 [12.1] 
8639 [12.1] **The pause is two verbs on the simulation** ([D-268][d-268]). `pause!(sim)`
8640 [12.1] sets the pause flag and `resume!(sim)` clears it, from any task and in any
8641 [12.1] lifecycle state. `paused(sim)` reads it. The loop consults the flag at frame
8642 [12.1] top, so a `pause!` issued before `run!` starts the run paused at its first
8643 [12.1] frame top. While paused the loop blocks on a condition variable (notified on
8644 [12.1] un-pause and stop), not a spin. A stop issued while paused wakes the loop,
8645 [12.1] which observes the stop word and enters the tail with no further frame
8646 [12.1] ([§12.4][s12-4]). `step!` shares the frame top and blocks the same way, until
8647 [12.1] `resume!` or a stop from another task.
8648 [12.1] 
8649 [12.1] A paused run is still running. The lifecycle reads `running`, and the
8650 [12.1] [roster](#g-roster) (the list of attached device entries) stays frozen
8651 [12.1] ([D-106][d-106]).
8652 [12.1] 
8653 [12.1] **The tail clears the pause flag beside the sticky stopped status**
8654 [12.1] ([D-268][d-268]). The flag is never cleared at a run's start. The stop word,
8655 [12.1] by contrast, is cleared at the top of each run, because a stop belongs to the
8656 [12.1] run it ended. A pause left set by a run that stopped while paused would
8657 [12.1] instead hold the next run at its first frame top, on a flag nobody remembers
8658 [12.1] setting. Clearing it in the tail removes that trap and keeps the start-paused
8659 [12.1] spelling above.
8660 [12.1] 
8661 [12.1] The device handle carries no pause ([§11.6][s11-6]). Whether it gains the
8662 [12.1] control verbs for the bridge ([§11.7][s11-7]) is pending (`pending.md`, [D-310][d-310]).
8663 [12.1] 
8664 [12.1] **Pace and `margin` are two more verbs and two readers** ([D-269][d-269]).
8665 [12.1] `pace!(sim, p)` and `margin!(sim, m)` set them from any task in any lifecycle
8666 [12.1] state, and `pace(sim)` and `margin(sim)` read them. `margin` ([§10.7][s10-7]) rides on
8667 [12.1] the control plane for the same reason `pace` does. It tunes the wait, never the
8668 [12.1] arithmetic, so retuning the coarse/spin split mid-run is safe by construction.
8669 [12.1] `run!` and `replay!` take both as keywords and write them when called. Both are
8670 [12.1] declared by the advance, as `t_end` is. `pace` defaults to `Inf` and `margin` to
8671 [12.1] 2 ms ([Appendix B][sB], [D-133][d-133]). `step!` takes neither and never waits on the pacer. A
8672 [12.1] stepping session is the harness mode ([§12.6][s12-6]), and its frames are bit-identical
8673 [12.1] to `run!`'s by [§10.7][s10-7]'s invariant. `pace` is a positive real, `Inf` admitted, and
8674 [12.1] `margin` a non-negative real, `Inf` admitted. The two setters and the two
8675 [12.1] keywords refuse anything else under `ArgumentInvalid` ([Appendix C][sC]).
8676 [12.1] 
8677 [12.1] Beside pause, pace and `margin`, `Control` keeps the stop word, the sticky
8678 [12.1] stopped status, the running flag ([D-317][d-317]) and the wait
8679 [12.1] ([§12.3][s12-3]). **`Control` also keeps the shutdown tail's `join_timeout`**
8680 [12.1] ([§12.4][s12-4], [D-256][d-256]). The lifecycle state is not stored, and
8681 [12.1] `lifecycle(sim)` derives it ([§12.6][s12-6]). A run's outcome is not a control
8682 [12.1] surface, so `termination` belongs to the [`Run`](#g-run) (the state one run
8683 [12.1] owns), not to `Control` ([§12.6][s12-6], [D-255][d-255]). The control plane is
8684 [12.1] what anyone may poke.
8685 [12.1] 
8686 [12.2] ### 12.2 Loop scheduling: wait primitive, yields, thread budget
8687 [12.2] 
8688 [12.2] [§10.7][s10-7] fixed the shape of the pacer's wait, hybrid sleep-then-spin,
8689 [12.2] and leaves the choice of the coarse phase's primitive to this section. That
8690 [12.2] choice is a scheduling decision rather than an arithmetic one. It settles
8691 [12.2] what else can run while a [frame](#g-frame) (one iteration of the loop) waits.
8692 [12.2] It is made here, together with the two questions that trail it, whether a
8693 [12.2] frame is guaranteed to yield at all and how many threads a session needs. The
8694 [12.2] section ends with the liveness heartbeat that makes a starved device
8695 [12.2] diagnosable.
8696 [12.2] 
8697 [12.2] #### The coarse phase's primitive
8698 [12.2] 
8699 [12.2] **The coarse phase uses task-yielding `sleep`**, and there is no
8700 [12.2] `systemsleep` variant ([D-027][d-027]). What the choice buys is the wait slot.
8701 [12.2] `sleep` releases the loop's thread, which makes the pacer's wait the natural
8702 [12.2] scheduling window for the co-resident tasks of [devices](#g-device) (the
8703 [12.2] attached participants in the periphery). The design already spends that slot
8704 [12.2] twice, as the staging slot ([§10.7][s10-7]) and as what the
8705 [12.2] [drain](#g-drain) (the frame-top swap that applies staged device writes) takes
8706 [12.2] ([§11.4][s11-4]).
8707 [12.2] 
8708 [12.2] A `systemsleep` variant for dedicated-thread hard real-time deployments is a
8709 [12.2] [guarded addition](#g-guarded-addition) (a capability the design admits but
8710 [12.2] does not build).
8711 [12.2] 
8712 [12.2] #### The per-frame yield
8713 [12.2] 
8714 [12.2] **With devices attached, every frame yields at least once** ([D-027][d-027]).
8715 [12.2] The rule is semantically free, because [pacing](#g-pacing) (the waits inserted
8716 [12.2] between completed frames), and hence yielding, is outside the semantics
8717 [12.2] ([§10.7][s10-7], [D-021][d-021]).
8718 [12.2] 
8719 [12.2] The yield is implicit in the coarse-phase `sleep` whenever that phase runs.
8720 [12.2] An explicit `yield()` covers the frames where it does not run, namely unpaced
8721 [12.2] runs and pure-spin frames with budget ≤ margin. The spin phase itself never
8722 [12.2] yields ([D-027][d-027]). Yielding there would trade its µs precision for
8723 [12.2] scheduler noise.
8724 [12.2] 
8725 [12.2] The consequence is a bound on thread occupancy. The loop holds a thread for at
8726 [12.2] most one frame before the scheduler can run anyone else. Julia's scheduler is
8727 [12.2] cooperative, and a stall of that scheduler requires a thread monopolist, a
8728 [12.2] never-yielding task that holds its thread forever. That precondition is
8729 [12.2] structurally absent from framework tasks.
8730 [12.2] 
8731 [12.2] #### The thread budget
8732 [12.2] 
8733 [12.2] **The thread budget is a documented sizing rule and a startup warning, not a
8734 [12.2] hard error** ([D-027][d-027]).
8735 [12.2] 
8736 [12.2] The scheduler stall FlightCore's `nthreads` error prevented cannot reproduce
8737 [12.2] here, for two reasons. The loop yields every frame. Nothing couples a stall to
8738 [12.2] anyone else, the GUI least of all. It waits on nothing, ever. It uses a snapshot
8739 [12.2] acquire-load, its own [staging cell](#g-staging-cell) and atomic control. Under any starvation,
8740 [12.2] then, the front end keeps rendering and the stop button keeps working.
8741 [12.2] 
8742 [12.2] Undersized sessions degrade to laggy inputs and stale [snapshots](#g-snapshot) (the immutable
8743 [12.2] per-boundary publications), which are visible, recoverable states.
8744 [12.2] 
8745 [12.2] At `run!` or `replay!`, a run warns under `ThreadBudget` ([Appendix C][sC]) when
8746 [12.2] `Threads.nthreads()` is tight for the attached population, naming the `julia -t`
8747 [12.2] remedy. That is one check per run, against the frozen [roster](#g-roster), the list of
8748 [12.2] attached device entries ([§11.3][s11-3]). Tight means fewer threads than the roster plus
8749 [12.2] one. Every rostered device is a task, and the [calling task](#g-calling-task) (the task that
8750 [12.2] invoked `run!`) hosts the loop, so a run occupies one task more than its roster
8751 [12.2] ([D-310][d-310]). The sizing guidance behind the remedy is one thread for the loop and
8752 [12.2] headroom for compute-heavy or blocking-ccall devices. I/O backed by libuv (the
8753 [12.2] I/O library under Julia's tasks) yields. Raw blocking ccalls pin their thread
8754 [12.2] for the duration. There is no pinning and there are no sticky tasks.
8755 [12.2] 
8756 [12.2] #### The liveness heartbeat
8757 [12.2] 
8758 [12.2] Since starvation is survivable, it must be diagnosable ([D-027][d-027]). The record is
8759 [12.2] the published [framework status](#g-framework-status), the frozen diagnostics value each snapshot
8760 [12.2] carries beside the table. It includes per-device liveness (the liveness
8761 [12.2] timestamp and the device's `task_state`) next to the pacer diagnostics. The
8762 [12.2] mechanism is the per-writer [diagnostic cell](#g-diagnostic-cell) ([D-136][d-136]), specified in full by [§11.8][s11-8],
8763 [12.2] plus the device `Task` handles the loop already owns, and nothing besides. A
8764 [12.2] diagnostic cell is a writer's own holding place for runtime diagnostics and
8765 [12.2] liveness. It carries the single liveness timestamp. The loop reads `task_state`
8766 [12.2] off those handles where it publishes ([D-193][d-193]). A starved, blocked or crashed
8767 [12.2] device task shows in a front end as a stale heartbeat with a name on it, not as
8768 [12.2] mysteriously frozen physics ([D-310][d-310]).
8769 [12.2] 
8770 [12.2] **`task_state` has three values** ([D-270][d-270]). Outside a run every device
8771 [12.2] reads `:none`. Inside a run a device reads `:running` while its task lives and
8772 [12.2] `:done` once that task has ended, whether the body returned or the wrapper
8773 [12.2] caught its crash ([§11.6][s11-6]). A device whose `init!` threw, and so
8774 [12.2] spawned no task, reads `:done` as well. The record's crash count tells a crash
8775 [12.2] from a return. `orphaned` on a record is exactly `:done` ([§11.7][s11-7],
8776 [12.2] [D-270][d-270]).
8777 [12.2] 
8778 [12.2] **Stale means a liveness timestamp more than 2 s behind wall clock**
8779 [12.2] ([D-133][d-133]). The threshold is deliberately loose, because the heartbeat
8780 [12.2] is advisory. It is a liveness display and a record, never a kill trigger,
8781 [12.2] never a detach. It must also tolerate a device legitimately parked in a
8782 [12.2] blocking read between rare data.
8783 [12.2] 
8784 [12.3] ### 12.3 The next-snapshot wait
8785 [12.3] 
8786 [12.3] Rate-matched output [devices](#g-device) (attached participants in the
8787 [12.3] periphery), such as telemetry and disk streaming, act once per boundary. A
8788 [12.3] [boundary](#g-boundary) is a published consistency point. What they need from
8789 [12.3] the framework is a way to learn that a boundary has happened without polling
8790 [12.3] for it. The [next-snapshot wait](#g-next-snapshot-wait) (a device's blocking
8791 [12.3] wait for the next publication) is that way. This section gives its two
8792 [12.3] artifacts, the loop's side, the device's side, its newest-wins semantics, and
8793 [12.3] how the counter differs from the other two indices, in that order.
8794 [12.3] 
8795 [12.3] **Two artifacts provide the wait, a monotonic [boundary counter](#g-boundary-counter) plus one
8796 [12.3] `Threads.Condition`** ([D-028][d-028]). The boundary counter is the loop's own. It counts
8797 [12.3] *published boundaries*, that is grid, `t*` and [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the initialization
8798 [12.3] boundary at `t₀`), not frames ([§10.1][s10-1], [§10.4][s10-4]). Consecutive wakes are therefore
8799 [12.3] not necessarily `h` apart.
8800 [12.3] 
8801 [12.3] The loop's publication is `lock; counter += 1; notify; unlock`, nanoseconds
8802 [12.3] of framework-only code. Waiters never block it. One parked in `wait` has
8803 [12.3] released the lock as part of parking.
8804 [12.3] 
8805 [12.3] The order of the two publications is normative. **The release-store of [`latest`](#g-latest),
8806 [12.3] the atomic reference readers acquire-load ([§11.2][s11-2]), happens before the counter
8807 [12.3] increment under the lock** ([D-114][d-114]).
8808 [12.3] 
8809 [12.3] ```julia
8810 [12.3] # the loop, at every published boundary
8811 [12.3] @atomic latest = snap    # 1. release-store: the snapshot becomes reachable (§11.2)
8812 [12.3] lock(cond) do            # 2. only then the counter, under the lock
8813 [12.3]     counter += 1
8814 [12.3]     notify(cond)         #    parked waiters wake and re-test their predicate
8815 [12.3] end
8816 [12.3] ```
8817 [12.3] 
8818 [12.3] The device side's test `counter > last_seen` (below) therefore implies that
8819 [12.3] `latest` holds at least that boundary, and a waiter can never wake onto a
8820 [12.3] [snapshot](#g-snapshot) (the immutable per-boundary publication) that is stale. The converse is
8821 [12.3] expected and correct. A waiter may observe a *newer* snapshot than the increment
8822 [12.3] that woke it. Newest wins.
8823 [12.3] 
8824 [12.3] The device side is `wait_next_snapshot(handle)`, which blocks until
8825 [12.3] `counter > last_seen && running` under the canonical predicate-loop idiom. That
8826 [12.3] idiom handles waiters at different paces, boundaries skipped while transmitting,
8827 [12.3] and shutdown, all with no per-frame reset. Shutdown works because [§12.4][s12-4] wakes
8828 [12.3] all waiters and each predicate then routes its owner out ([D-028][d-028]).
8829 [12.3] 
8830 [12.3] **The wait's semantics are newest-wins, no queues** ([D-028][d-028]). A slow consumer
8831 [12.3] skips boundaries and always receives the current world. This mirrors the inbound
8832 [12.3] side. Inbound [coalescing](#g-coalescing) (the compare-and-swap merge that leaves one pending
8833 [12.3] batch per device) keeps the newest level per face, and the wait delivers the
8834 [12.3] newest snapshot, the same zero-order hold (ZOH) decision. No backpressure exists
8835 [12.3] in either direction, and the loop never waits on anyone. Per-consumer
8836 [12.3] every-boundary queues are rejected ([D-028][d-028]).
8837 [12.3] 
8838 [12.3] A `Base.Event` latch is the wrong primitive here ([D-028][d-028]). Condition
8839 [12.3] variables carry no facts, only "look again". The facts that matter, the
8840 [12.3] counter and `running`, live in state each waiter tests privately.
8841 [12.3] 
8842 [12.3] Two indices number the publications, and each lives in its own place. The
8843 [12.3] snapshot carries the trajectory's *published-boundary ordinal* with `t`.
8844 [12.3] Boundary zero is 0, and every publication after it, grid or `t*`, counts one.
8845 [12.3] Any holder of a snapshot therefore indexes it without consulting the loop,
8846 [12.3] whether the [log](#g-log) (the retained sequence of published snapshots) or a post-run
8847 [12.3] inspector. A new trajectory restarts the ordinal at zero, with everything else
8848 [12.3] `init!` resets. The boundary counter is a different number. It is the loop's
8849 [12.3] monotone count of every publication it has ever made, never reset across
8850 [12.3] trajectories, and its absolute value is nowhere normative. It exists for the
8851 [12.3] wait predicate alone, and it is never reset because a waiter's `last_seen` must
8852 [12.3] never run ahead of it ([D-230][d-230]). An error's pointer for [replay](#g-replay) (the ordinary loop
8853 [12.3] re-driven from the trace) is a third index, the frame-entry boundary index of
8854 [12.3] [§13.4][s13-4], a frame count that names no `t*` boundary ([D-128][d-128]).
8855 [12.3] 
8856 [12.3] The GUI does not use the wait. Being VSync-paced, it reads `latest` at each
8857 [12.3] render.
8858 [12.3] 
8859 [12.4] ### 12.4 Shutdown protocol
8860 [12.4] 
8861 [12.4] A run ends when its time is up, when someone stops it, or when the model
8862 [12.4] itself says so. Whatever the cause, the same work has to happen. The loop
8863 [12.4] has to stop on a [boundary](#g-boundary) (a published consistency point).
8864 [12.4] The task each [device](#g-device) (any attached participant in the
8865 [12.4] periphery) runs on has to be woken out of whatever it was blocked on. Every
8866 [12.4] resource acquired for the run has to be released before `run!` returns.
8867 [12.4] This section specifies that sequence, which it calls the tail throughout. It
8868 [12.4] also specifies the two things that bracket the tail. One is the pre-spawn
8869 [12.4] initialization at the top of a run, which is a step of this same protocol.
8870 [12.4] The other is the [operator interrupt](#g-operator-interrupt) (Ctrl-C in an
8871 [12.4] interactive session), which enters the tail rather than bypassing it.
8872 [12.4] 
8873 [12.4] #### The tail: the ordered sequence every stop takes
8874 [12.4] 
8875 [12.4] **Steps (1) through (5) below run in that order on every stop**, whatever
8876 [12.4] initiated it ([D-030][d-030]). Steps (6) and (7) specify what happens when a
8877 [12.4] device task or the loop itself ends first.
8878 [12.4] 
8879 [12.4] 1. Initiation. Three events start a shutdown. Either `t_end` is reached, or a
8880 [12.4]    stop is issued on the [control plane](#g-control-plane) (the separate atomic surface carrying
8881 [12.4]    pause, pace and stop), or a [stop request](#g-stop-request) the advance honours reads
8882 [12.4]    `STOP_REQUESTED` in the just-published [snapshot](#g-snapshot) ([D-316][d-316]). A stop request is an
8883 [12.4]    output port of the framework enum `StopFlag`, and a snapshot is the immutable
8884 [12.4]    per-boundary publication. The control-plane stop's issuers are a front end
8885 [12.4]    ([D-310][d-310]), a device handle, code, or an operator interrupt (Ctrl-C, treated
8886 [12.4]    below). The third event is model-detected termination ([§13.5][s13-5]). The loop never
8887 [12.4]    stops mid-boundary. It always completes the boundary sequence in progress,
8888 [12.4]    which a stop at [`t*`](#g-t) (the localized event time) places inside a [frame](#g-frame), one
8889 [12.4]    iteration of the loop. The loop then publishes the final snapshot. Only then
8890 [12.4]    does it set the sticky stopped status.
8891 [12.4] 2. Wake all framework waits. The waits are the [next-snapshot
8892 [12.4]    wait](#g-next-snapshot-wait) (`wait_next_snapshot(handle)`) and the pause.
8893 [12.4]    Each waiter observes the stopped status and unwinds. A stop issued while
8894 [12.4]    paused therefore works.
8895 [12.4] 3. Unblock device-specific blocking calls. The hook is `unblock!(device)`,
8896 [12.4]    default no-op. A network input's override closes its own socket, which
8897 [12.4]    raises in the blocked task. The framework wrapper catches that raise and
8898 [12.4]    treats it as shutdown. This demotes FlightCore's EOT convention from
8899 [12.4]    essential shutdown mechanism to an optional wire-protocol courtesy between
8900 [12.4]    remote peers.
8901 [12.4] 4. Loop bodies exit. The exit is the author's own `while running(handle)`
8902 [12.4]    loop, the authoring contract of [§11.6][s11-6]. That authoring contract
8903 [12.4]    teaches two obligations, the predicate check and interruptible blocking
8904 [12.4]    ([D-373][d-373]). Steps (2) and (3) are the framework's half. They wake its
8905 [12.4]    own waits and call each device's `unblock!`. The wrapper's `finally
8906 [12.4]    shutdown!(device)` is guaranteed on every exit path.
8907 [12.4] 5. Join under the `join_timeout` cap. It is a keyword of the `Simulation`
8908 [12.4]    constructor, held on `Control`, the tail's own owner ([§12.1][s12-1], [D-256][d-256]), a
8909 [12.4]    positive real in seconds of wall clock, defaulting to 5 ([Appendix B][sB]). The
8910 [12.4]    constructor refuses any other value as `ArgumentInvalid`. A device task
8911 [12.4]    exceeding the cap is reported *by name* in a `DeviceJoinTimeout` diagnostic
8912 [12.4]    ([Appendix C][sC]). It is then abandoned rather than left to hang `run!`. **The
8913 [12.4]    diagnostic is written to the loop's own [diagnostic cell](#g-diagnostic-cell)**, a writer's own
8914 [12.4]    holding place for runtime diagnostics and liveness ([D-203][d-203]). The run's-end
8915 [12.4]    sweep, the loop's last pass over every diagnostic cell, collects the
8916 [12.4]    diagnostic into the [termination record](#g-termination-record) (the stopped-sim value naming how the
8917 [12.4]    run ended). The logging backend presents the diagnostic, with the terminal
8918 [12.4]    snapshot preceding the join ([D-201][d-201], [D-203][d-203]).
8919 [12.4] 6. Device-initiated paths. A device exits voluntarily when its loop body
8920 [12.4]    returns, at a window ✕ or a peer EOT. No `should_close` hook exists ([§11.6][s11-6]).
8921 [12.4]    With [`should_abort`](#g-should_abort) (the per-attachment failure policy, an `attach!` keyword)
8922 [12.4]    set, the wrapper's exit path also requests a sim stop. Otherwise the sim
8923 [12.4]    continues with the device's *task* absent. Its [staging cell](#g-staging-cell) (where the
8924 [12.4]    device's pending write batch waits between drains) stops filling, and the
8925 [12.4]    loop is structurally indifferent. Its [roster](#g-roster) entry and its [claims](#g-claim) persist to
8926 [12.4]    run end, because [§11.3][s11-3] freezes the roster for the run and death is not
8927 [12.4]    detach. The roster is the list of attached device entries, and a claim is the
8928 [12.4]    set of faces a device may write. The orphaned [root inputs](#g-root-input) (the root
8929 [12.4]    component's own input faces) hold their last-drained values, visibly ([§11.7][s11-7]).
8930 [12.4]    A crashing device task is caught by the framework wrapper and follows the
8931 [12.4]    same path, logged with the device's name (`DeviceCrash`, [Appendix C][sC]).
8932 [12.4] 7. Loop-side failure. A failure on the loop's own side runs steps (1) through
8933 [12.4]    (5) from the catch path ([D-030][d-030]). [§13.6][s13-6] specifies that
8934 [12.4]    path, which discards the failed boundary and promotes the previous snapshot
8935 [12.4]    to final. FlightCore's `SimulationTermination` catch path was the
8936 [12.4]    precedent. The exception-based termination idiom itself has no place here
8937 [12.4]    ([§13.5][s13-5], [D-060][d-060]). Devices therefore unwind cleanly
8938 [12.4]    regardless of who died.
8939 [12.4] 
8940 [12.4] The ordered part, in one line:
8941 [12.4] 
8942 [12.4] > (1) stop observed → current boundary completed → final snapshot published
8943 [12.4] > → sticky `stopped` set → (2) framework waits woken → (3) `unblock!`
8944 [12.4] > per device → (4) loop bodies return, each through its wrapper's
8945 [12.4] > `finally shutdown!` → (5) join under the `join_timeout` cap → `run!`
8946 [12.4] > returns
8947 [12.4] 
8948 [12.4] `t_end` lands on the grid. **The run ends at the first grid boundary whose time
8949 [12.4] reaches or exceeds `t_end`** ([D-133][d-133]). The run takes whole frames only, never a
8950 [12.4] shortened final step, which grid integrity forbids ([§10.4][s10-4]). Each grid time is
8951 [12.4] indexed, `tₖ = t₀ + k·h`, and never accumulated ([§10.4][s10-4]). The final boundary may
8952 [12.4] therefore overshoot `t_end` by up to `h`. The termination record carries the
8953 [12.4] actual final `t` ([§13.5][s13-5]). This is the `t_plus` spelling ([§12.6][s12-6]) applied to the
8954 [12.4] run's own clock.
8955 [12.4] 
8956 [12.4] The two termination sources differ in nature. `t_end` is a grid fact,
8957 [12.4] checked against boundary times on the grid. Stop requests are checked at
8958 [12.4] *every* published boundary, `t*` included ([§13.5][s13-5]).
8959 [12.4] 
8960 [12.4] The final snapshot goes out before the status is set. Publishing first
8961 [12.4] guarantees that output devices can flush the true final state. The status in
8962 [12.4] that terminal snapshot carries the run's cumulative diagnostic counters
8963 [12.4] ([§11.8][s11-8]), the warning account of a run nobody watched, complete up to
8964 [12.4] that snapshot's own frame top. **What the tail itself produces is folded into
8965 [12.4] the termination record and presented through the logging backend, never
8966 [12.4] published** ([D-201][d-201], [D-203][d-203]). It comes later by construction.
8967 [12.4] 
8968 [12.4] That terminal snapshot is retained in the [log](#g-log) (the retained
8969 [12.4] sequence of published snapshots) unconditionally, under any `log_every` and
8970 [12.4] any `log_max` ([§11.2][s11-2], [D-368][d-368]).
8971 [12.4] 
8972 [12.4] The `join_timeout` default of five seconds is generous for front-end window
8973 [12.4] teardown and socket closes. It is short enough that an abandoned join reads as a
8974 [12.4] diagnosed timeout rather than a hang.
8975 [12.4] 
8976 [12.4] **The cap is a per-simulation setting, not an implementation constant** ([D-198][d-198]).
8977 [12.4] That is the disposition [§10.4][s10-4] gives its own two constants, extended here to an
8978 [12.4] operational one. Usage contexts legitimately differ in patience. A series of
8979 [12.4] runs nobody watches wants to fail fast, a test battery exercising the
8980 [12.4] abandonment path faster still, while a device with slow teardown may warrant
8981 [12.4] more. The cap is not trajectory-determining, because the trajectory has ended at
8982 [12.4] the final snapshot before any join begins. So [replay](#g-replay) (the ordinary loop with the
8983 [12.4] drain reading recorded batches) neither records nor compares the cap ([§11.5][s11-5],
8984 [12.4] [§12.7][s12-7]), just as for `log_max` ([§11.2][s11-2]). The cap therefore lives on `Control`, not
8985 [12.4] on the `Deployment` ([D-256][d-256]).
8986 [12.4] 
8987 [12.4] #### What survives the tail
8988 [12.4] 
8989 [12.4] After (5) the task set is empty, since device tasks are per-run artifacts
8990 [12.4] ([§11.1][s11-1], [D-093][d-093]). `shutdown!` has also released each
8991 [12.4] device's OS resources. What survives a stop is the roster entry, with its
8992 [12.4] binding, claims and stable device id ([§11.3][s11-3]). A task never
8993 [12.4] survives, and neither does a live resource. That holds for a device whose
8994 [12.4] task died mid-run too. Its roster entry is indistinguishable at this point
8995 [12.4] from any other's. Once stopped, `detach!` can remove a roster entry and
8996 [12.4] release its claims ([D-106][d-106]).
8997 [12.4] 
8998 [12.4] The next run re-acquires everything. The next `run!` re-runs device
8999 [12.4] `init!`, since resource acquisition is per-run ([D-093][d-093]). The
9000 [12.4] precedent is FlightCore's network device, `network.jl`, which created a new
9001 [12.4] socket at each `init!`. The next `run!` also spawns fresh tasks against the
9002 [12.4] [§12.3][s12-3] counter, which is never re-armed. Each task reads its
9003 [12.4] `last_seen` off the counter at spawn. While stopped there are no device
9004 [12.4] tasks at all, so voluntary exit and the [§12.2][s12-2] liveness heartbeat are
9005 [12.4] run-scoped observables. A device unplugged while stopped surfaces as the
9006 [12.4] next run's `init!` failure, disposed of by the initialization bracket below.
9007 [12.4] 
9008 [12.4] #### Initialization: the pre-spawn bracket
9009 [12.4] 
9010 [12.4] **Initialization is a step of this protocol, taken at the top of a run**
9011 [12.4] ([D-138][d-138]). Before any task is spawned, the loop calls `init!` once per
9012 [12.4] roster entry, in attachment order, on the [calling task](#g-calling-task)
9013 [12.4] (the task that invoked `run!`). Each call sits in its own bracket.
9014 [12.4] 
9015 [12.4] ```julia
9016 [12.4] for entry in roster                       # attachment order, calling task
9017 [12.4]     push!(live, entry)                    # listed for release before `init!` begins
9018 [12.4]     try
9019 [12.4]         init!(entry.device)
9020 [12.4]     catch e
9021 [12.4]         shutdown!(entry.device)           # release, unconditionally (§11.6)
9022 [12.4]         pop!(live)                        # released, so unlisted
9023 [12.4]         if e isa InterruptException       # the operator's stop, never a crash (below)
9024 [12.4]             stop!(control, :interrupt)
9025 [12.4]         else
9026 [12.4]             # pre-spawn: the entry is the address, no task holds a handle yet
9027 [12.4]             report!(entry, DeviceCrash(e, entry.should_abort))
9028 [12.4]             entry.should_abort && stop!(entry.handle)
9029 [12.4]         end
9030 [12.4]                                           # no dead-marking: no task is spawned (§12.2)
9031 [12.4]     end
9032 [12.4] end
9033 [12.4] ```
9034 [12.4] 
9035 [12.4] The bracket is what makes "guaranteed on every exit path" ([§11.6][s11-6])
9036 [12.4] true of the path outside that wrapper. A device that throws half-way through
9037 [12.4] acquisition is handed back to `shutdown!` right there, so its partially
9038 [12.4] acquired OS resources are released rather than leaked ([D-138][d-138]). That
9039 [12.4] is exactly why `shutdown!` owes tolerance of a partially initialized device,
9040 [12.4] a rule [§11.6][s11-6] teaches.
9041 [12.4] 
9042 [12.4] The roster entry is listed for release before its `init!` begins
9043 [12.4] ([D-268][d-268]). The bracket unlists it once its own `shutdown!` has
9044 [12.4] returned. An operator interrupt that escapes the bracket therefore finds
9045 [12.4] listed every device whose `init!` began and that the bracket has not
9046 [12.4] released, and the run releases each before it ends. A device listed and not
9047 [12.4] yet initialized can be released having opened nothing, which `shutdown!`
9048 [12.4] tolerates ([§11.6][s11-6]).
9049 [12.4] 
9050 [12.4] **The report is the ordinary `DeviceCrash`, not a kind of its own**
9051 [12.4] ([D-138][d-138], [Appendix C][sC]). Its [payload](#g-payload) (the
9052 [12.4] structured data a diagnostic carries beside its kind) already carries the
9053 [12.4] cause exception and whether `should_abort` was set. The diagnostic cell it
9054 [12.4] is written to names the device. That is everything an init-time failure has
9055 [12.4] to say. The name is honest. A device that cannot acquire its resources has
9056 [12.4] crashed before it lived.
9057 [12.4] 
9058 [12.4] **The report is written through the ordinary `report!(address, diagnostic)`
9059 [12.4] entry point**, addressed by the roster entry rather than by a handle ([D-189][d-189]).
9060 [12.4] There is no device task to hold a handle before the spawn. The address supplies
9061 [12.4] the device identity either way, which is why no call passes a device id ([§11.8][s11-8]).
9062 [12.4] 
9063 [12.4] No task is spawned for a failed device, so it is dead from the run's first frame
9064 [12.4] top ([D-138][d-138]). That needs no machinery. Its `task_state` reads `:done` from the
9065 [12.4] first publication ([§12.2][s12-2]). A device that never ran never beat, so its diagnostic
9066 [12.4] cell also reads stale against the [§12.2][s12-2] threshold from the start ([D-138][d-138]).
9067 [12.4] 
9068 [12.4] The claims of a failed device persist to run end ([D-138][d-138]). This is the
9069 [12.4] death-is-not-detach disposition ([§11.3][s11-3]), applied one step earlier than (6)'s.
9070 [12.4] The roster is frozen for the run, and the orphaned root inputs hold the values
9071 [12.4] the run started with. Those values are well-defined by [root-input totality](#g-root-input-totality), the
9072 [12.4] rule that every root input must hold a value ([§14.6][s14-6]). An orphan of (6) holds a
9073 [12.4] last drained batch instead.
9074 [12.4] 
9075 [12.4] **The run's disposition splits on `should_abort`, uniformly with (6)**
9076 [12.4] ([D-138][d-138]). With the flag clear, which is the default
9077 [12.4] ([§11.6][s11-6]), the remaining roster entries initialize, the run starts,
9078 [12.4] and the sim runs with that device absent from the run's first frame. That
9079 [12.4] is (6)'s "the sim continues with the device's *task* absent", shifted to the
9080 [12.4] run's start.
9081 [12.4] 
9082 [12.4] With the flag set, the failure requests a control-plane stop, and that stop is
9083 [12.4] simply *already pending* at the run's first frame top. The loop reads it there,
9084 [12.4] before any frame, so the run advances no frame. This protocol already has that
9085 [12.4] path, so no new exit protocol is needed. The remaining roster entries still
9086 [12.4] initialize, every rostered device getting its `init!`/`shutdown!` pair
9087 [12.4] uniformly. The run ends `stopped` where it started, through this same tail, with
9088 [12.4] its termination record naming the device ([§13.5][s13-5]). What the operator is left with
9089 [12.4] is an ordinary stopped simulation, whose terminal snapshot is the one the run
9090 [12.4] started from and whose termination record's tail residue (the diagnostics the
9091 [12.4] run's-end sweep folds in) holds the `DeviceCrash`. It is fully serviceable by
9092 [12.4] [§14][s14] and resumable by the next `run!` ([§12.6][s12-6]) once the device is plugged back in.
9093 [12.4] 
9094 [12.4] **An `InterruptException` inside a device's `init!` is the operator's stop, not
9095 [12.4] that device's crash** ([D-268][d-268]). The bracket releases the device through
9096 [12.4] `shutdown!` as for any throw and spawns no task for it. In place of the
9097 [12.4] `DeviceCrash` report it sets the `:interrupt` stop through the stop word (the
9098 [12.4] control plane's stop command, one atomic word holding its issuer). The run's
9099 [12.4] `should_abort` split above therefore does not arise. The remaining roster
9100 [12.4] entries still initialize, and the run ends at its first frame top with zero
9101 [12.4] frames advanced, through the pending-stop path above. The device wrapper makes
9102 [12.4] the same discrimination for a raise inside a loop body ([§11.6][s11-6]).
9103 [12.4] 
9104 [12.4] #### The operator interrupt
9105 [12.4] 
9106 [12.4] **The operator interrupt is a stop, not a failure** ([D-132][d-132]). Ctrl-C
9107 [12.4] in an interactive session is a control-plane stop command issued by hand.
9108 [12.4] The run completes the current boundary, publishes the final snapshot, takes
9109 [12.4] this tail like any other stop, and ends `stopped`. The result is
9110 [12.4] boundary-consistent. It is fully serviceable by the [§14][s14] stopped-sim
9111 [12.4] services and resumable by the next `run!` ([§12.6][s12-6]).
9112 [12.4] 
9113 [12.4] The interrupt is the escape from a run nothing else ends, and the deviceless run
9114 [12.4] has no other. A run with no finite `t_end` and no honoured stop request is the
9115 [12.4] configuration `UnboundedRun` names ([Appendix C][sC]). The interrupt needs no entry
9116 [12.4] point of its own ([D-132][d-132]). The stop already rides on the control plane ([§12.1][s12-1]).
9117 [12.4] The exceptions-are-abnormal doctrine ([§13.5][s13-5], [D-060][d-060]) is untouched. That doctrine
9118 [12.4] is about *model* code, while this is the one exception whose meaning the
9119 [12.4] framework knows.
9120 [12.4] 
9121 [12.4] The interrupt's scope is the interactive session. Outside the REPL, Julia's
9122 [12.4] default (`exit_on_sigint(true)`) kills the process on SIGINT before any of
9123 [12.4] this machinery runs. The framework flips nothing process-global
9124 [12.4] ([D-132][d-132]). [Unattended runs](#g-unattended-run), those with empty
9125 [12.4] staging and no snapshot readers, rely on `t_end` and stop requests, as they
9126 [12.4] already must.
9127 [12.4] 
9128 [12.4] **Masking across the boundary is normative, not an implementation hint**
9129 [12.4] ([D-132][d-132]). The reason is that an `InterruptException` is delivered asynchronously.
9130 [12.4] An interrupt landing in the middle of a [sweep](#g-sweep) (one pass through the execution
9131 [12.4] order) would therefore destroy the boundary this protocol is built on
9132 [12.4] completing. It would also leave [stores](#g-store) (the framework-owned homes of a
9133 [12.4] component's state) half-written ([§13.6][s13-6]). That forces a choice between `stopped`
9134 [12.4] with dirty stores and a terminal `errored`. The `stopped`-with-consistent-stores
9135 [12.4] guarantee is exactly what the masking buys.
9136 [12.4] 
9137 [12.4] The loop masks delivery across the boundary macro-sequence, using Julia's
9138 [12.4] `disable_sigint`, a sigatomic counter increment that is negligible per
9139 [12.4] frame. It takes the deferred raise at the unmask points. They are the frame
9140 [12.4] top, where it already consults the control plane ([§12.1][s12-1]), and
9141 [12.4] points inside its wait and pause blocks. All of those points are
9142 [12.4] boundary-consistent. Caught at one of them, the interrupt sets the
9143 [12.4] control-plane stop and enters this tail. The catch site ([§13.4][s13-4])
9144 [12.4] therefore never sees a single interrupt.
9145 [12.4] 
9146 [12.4] **A deferred interrupt yields to the frame's own stop request**
9147 [12.4] ([D-268][d-268]). Stop requests are consulted at each publication and the
9148 [12.4] stop word at the next frame top ([§13.5][s13-5]), and the deferred raise
9149 [12.4] lands between the two. Where the frame's publication found an honoured
9150 [12.4] request holding, that request is the recorded source and the interrupt is
9151 [12.4] satisfied by the run ending. Where none held, the interrupt sets the stop
9152 [12.4] word and the source is `ControlRequestedStop(:interrupt)`.
9153 [12.4] 
9154 [12.4] **A frame that throws with an interrupt pending ends `errored`**
9155 [12.4] ([D-268][d-268]). The frame's own throw is the disposition
9156 [12.4] ([§13.4][s13-4]). The catch wraps the cause under the mask and consumes the
9157 [12.4] pending interrupt on its way out. The run then takes the abnormal shutdown entry
9158 [12.4] under that `StepError` ([§13.6][s13-6]). The alternative, `stopped` over a
9159 [12.4] half-written boundary, is what the masking exists to prevent. The interrupt
9160 [12.4] is satisfied by the run ending.
9161 [12.4] 
9162 [12.4] **An interrupt after a frame's throw does not displace it**
9163 [12.4] ([D-268][d-268]). `run!`, `replay!` and `step!` store the loop's throw before
9164 [12.4] an interrupt can displace it, and the masked bookkeeping below builds the
9165 [12.4] record from that store. The run therefore ends `errored` under its
9166 [12.4] `StepError` wherever a later interrupt lands. An interrupt that cuts the
9167 [12.4] handling of the throw itself propagates out of the call raw, and the
9168 [12.4] disposition of the failure is skipped ([§13.4][s13-4]). The record is still
9169 [12.4] written and the lifecycle is still `errored`.
9170 [12.4] 
9171 [12.4] **The tail's bookkeeping is masked too** ([D-268][d-268]). After the joins, the run's-end
9172 [12.4] sweep, the termination record and the terminal lifecycle state are written under
9173 [12.4] the same mask. An interrupt landing there would otherwise abort the sequence
9174 [12.4] half-way and leave the lifecycle `running`, every service refusing. A raise
9175 [12.4] deferred to the end of that bookkeeping propagates out of `run!` raw, the
9176 [12.4] simulation already terminal.
9177 [12.4] 
9178 [12.4] **A forced throw reaches the defensive arm** ([D-268][d-268]). Julia stops deferring
9179 [12.4] after repeated SIGINTs in a short window, warns that it is forcing the throw,
9180 [12.4] and raises inside the mask. That is the one way the branch [§13.4][s13-4] keeps is
9181 [12.4] reached. The arm abandons the frame unpublished and the run ends `stopped`, the
9182 [12.4] source `ControlRequestedStop(:interrupt)`. What the run published is consistent,
9183 [12.4] since the frame published nothing. The stores may hold mid-boundary values until
9184 [12.4] the next `init!`, `restore!` or `replay!` resets them, as after a throw inside
9185 [12.4] [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero), the initialization boundary, which runs the ordinary
9186 [12.4] macro-sequence with an empty integrate ([§13.4][s13-4]). `checkpoint` refuses such a run
9187 [12.4] as `CheckpointMidFrame`, since the [executor](#g-executor) (the compiled form of the stage
9188 [12.4] execution order) no longer rests where its last snapshot was published ([§12.6][s12-6]).
9189 [12.4] The [trace](#g-trace) (the primary record of a session) is the reproduction ([§12.7][s12-7]).
9190 [12.4] 
9191 [12.4] **A second interrupt during the tail collapses the remaining joins
9192 [12.4] immediately** ([D-132][d-132]). That is (5)'s abandonment path taken at once,
9193 [12.4] with devices still reported by name (`DeviceJoinTimeout`). The run still
9194 [12.4] ends `stopped`. Escalation shortens the tail, never reclassifies the run.
9195 [12.4] 
9196 [12.5] ### 12.5 Scripts and the mid-run mutation doctrine
9197 [12.5] 
9198 [12.5] This section settles what the [periphery](#g-periphery) (everything outside the loop that
9199 [12.5] exchanges data with it) may do to a running model. The periphery's attached
9200 [12.5] participants are its [devices](#g-device). The section surveys FlightCore's mid-run
9201 [12.5] mutations, states the clock criterion that maps scripts to [components](#g-component) (units of
9202 [12.5] modeling), eliminates FlightCore's per-step hook `user_callback!`, covers manual
9203 [12.5] events and mid-run re-initialization, and ends with the doctrine's final form.
9204 [12.5] 
9205 [12.5] What the consumers demonstrably mutate mid-run is surveyed here. The line
9206 [12.5] references are to `c172_demos.jl`, FlightCore's demo file. FlightCore's
9207 [12.5] `user_callback!` has exactly two archetypes. The first is the timetable
9208 [12.5] script, `elevator_offset` as a function of `t` (c172_demos.jl:290). The second
9209 [12.5] is the synthetic pilot, a phase FSM (finite-state machine) reading `y` and
9210 [12.5] writing mode requests, references, flaps and wind (c172_demos.jl:423, 525).
9211 [12.5] Both write only `u` fields. No demo, test or GUI path pokes `x`/`s` mid-run,
9212 [12.5] and `init!`/trim appear only between construction and `run!`
9213 [12.5] (c172_demos.jl:303).
9214 [12.5] 
9215 [12.5] **A sim-time script becomes a [scenario component](#g-scenario-component), and a wall-clock interaction
9216 [12.5] becomes a device** ([D-031][d-031]). A scenario component is an ordinary periodic
9217 [12.5] discrete component holding a sim-time script. Sim-time scripts are model
9218 [12.5] behavior, which is why they become scenario components. Both archetypes are
9219 [12.5] clocked by *sim time*, `t`, the trajectory. Mapping them to devices is rejected
9220 [12.5] ([D-031][d-031]). The clock is the criterion.
9221 [12.5] 
9222 [12.5] A script mapped to a component is periodic discrete, with `K = 1` for the
9223 [12.5] `dt = 0.02` callbacks FlightCore had, `K` being the multiplier in
9224 [12.5] `Relative(K)` ([§10.5][s10-5]). It executes synchronously in the loop,
9225 [12.5] deterministic paced or unpaced. It is replayed by recomputation, with no
9226 [12.5] [trace](#g-trace) (the records of each frame's drained inputs). A device, by
9227 [12.5] contrast, is traced and replayed from the trace.
9228 [12.5] 
9229 [12.5] The component mapping is strictly richer than the callback it replaces.
9230 [12.5] 
9231 [12.5] - The callback's `Ref(:init)` phase closure becomes honest `s`, which a
9232 [12.5]   [port](#g-port) (one declared name with its cell) can publish to
9233 [12.5]   [snapshots](#g-snapshot), logs and plots. A snapshot is the immutable
9234 [12.5]   per-boundary publication.
9235 [12.5] - Inputs arrive fresh from the same [boundary](#g-boundary) (a published consistency point), by
9236 [12.5]   topological order. The callback ran post-step, one boundary staler.
9237 [12.5] - The pure timetable script is a one-liner reading the clock out of its
9238 [12.5]   [bundle](#g-bundle) (the NamedTuple of zero-copy views a component function
9239 [12.5]   receives). That one-liner is `y_direct(c, (; t)) = (; offset = profile(t))`,
9240 [12.5]   exact at its own [ticks](#g-tick), with no latch state of its own. A tick
9241 [12.5]   is an instant at which a discrete component's stages and update run.
9242 [12.5] - In a scenario configuration the script drives the input ports of the avionics
9243 [12.5]   (the onboard flight systems). [§11.7][s11-7] therefore renders a front end's
9244 [12.5]   corresponding widgets read-only, each showing its source ([D-310][d-310]). Its
9245 [12.5]   port-resolution rule resolves FlightCore's demo-vs-GUI dead-slider fight.
9246 [12.5] 
9247 [12.5] **`user_callback!` is eliminated** ([D-031][d-031]). It is the periphery's
9248 [12.5] `f_step!`, FlightCore's unconditional per-step hook ([§2.5][s2-5]), and cheap
9249 [12.5] composition leaves it without justification. Its call sites migrate to
9250 [12.5] scenario components, not devices.
9251 [12.5] 
9252 [12.5] **Manual event triggering needs no mechanism** ([D-031][d-031]). It takes a
9253 [12.5] [root input](#g-root-input) (the root component's own input face) plus a
9254 [12.5] [boundary-detected](#g-boundary-detected) [guard](#g-guard) (an edge check at
9255 [12.5] step boundaries only, with no root-finding) reading that root input. That is
9256 [12.5] already expressible in settled machinery. The levels doctrine applies
9257 [12.5] ([§11.4][s11-4]), with latched commands or counters. The engine start/stop
9258 [12.5] buttons of FlightCore's demos were `u`-writes.
9259 [12.5] 
9260 [12.5] **Mid-run re-initialization is not built** ([D-031][d-031]), because it is not
9261 [12.5] demonstrated. Initialization and trim are stopped-sim workflows, first-class
9262 [12.5] services of [§14][s14]. No concurrency perimeter exists there. There is no loop and no
9263 [12.5] devices, just plain single-task code. The shape of the [guarded addition](#g-guarded-addition) (a
9264 [12.5] capability the design admits but does not build) is on record should demand
9265 [12.5] appear. It would be a traced, boundary-executed intervention command applied
9266 [12.5] through project → [sweep](#g-sweep) → publish, so that no consumer ever observes un-decoded
9267 [12.5] state. A sweep is one pass through the execution order against the current
9268 [12.5] state.
9269 [12.5] 
9270 [12.5] In the doctrine's final form, while a simulation runs, **the periphery stages
9271 [12.5] root-input writes and issues control commands** ([§2.2][s2-2],
9272 [12.5] [D-031][d-031]). Structurally, it does nothing else. Anything that wants to
9273 [12.5] poke the model mid-run is one of three things.
9274 [12.5] 
9275 [12.5] - It is an *input* in disguise, so wire a root input and a guard.
9276 [12.5] - It is *model behavior* in disguise, so add a scenario component.
9277 [12.5] - It is a *wall-clock interaction*, so attach a device.
9278 [12.5] 
9279 [12.5] Graceful termination follows the same shape ([§13.5][s13-5]). It is a
9280 [12.5] [stop request](#g-stop-request) (an output port of the framework enum
9281 [12.5] `StopFlag`) declared in the model, honoured unless the advance ignores it
9282 [12.5] ([D-316][d-316]). It is never a callback, and never a thrown exception
9283 [12.5] ([D-060][d-060]).
9284 [12.5] 
9285 [12.6] ### 12.6 Run lifecycle and partial advance
9286 [12.6] 
9287 [12.6] This section specifies what a `Simulation` and its run own, the lifecycle a
9288 [12.6] simulation moves through, how it advances and restarts, what it keeps of its
9289 [12.6] [devices](#g-device) (any attached participants in the periphery) across runs, and the stop
9290 [12.6] policy each advance declares. A simulation is in one of five lifecycle states,
9291 [12.6] built, initialized, running, stopped or errored. A *door* is an entry point that
9292 [12.6] builds a run, and there are three, `init!`, `restore!` and `replay!`. A run's
9293 [12.6] `feed` is the recording `replay!` attached, or `nothing`. The [input mode](#g-input-mode) says
9294 [12.6] where the next [frame](#g-frame) (one grid step, the unit of scheduling) takes its inputs
9295 [12.6] from.
9296 [12.6] 
9297 [12.6] #### The run and the simulation
9298 [12.6] 
9299 [12.6] **[`Run`](#g-run) is what one run owns** ([D-255][d-255], [D-260][d-260], [D-317][d-317]). It is a mutable struct of
9300 [12.6] six fields. Two are `const` and last the run's whole life. One is the [log](#g-log) (the
9301 [12.6] retained sequence of published snapshots). The other is the [trace](#g-trace) (the records
9302 [12.6] of each frame's drained inputs), which is `nothing` under `trace = false`. The
9303 [12.6] door that builds the run takes the four recording keywords, `trace`, `log`,
9304 [12.6] `log_every` and `log_max` ([Appendix B][sB], [D-261][d-261]). A run's recording is therefore
9305 [12.6] that door's declaration, and the next door may declare otherwise ([D-261][d-261]).
9306 [12.6] 
9307 [12.6] Four fields evolve during the run. `feed` is described above. `termination` is
9308 [12.6] the record the loop's tail writes. `frame` and `boundary` are the trajectory's
9309 [12.6] counters, the frame index and the boundary ordinal. `init!`, `restore!` and
9310 [12.6] `replay!` construct a run, and nothing else rebinds it. `live!` ([§12.7][s12-7]) and the
9311 [12.6] halt at a recording's end write `feed`. The loop's tail writes `termination`
9312 [12.6] once, and `closed(run)` is `termination !== nothing`. Each door sets the two
9313 [12.6] counters as it builds the run, `init!` to zero and `restore!` and `replay!` to
9314 [12.6] those of the [checkpoint](#g-checkpoint) (the `Model`'s state at a frame top, beside the run's
9315 [12.6] frame index and boundary ordinal), and the loop advances them ([D-317][d-317]).
9316 [12.6] `restore!` of a bare state sets the frame index to the state's frame and the
9317 [12.6] boundary ordinal to zero, as `init!` does ([D-319][d-319]).
9318 [12.6] 
9319 [12.6] The origin `t₀` is not a run field ([D-260][d-260]). The clock of the [executor](#g-executor) (the
9320 [12.6] compiled form of the stage execution order) holds it beside `t`. `init!` sets it
9321 [12.6] on the clock before [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero), the initialization boundary run as the
9322 [12.6] ordinary macro-sequence with an empty integrate ([§14.5][s14-5]). A checkpoint carries it
9323 [12.6] with the clock ([§11.5][s11-5], [D-273][d-273]). It is a `Float64`, as `h` and `t_end` are
9324 [12.6] ([D-260][d-260]).
9325 [12.6] 
9326 [12.6] **A `Simulation` is constructed with a placeholder run** ([D-255][d-255]). The
9327 [12.6] placeholder is an empty log, no trace, no feed and no termination, and
9328 [12.6] `init!`, `restore!` and `replay!` replace it ([D-255][d-255]). The field is
9329 [12.6] always a `Run` and never `nothing`, so every accessor has a run to read, and
9330 [12.6] the lifecycle state below says whether that run ever started. The placeholder
9331 [12.6] carries no configuration and no compiled state ([D-261][d-261]). `init!`
9332 [12.6] allocates fresh objects rather than clearing them ([D-255][d-255]).
9333 [12.6] 
9334 [12.6] **`Simulation` is a mutable struct of four fields**, the [`Model`](#g-model) (the deployment
9335 [12.6] and its executor at one scalar), the run, the data plane ([§11][s11]) and the control
9336 [12.6] plane ([D-317][d-317]). The [control plane](#g-control-plane) is the atomic surface carrying pause, pace and
9337 [12.6] stop. The `Model` holds the [deployment](#g-deployment), the executor, its status and its
9338 [12.6] [diagnostic cell](#g-diagnostic-cell). The deployment is the scalar-free artifact the grid parameters
9339 [12.6] fix, carrying the build. A diagnostic cell is a writer's own holding place for
9340 [12.6] runtime diagnostics. A simulation owns its `Model`, claimed at construction and
9341 [12.6] never shared ([§9.2][s9-2], [D-318][d-318]).
9342 [12.6] 
9343 [12.6] Every other value belongs to one of the four ([D-256][d-256]). The build is reached
9344 [12.6] through the deployment, never held twice. The grid parameters, the [schedule](#g-schedule) (the
9345 [12.6] typed per-component `(D, Φ, Δt)` tick table) and the event parameters are the
9346 [12.6] deployment's ([§9.2][s9-2]). `chunk_size`, the stepper and the arrival buffers are the
9347 [12.6] executor's. `join_timeout` is `Control`'s ([§12.1][s12-1]). The loop's diagnostic cell,
9348 [12.6] its account and the published-snapshot holder ([§11.2][s11-2]) are the data plane's, and
9349 [12.6] the frame's own diagnostic cell is the `Model`'s ([§11.8][s11-8]). The log, the trace and
9350 [12.6] the two counters are the run's. The stop policy, with its `t_end` and ignored
9351 [12.6] requesters, is the advance's argument, and the [termination record](#g-termination-record) (the
9352 [12.6] stopped-sim value naming how the run ended) keeps the terminating one ([D-260][d-260]).
9353 [12.6] The pacer's schedule and counters are `run!`'s own, created per call and passed
9354 [12.6] to the loop and its publications as the policy is. The status of each [snapshot](#g-snapshot)
9355 [12.6] (the immutable per-boundary publication) carries the pacer's frozen record
9356 [12.6] ([§10.7][s10-7], [D-269][d-269]).
9357 [12.6] 
9358 [12.6] **A struct holds what it owns or what it must retain across calls**
9359 [12.6] ([D-261][d-261]). A callee takes what it reads as an argument, never off a
9360 [12.6] field added to the container it already holds. **An object is built after its
9361 [12.6] inputs exist**, and the placeholder run above is the one exception
9362 [12.6] ([D-261][d-261]).
9363 [12.6] 
9364 [12.6] #### Lifecycle states and the input mode
9365 [12.6] 
9366 [12.6] **A `Simulation` moves through five states**, *built*, *initialized*, *running*,
9367 [12.6] and terminally *stopped* or *errored* ([§13.4][s13-4], [D-088][d-088]). *Built* means [stores](#g-store) (the
9368 [12.6] framework-owned homes of a component's state) allocated and boundary zero not
9369 [12.6] completed ([D-223][d-223]). It is the cold state, and the state a throw inside boundary
9370 [12.6] zero returns the simulation to ([§13.4][s13-4]). *Initialized* means `init!` has
9371 [12.6] completed boundary zero ([§14.5][s14-5]), or `restore!` or `replay!` has restored a
9372 [12.6] checkpoint.
9373 [12.6] 
9374 [12.6] **`lifecycle(sim)` derives the state rather than storing it** ([D-317][d-317]).
9375 [12.6] It reads three facts, the control plane's running flag, the `Model`'s status
9376 [12.6] and whether the run is closed. The status is `:built`, `:consistent` or
9377 [12.6] `:inconsistent`, and only the `Model`'s own entry points and catch sites write
9378 [12.6] it. A claimed model's status is written only through its simulation's entry
9379 [12.6] points, since the model's entry points refuse a direct call on a claimed model
9380 [12.6] ([D-318][d-318]).
9381 [12.6] 
9382 [12.6] | running flag | `Model` status | run closed | state |
9383 [12.6] |---|---|---|---|
9384 [12.6] | set | any | any | running |
9385 [12.6] | clear | `:built` | any | built |
9386 [12.6] | clear | `:inconsistent` | any | errored |
9387 [12.6] | clear | `:consistent` | no | initialized |
9388 [12.6] | clear | `:consistent` | yes | stopped |
9389 [12.6] 
9390 [12.6] **`errored` is terminal** ([D-059][d-059]). Reproduction is trace
9391 [12.6] [replay](#g-replay), not resurrection ([§12.7][s12-7]). Replay is the ordinary
9392 [12.6] loop re-driven from the trace.
9393 [12.6] 
9394 [12.6] Beside the state, the run carries an input mode, `:live` or `:replay`, read as
9395 [12.6] `mode(sim)`. **The mode is read off the run's `feed`**, `:replay` while a
9396 [12.6] recording is attached and `:live` otherwise ([D-260][d-260]). The mode names where the
9397 [12.6] next frame's [drain](#g-drain) takes its [batches](#g-batch) from, the [staging cells](#g-staging-cell) (where each
9398 [12.6] device's pending batch waits between drains) or the attached recording ([§12.7][s12-7],
9399 [12.6] [D-218][d-218]). The drain is the frame-top swap that publishes staged device writes into
9400 [12.6] the root inputs, and a batch is a device's staged set of writes. State and mode
9401 [12.6] are orthogonal. The state says whether the simulation may advance, and the mode
9402 [12.6] says what it will advance on.
9403 [12.6] 
9404 [12.6] **`init!` is mandatory** ([D-088][d-088]). `run!` or `step!` on a simulation
9405 [12.6] whose boundary zero has not completed is `MissingInit`, an error in the kind
9406 [12.6] set ([§13.2][s13-2]) naming `init!`. That is distinct from
9407 [12.6] `UninitializedInputs`, which fires *inside* `init!` ([§14.6][s14-6]).
9408 [12.6] `restore!` and `replay!` are the two alternative doors. Each restores a
9409 [12.6] checkpoint and runs no boundary zero ([§12.7][s12-7], [D-274][d-274]). A throw
9410 [12.6] inside boundary zero, which only `init!` runs, leaves the simulation `built`
9411 [12.6] ([§13.4][s13-4], [D-223][d-223]), so the next `run!` or `step!` meets the same
9412 [12.6] refusal.
9413 [12.6] 
9414 [12.6] #### Partial advance
9415 [12.6] 
9416 [12.6] Deviceless, `run!` is fully synchronous. [§11.1][s11-1] says where the loop and the
9417 [12.6] device tasks run, and defines the [unattended run](#g-unattended-run) (a run with empty staging and
9418 [12.6] no snapshot readers).
9419 [12.6] 
9420 [12.6] `step!(sim; frames = 1)` advances whole frames synchronously through the
9421 [12.6] ordinary frame sequence (drain, integrate, boundaries, publication) and
9422 [12.6] returns ([D-088][d-088]). A stepped simulation is bit-identical to the same
9423 [12.6] frames under `run!` ([§2.4][s2-4]), and it never waits on the pacer, whatever
9424 [12.6] pace the control plane holds ([D-269][d-269]). `step!(sim; t_plus = 10.0)` is
9425 [12.6] the duration spelling. It is mutually exclusive with `frames`, and passing
9426 [12.6] both is `ArgumentInvalid`. It advances whole frames until the boundary time
9427 [12.6] first covers the duration, which is the migration suite's advance-by-duration
9428 [12.6] idiom.
9429 [12.6] 
9430 [12.6] Partial advance is the test-harness mode of advance, assert, advance. It is
9431 [12.6] equally the REPL mode of fly a while, inspect, continue. Neither is a script,
9432 [12.6] so the scenario-component doctrine does not absorb them ([§12.5][s12-5]).
9433 [12.6] 
9434 [12.6] **A stepping session is deviceless by construction** ([D-093][d-093]). Device
9435 [12.6] tasks are per-`run!` artifacts ([§11.1][s11-1]), and a device loop's
9436 [12.6] `while running(handle)` is false outside a run. Between `step!` calls the
9437 [12.6] simulation is in a stopped-sim state, `initialized` (below), so `attach!` is
9438 [12.6] legal there and does what it always does. It registers ([§11.3][s11-3]), and
9439 [12.6] the task appears at the next `run!`.
9440 [12.6] 
9441 [12.6] The frame-top drain still runs, so `step!` frames stay bit-identical to `run!`
9442 [12.6] frames. In a stepping session the staging cell written is the [harness cell](#g-harness-cell) (the
9443 [12.6] always-present staging cell of the [harness writer](#g-harness-writer)). The harness write path is
9444 [12.6] `stage!(sim, "face" => value, …)`, and [§11.3][s11-3] specifies the writer. Staged
9445 [12.6] batches are ordinary batches. They are traced, so replay and bit-identity hold,
9446 [12.6] and they are applied at the next frame top.
9447 [12.6] 
9448 [12.6] The read half is `latest(sim)`. It hands back the same immutable snapshot
9449 [12.6] value a device handle acquires ([§11.2][s11-2]), navigated directly for
9450 [12.6] assertions. Advance-assert-advance is `stage!` → `step!` → `latest`
9451 [12.6] ([D-096][d-096]). Both entry points work under `run!` too. The harness cell is
9452 [12.6] not step-scoped. An inspection accessor leaves the rejection of closure-based
9453 [12.6] termination untouched ([§13.5][s13-5], [D-096][d-096]).
9454 [12.6] 
9455 [12.6] #### Status, termination and `run!` after `step!`
9456 [12.6] 
9457 [12.6] Between `step!` calls a simulation reports `initialized` ([D-088][d-088]). No
9458 [12.6] loop task exists, so `running` would lie. Nothing is terminal, so `stopped`
9459 [12.6] would lie too. The state reads "boundary-consistent and ready to advance", not
9460 [12.6] "sitting at boundary zero". `run!` may therefore follow `step!`, continuing
9461 [12.6] from the current boundary, and so may another `step!`.
9462 [12.6] 
9463 [12.6] The stop policy is honoured throughout, as bit-identity requires. `t_end`
9464 [12.6] reached, or a [stop request](#g-stop-request) (an output port of the framework enum `StopFlag`)
9465 [12.6] [holding](#g-edge-semantics) (publishing `STOP_REQUESTED`) at frame 3 of `step!(sim; frames = 10)`,
9466 [12.6] ends the run there through the ordinary [§12.4][s12-4] tail and leaves the simulation
9467 [12.6] `stopped`. `step!` therefore returns the number of frames *actually advanced*.
9468 [12.6] That is the requested count in the ordinary case, and fewer when the run
9469 [12.6] terminated inside the call. That return is how a harness detects the truncation
9470 [12.6] without inspecting the clock.
9471 [12.6] 
9472 [12.6] #### Re-running and checkpoints
9473 [12.6] 
9474 [12.6] **`stopped → init! → run!` remains the authored re-run** ([D-088][d-088]).
9475 [12.6] `init!` re-runs boundary zero from its [condition](#g-condition) (a
9476 [12.6] path-addressed overlay that sets the build to a state). The warm restart is
9477 [12.6] `checkpoint` → `restore!` → `run!` ([§12.7][s12-7], [D-274][d-274]).
9478 [12.6] `restore!` is a door like `init!`, and it runs no boundary zero. Each builds a
9479 [12.6] fresh run, so the trace, the log and the termination record ([§13.5][s13-5])
9480 [12.6] are new objects rather than cleared ones, and each clears any batches still in
9481 [12.6] staging cells. The [recorders](#g-recorders) (the trace and the log jointly)
9482 [12.6] restart with the run they record, and no stale batch survives to clobber the
9483 [12.6] frame it predates.
9484 [12.6] 
9485 [12.6] `init!` and `restore!` also return the input mode to `:live`, and a fresh
9486 [12.6] `replay!` sets the mode exactly as it sets the trajectory. `live!` is the one
9487 [12.6] call that moves the mode alone. It takes a replaying simulation live where it
9488 [12.6] stands ([§12.7][s12-7]). A terminal state makes the mode moot. From `stopped`,
9489 [12.6] nothing advances until `init!`, `restore!` or `replay!` opens a new run, and
9490 [12.6] `errored` is terminal ([D-218][d-218], [D-219][d-219]).
9491 [12.6] 
9492 [12.6] `checkpoint` and `restore!` exist on the `Model` and on the `Simulation`, and
9493 [12.6] **a `Checkpoint` is the `Model`'s state beside the run's cursor**
9494 [12.6] ([D-319][d-319]). `checkpoint(model)` returns the state alone, a
9495 [12.6] `ModelState`. It holds the flat buffer, the `s` and `m` stores, the whole
9496 [12.6] signal table, the guard priors, `t`, `t₀`, the fingerprint (the structural
9497 [12.6] layout) and the deployment. It is legal at `:consistent` with the clock on a
9498 [12.6] grid top. `checkpoint(sim)` wraps that state with the run's frame index and
9499 [12.6] boundary ordinal ([D-319][d-319]). It is a stopped-sim service, legal in
9500 [12.6] `initialized` and `stopped` ([§14][s14]), and refused as `ServiceLifecycle` in
9501 [12.6] any other state. It is refused wherever the executor is not at the rest a
9502 [12.6] published frame top leaves (`CheckpointMidFrame`, [Appendix C][sC]). That is
9503 [12.6] after a `t*` stop, which leaves the clock mid-frame with the remainder
9504 [12.6] abandoned, and after a frame abandoned unpublished ([§12.4][s12-4]). The test
9505 [12.6] is that the clock sits on a grid time and that the latest snapshot is of that
9506 [12.6] very boundary.
9507 [12.6] 
9508 [12.6] `restore!(model, model_state)` checks the state's fingerprint and deployment
9509 [12.6] against the model, and a mismatch is `CheckpointMismatch`. It also checks that
9510 [12.6] the state's `t` is a grid time `t₀ + k·h` of the state's own origin and step,
9511 [12.6] and a state off its grid is `CheckpointMidFrame`. Every mismatch of either kind
9512 [12.6] is collected into one throw before any write. The entry point then copies the
9513 [12.6] state back, settles the restored boundary through the hooks and writes
9514 [12.6] `:consistent` last ([D-318][d-318], [D-319][d-319]).
9515 [12.6] 
9516 [12.6] `restore!(sim, cp)` takes the four recording keywords `init!` takes. It checks
9517 [12.6] the state's fingerprint and deployment against the simulation as replay checks
9518 [12.6] the [trace header](#g-trace-header) (the trace's fixed preamble), and a
9519 [12.6] mismatch is `CheckpointMismatch` ([§12.7][s12-7]). It checks the state's `t`
9520 [12.6] against its grid as the model's entry point does, collecting both kinds into
9521 [12.6] one throw. It then copies the state back, opens a fresh run with the
9522 [12.6] checkpoint as its trace header, and publishes one snapshot at the
9523 [12.6] checkpoint's `t`. It runs no [sweep](#g-sweep) (one pass through the execution order against
9524 [12.6] the current state), evaluates no guard, runs no update and
9525 [12.6] resets no prior. The next frame is the original lattice's next frame, because
9526 [12.6] the clock came with the checkpoint. The boundary ordinal continues, being the
9527 [12.6] trajectory's ([§12.3][s12-3], [D-274][d-274]). The snapshot `restore!`
9528 [12.6] publishes is a re-publication. It carries the ordinal of the boundary the
9529 [12.6] checkpoint was taken at, and the next frame publishes under the next ordinal,
9530 [12.6] as it did in the original run.
9531 [12.6] 
9532 [12.6] `restore!(sim, model_state)` is the door a state prepared on a standalone
9533 [12.6] model takes into a simulation ([§9.2][s9-2], [D-319][d-319]). It takes the
9534 [12.6] same keywords and makes the same check of the state's fingerprint and
9535 [12.6] deployment, and it refuses a state whose `t` is off the grid as
9536 [12.6] `CheckpointMidFrame`. It opens a fresh trajectory at the state's frame, and
9537 [12.6] the restored boundary publishes under ordinal 0, as boundary zero's does. A
9538 [12.6] standalone model published nothing, so the trajectory has no ordinals to
9539 [12.6] continue. A `Checkpoint` holds a `ModelState{Float64}`, so it is nominal by
9540 [12.6] type. A state taken at another scalar is refused by dispatch with the same
9541 [12.6] `CheckpointMismatch`, on either `restore!` that takes a bare state.
9542 [12.6] 
9543 [12.6] #### Device attachments across runs
9544 [12.6] 
9545 [12.6] **Device attachments persist across re-initialization** ([D-088][d-088]), because
9546 [12.6] attachment is orthogonal to the run lifecycle ([§11.3][s11-3]). Persistence means
9547 [12.6] *roster* persistence, the [roster](#g-roster) being the list of attached device entries.
9548 [12.6] Binding, [claims](#g-claim) (the set of faces a device may write) and device id survive.
9549 [12.6] Tasks and OS resources do not, the per-run topology being specified in [§11.1][s11-1] and
9550 [12.6] the teardown in [§12.4][s12-4]. Each `run!` re-initializes every rostered device and
9551 [12.6] spawns a task for each whose `init!` succeeds ([§11.1][s11-1], [D-093][d-093]). `attach!` while
9552 [12.6] stopped only registers, and the task appears at the next `run!`.
9553 [12.6] 
9554 [12.6] Which devices get a task at each `run!` is decided as [§11.1][s11-1]
9555 [12.6] specifies. **A front end attached by hand is still rostered**
9556 [12.6] ([D-310][d-310]), so the next `run!` spawns its task again.
9557 [12.6] 
9558 [12.6] #### The stop policy per advance
9559 [12.6] 
9560 [12.6] **The stop policy is declared per advance** ([D-255][d-255], [D-316][d-316]). `t_end` and
9561 [12.6] `ignore_stop_requests` are keywords of `run!`, `replay!` and `step!`. Each call
9562 [12.6] builds and validates the [`StopPolicy`](#g-stop-policy) (the immutable value of `t_end` plus the
9563 [12.6] ignored requester paths) it passes to the loop ([§13.5][s13-5], [D-255][d-255], [D-316][d-316]). The value
9564 [12.6] lives as long as the call, and afterwards only on the termination record of the
9565 [12.6] advance that ended the run ([D-260][d-260]). A second run, or a `step!` sequence between
9566 [12.6] two runs, can therefore stop on a different clock or honour a different set of
9567 [12.6] stop requests without a rebuild.
9568 [12.6] 
9569 [12.6] 
9570 [12.7] ### 12.7 Replay: the trace re-drives the ordinary loop
9571 [12.7] 
9572 [12.7] This section specifies [replay](#g-replay), re-running the ordinary loop on a recorded input
9573 [12.7] [trace](#g-trace), so that a recorded session can be inspected, its errors reproduced and
9574 [12.7] its run continued live. A trace is the primary record of a session, a fixed
9575 [12.7] [trace header](#g-trace-header) plus the [batches](#g-batch) drained at each [frame](#g-frame). A batch is a [device](#g-device)'s
9576 [12.7] staged set of writes, and a device is any attached participant in the periphery.
9577 [12.7] A frame is one iteration of the loop, from drain to publication. The [drain](#g-drain) is
9578 [12.7] the frame-top swap that applies staged device writes to the [root inputs](#g-root-input). The
9579 [12.7] root inputs are the root component's own inputs, the only thing the periphery
9580 [12.7] may write. `replay!` is the entry point the trace exists for ([§11.5][s11-5]):
9581 [12.7] 
9582 [12.7] ```julia
9583 [12.7] trc  = trace(sim)                     # the recorded session: header + per-frame batches
9584 [12.7] sim2 = Simulation(world)              # the same build
9585 [12.7] replay!(sim2, trc)                    # restore the header, then re-drive every recorded frame
9586 [12.7] replay!(sim2, trc; to_boundary = k)   # partial: the §13.4 replay-pointer idiom
9587 [12.7] replay!(sim2, trc; to_time = 100.0)   # partial: the same halt addressed by time
9588 [12.7] cp   = checkpoint(sim)                # the state at a frame-top rest
9589 [12.7] restore!(sim2, cp)                    # that state alone: a warm restart, no feed
9590 [12.7] ```
9591 [12.7] 
9592 [12.7] The parts below cover the one substitution replay makes, halting and the
9593 [12.7] [input mode](#g-input-mode) (where the drain takes its batches from) across
9594 [12.7] halts, what stays the loop's, and the validation up front. They then cover the
9595 [12.7] [what-if replay](#g-what-if-replay), re-driving the recorded inputs through a
9596 [12.7] modified model, with the `restore = false` form, which attaches the feed to
9597 [12.7] the simulation as it stands. The rejected shapes close the section.
9598 [12.7] 
9599 [12.7] #### One substitution
9600 [12.7] 
9601 [12.7] **`replay!` is the ordinary loop with exactly one substitution**, not a second
9602 [12.7] loop ([D-101][d-101], [D-274][d-274]). That is what keeps every property proved of the loop true
9603 [12.7] of replay. The substitution is the drain, which reads the trace. Under the
9604 [12.7] default form, `replay!(sim2, trc)` above, the loop also starts from a restored
9605 [12.7] state.
9606 [12.7] 
9607 [12.7] The default form restores from the header. It restores the trace's [checkpoint](#g-checkpoint)
9608 [12.7] (the `Model`'s state at a frame top, beside the run's frame index and boundary
9609 [12.7] ordinal), the state the recording opened from. It runs no [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the
9610 [12.7] initialization boundary at `t₀`). This form stands in the `init!` position of
9611 [12.7] the lifecycle ([§12.6][s12-6]) and does what `restore!` does, with the trace header as
9612 [12.7] the checkpoint ([D-274][d-274]). There is no [condition](#g-condition) resolution, and the
9613 [12.7] [root-input totality](#g-root-input-totality) (every root input must hold a value) ([§14.6][s14-6]) held when the
9614 [12.7] recording's `init!` ran. A condition is the datum that says "set this build to
9615 [12.7] this state". Whatever fired at `t₀` is part of the restored state, so nothing is
9616 [12.7] applied twice and nothing is skipped ([D-274][d-274]).
9617 [12.7] 
9618 [12.7] The drain reads the trace. Each frame top applies the recording's batches for
9619 [12.7] that [frame ordinal](#g-frame-ordinal), the frame index a batch replays at ([D-101][d-101]). It does not swap
9620 [12.7] the [staging cells](#g-staging-cell) of the [roster](#g-roster) (the list of attached device entries). A staging
9621 [12.7] cell is where a device's pending write batch waits between drains. Ordinal
9622 [12.7] keying is exact because the frame sequence is itself deterministic under replay
9623 [12.7] (`t*` boundaries derive from state, [§10.4][s10-4]). Frame *k* of the replay *is* frame
9624 [12.7] *k* of the recording. Recorded batches apply verbatim, with no surface re-check
9625 [12.7] ([D-101][d-101]). The [write-surface](#g-write-surface) rule (the bound on the [faces](#g-face) a writer's batch entries
9626 [12.7] may reach) ([§11.3][s11-3]) ran at recording time, and [claims](#g-claim) (the set of faces a device
9627 [12.7] may write) are a live-roster fact of the recorded session that replay does not
9628 [12.7] reconstruct. A face is the name a port wears on its component's boundary.
9629 [12.7] 
9630 [12.7] A simulation is in one of two input modes, `:live` or `:replay`, and **the mode
9631 [12.7] decides which source that one drain branch reads** ([D-218][d-218]). Under `:live` it
9632 [12.7] reads the staging cells, and under `:replay` the attached recording. **`replay!`
9633 [12.7] attaches the recording and enters `:replay`** ([D-218][d-218]). `step!` and `run!`
9634 [12.7] consume it from there, frame by frame, exactly as the drain paragraph above
9635 [12.7] describes. There is still one loop and one drain ([D-218][d-218]).
9636 [12.7] 
9637 [12.7] A mode that outlives the call makes a partial replay a resumable position rather
9638 [12.7] than the end of an operation ([D-218][d-218]). Whatever advances the simulation next
9639 [12.7] reads the same mode and finds the same records. That is what lets the
9640 [12.7] reproduction workflow of [§13.4][s13-4] run through the ordinary entry points, with no
9641 [12.7] replay-only spelling of `step!`.
9642 [12.7] 
9643 [12.7] #### Halting, and the mode across halts
9644 [12.7] 
9645 [12.7] **In `:replay` the recording is the bound** ([D-218][d-218]). Every advance's frame
9646 [12.7] budget is capped at the recording's last frame, and `to_boundary = k` caps it
9647 [12.7] earlier. That keyword is the consumer of the replay pointer ([§13.4][s13-4]). `to_time`
9648 [12.7] is the same cap addressed by time (below). Either keyword names a frame from the
9649 [12.7] one the feed starts from through the recording's last, and anything else is
9650 [12.7] refused (`ArgumentInvalid`). The feed starts from the header's frame under the
9651 [12.7] default form, and from the simulation's own under `restore = false` (below).
9652 [12.7] 
9653 [12.7] `to_boundary = k` takes the frame-entry boundary index ([§13.4][s13-4]), a count of frame
9654 [12.7] tops. It is not the published-boundary ordinal that a [snapshot](#g-snapshot) (the immutable
9655 [12.7] per-boundary publication) carries ([§12.3][s12-3]). **Replay runs through frame `k` and
9656 [12.7] halts exactly at the frame top after it** ([D-128][d-128]). Replay therefore always halts
9657 [12.7] at a frame top. A [localized](#g-localized) `t*` boundary (the crossing instant bracketed by
9658 [12.7] root-finding over trial sweeps) inside the frame is reproduced but not
9659 [12.7] stoppable-at ([D-128][d-128]). The trace stays frame-indexed, replay pointers and error
9660 [12.7] messages use the frame-entry boundary index, and snapshots carry the
9661 [12.7] published-boundary ordinal ([§10.4][s10-4], [§12.3][s12-3]).
9662 [12.7] 
9663 [12.7] Replay may also end earlier still under the ordinary policies, since `t_end` and
9664 [12.7] `ignore_stop_requests` are keywords of `replay!` exactly as of `run!` ([§12.6][s12-6],
9665 [12.7] [D-255][d-255], [D-316][d-316]). A termination the recorded session hit through a [stop request](#g-stop-request) (an
9666 [12.7] output port of the framework enum `StopFlag`) reproduces itself anyway,
9667 [12.7] deterministically ([D-127][d-127]). A replay honours requests by default too, and the
9668 [12.7] trace header records no policy. So a replay of a live run that ignored some must
9669 [12.7] pass the same `ignore_stop_requests`.
9670 [12.7] 
9671 [12.7] **`to_time` halts at the last frame top at or before the time given**, the same
9672 [12.7] halt addressed by time, and it is mutually exclusive with `to_boundary` ([D-219][d-219]).
9673 [12.7] It computes `k = ⌊(to_time − t₀)/h⌋` against the header's `t₀`, under either
9674 [12.7] form. A time falling between two frame tops floors onto the earlier one.
9675 [12.7] 
9676 [12.7] The rounding is the deliberate opposite of `t_end`'s, which ends a run at the
9677 [12.7] first grid boundary reaching or exceeding it ([§12.4][s12-4]). `t_end` bounds
9678 [12.7] a run, and `to_time` positions an inspection. The point of halting is to
9679 [12.7] stand *before* the anomaly, so a halt that stepped past it would defeat the
9680 [12.7] call ([D-219][d-219]).
9681 [12.7] 
9682 [12.7] `to_time` is validated as the other keywords are. The value must be real, finite
9683 [12.7] and at least `t₀`, and it must name a time the recording covers. The checks run
9684 [12.7] before the replay writes anything, the default form's restore included.
9685 [12.7] 
9686 [12.7] The halt flips the mode only at the recording's end. A frame budget that runs
9687 [12.7] out halts the loop at a frame top, leaving the simulation `initialized`
9688 [12.7] ([§12.6][s12-6]). **The mode becomes `:live` exactly when that halt lands at
9689 [12.7] the recording's last frame**, with the records exhausted ([D-218][d-218]). It
9690 [12.7] stays `:replay` otherwise, and the next `step!` or `run!` goes on consuming
9691 [12.7] the recording. So no advance ever flips mid-run. A `run!` whose `t_end` lies
9692 [12.7] past the recording halts at the last recorded frame, now `:live`, and the
9693 [12.7] *next* call is the live continuation ([D-218][d-218]).
9694 [12.7] 
9695 [12.7] `live!` is the one call that moves the mode alone. The flip above is automatic.
9696 [12.7] `live!(sim)` performs it by hand. **`live!` sets the mode to `:live` and
9697 [12.7] detaches the recording's remainder, touching nothing else** ([D-219][d-219]). The
9698 [12.7] trajectory stands where the replay left it, and so does the trace with its
9699 [12.7] header, inherited under the default form. The next `run!` or `step!` is
9700 [12.7] therefore the live continuation from the replayed boundary, and its drains
9701 [12.7] append to the re-recorded prefix. `live!` is a stopped-sim operation, legal only
9702 [12.7] on an `initialized` simulation in `:replay`. Every other state refuses under the
9703 [12.7] ordinary lifecycle gates ([§12.6][s12-6]), with `MissingInit` at `built` and
9704 [12.7] `ServiceLifecycle` in the rest. A simulation already `:live` refuses too, with
9705 [12.7] `ArgumentInvalid`. The call would have nothing to do, and a loud refusal beats a
9706 [12.7] silent no-op ([D-219][d-219]).
9707 [12.7] 
9708 [12.7] That call is what makes the rewrite workflow real. An interactive session is
9709 [12.7] interrupted at t = 110, and the last ten seconds are to be flown again:
9710 [12.7] 
9711 [12.7] ```julia
9712 [12.7] trc  = trace(sim)                     # the interrupted session, out to t = 110
9713 [12.7] sim2 = Simulation(world)
9714 [12.7] replay!(sim2, trc; to_time = 100.0)   # halt at the last frame top at or before 100
9715 [12.7] latest(sim2)                          # inspect the boundary landed on
9716 [12.7] live!(sim2)                           # drop the remainder; the mode is :live
9717 [12.7] run!(sim2; t_end = 130.0)             # live from t = 100, re-recording onward
9718 [12.7] ```
9719 [12.7] 
9720 [12.7] The trace `sim2` leaves behind is a complete recording of *itself*, the replayed
9721 [12.7] prefix out to t = 100 and then the frames flown live after it, since replay
9722 [12.7] re-records (below).
9723 [12.7] 
9724 [12.7] A replay's halt leaves the simulation `initialized`, never `stopped` ([D-218][d-218]).
9725 [12.7] That state is boundary-consistent and ready to advance, the same state `step!`
9726 [12.7] leaves ([§12.6][s12-6]). This is what makes three promised workflows real ([D-101][d-101]).
9727 [12.7] State-trajectory inspection asks "what was the private state at t = 37.2?", and
9728 [12.7] answers it by replaying there and reading the live stores ([§11.2][s11-2]). Error
9729 [12.7] reproduction replays to the frame-entry boundary `k` the error names, then
9730 [12.7] `step!`s the failing frame on its recorded inputs, under instrumentation
9731 [12.7] ([§13.4][s13-4]). Continuation is `run!` after `replay!`, a live session from the
9732 [12.7] replayed boundary. After a full replay that is the next call, the records
9733 [12.7] exhausted at the halt. After a partial one the remainder of the recording is
9734 [12.7] consumed first, across as many `step!` and `run!` calls as the caller makes, and
9735 [12.7] the session goes live at the recording's end. Or `live!` drops that remainder,
9736 [12.7] and the continuation starts at the halt.
9737 [12.7] 
9738 [12.7] #### What stays the loop's
9739 [12.7] 
9740 [12.7] **Replay re-records** ([D-101][d-101]). The drain records normally. Under the
9741 [12.7] default form the new trace inherits the old header and accumulates the
9742 [12.7] re-drained batches, a bit-identical prefix. Under `restore = false` the new
9743 [12.7] trace's header is the simulation's own checkpoint (below). A
9744 [12.7] replayed-then-continued session therefore leaves behind a complete, valid
9745 [12.7] trace of *itself*, with no special stitching.
9746 [12.7] 
9747 [12.7] **[Pacing](#g-pacing) and the [control plane](#g-control-plane) are unchanged** ([§10.7][s10-7], [§12.1][s12-1], [D-101][d-101]). The
9748 [12.7] control plane is the separate atomic surface carrying pause, pace and stop.
9749 [12.7] Pacing (waits inserted between completed frames, never altering the boundary
9750 [12.7] sequence) sits outside the semantics, so paused, slow-motion or real-time replay
9751 [12.7] is free. Paced replay with an attached visualizer *is* session playback, and
9752 [12.7] `replay!` takes `pace` and `margin` as `run!` does ([§12.1][s12-1], [D-269][d-269]). Stop
9753 [12.7] truncates, as anywhere.
9754 [12.7] 
9755 [12.7] Devices are readers ([D-218][d-218]). Rostered devices init and spawn normally
9756 [12.7] ([§11.1][s11-1]) and consume snapshots ([§11.2][s11-2], [§12.3][s12-3]). That
9757 [12.7] is the visualizer case. But no live staging cell is drained while the
9758 [12.7] simulation is in `:replay`. A batch found staged is discarded with a
9759 [12.7] rate-limited warning (`ReplayDiscardedStaging`, [Appendix C][sC]). Mixing live
9760 [12.7] writes into a replay would destroy the property replay exists to provide. A
9761 [12.7] session that wants live input is a continuation (`run!` after replay), not a
9762 [12.7] replay.
9763 [12.7] 
9764 [12.7] #### Validation up front
9765 [12.7] 
9766 [12.7] **Validation is loud and up front** ([D-101][d-101]). Before the first frame, the
9767 [12.7] checkpoint's [deployment](#g-deployment) (the scalar-free artifact the grid parameters fix) is
9768 [12.7] compared against the target's. Its fingerprint, the structural layout, is
9769 [12.7] compared against the `Build`. The fingerprint covers the store layout, the
9770 [12.7] root-input faces, and what a copy by position relies on ([§11.5][s11-5]). The trace's
9771 [12.7] batch entries are validated against the root input-face list, and each batch's
9772 [12.7] frame ordinal against the recording's frames, from the one after the header's to
9773 [12.7] the last ([D-217][d-217]). Each writer's face-name → position schema, on the trace's
9774 [12.7] `schemas` list ([§11.5][s11-5]), is validated in the same pass. A recorded schema that
9775 [12.7] disagrees with the target model's own root-input faces is a replay error. The
9776 [12.7] checks are attach-style, and a failure reports [did-you-mean](#g-did-you-mean), the offending name
9777 [12.7] plus the list-in-hand it should have matched. The kinds are
9778 [12.7] `CheckpointMismatch`, `ReplaySchemaMismatch` and `ReplayUnknownFace`
9779 [12.7] ([Appendix C][sC]). The pass runs under both forms of `replay!`.
9780 [12.7] 
9781 [12.7] The same pass pays the trace-record conversion in reverse. Every writer's
9782 [12.7] sparse records ([§11.5][s11-5]) are normalized to positional batches against
9783 [12.7] the trace's `schemas` list, once, off the loop ([D-101][d-101],
9784 [12.7] [D-176][d-176]). Replay has the whole trace in hand before frame 1. The replay
9785 [12.7] drain therefore applies compiled scatters exactly as the live drain does, and
9786 [12.7] no face name is resolved per frame under replay either.
9787 [12.7] 
9788 [12.7] *Structural* mismatch is an error. *Parametric* difference is not
9789 [12.7] ([D-101][d-101]).
9790 [12.7] 
9791 [12.7] **The checkpoint check compares two deployments as values** ([D-254][d-254]).
9792 [12.7] The checkpoint carries the recorded run's `Deployment`, the target
9793 [12.7] simulation's `Model` carries its own, and the check is their comparison, on
9794 [12.7] the *structural* side of that line. The value covers the
9795 [12.7] trajectory-determining parameters, `Δt_base`, `h`, `N_base`, the algorithm,
9796 [12.7] `localization_tol`, `localization_budget` ([§10.4][s10-4]) and
9797 [12.7] `firing_budget` ([§10.6][s10-6], [D-133][d-133]). It also covers the
9798 [12.7] [schedule](#g-schedule) (the typed per-component `(D, Φ, Δt)` tick table) with
9799 [12.7] every column, the anchor and rate-chain columns included. A rate re-declared
9800 [12.7] through a different anchor at the same tick table is therefore a different
9801 [12.7] deployment, and the replay is refused. The refusal costs one re-recording in
9802 [12.7] a rare case, and it keeps the equality a plain value equality rather than one
9803 [12.7] that ignores two of a row's columns. Mismatch is `CheckpointMismatch` with a
9804 [12.7] deployment-parameter discriminator, never a what-if ([D-127][d-127]). A
9805 [12.7] deployment change moves the times at which the frame-ordinal batches apply.
9806 [12.7] That is different inputs, not a modified model. The event trio (the
9807 [12.7] localization pair and `firing_budget`) is compared for exactly the same reason
9808 [12.7] the grid parameters are. It moves the trajectory, so a run that differs in it
9809 [12.7] is not re-driving the recorded one. Comparison by value is what makes the
9810 [12.7] check one expression rather than a field-by-field walk.
9811 [12.7] 
9812 [12.7] Under the default form the clock is *restored*, not compared. That form restores
9813 [12.7] `t₀` with the rest of the clock, so `replay!` takes no `t0` argument ([D-127][d-127]).
9814 [12.7] Under `restore = false` the simulation's own clock runs, and its origin must
9815 [12.7] equal the recording's. The checkpoint holds no policy ([D-255][d-255]). `t_end` and
9816 [12.7] `ignore_stop_requests` are keywords of this `replay!` call, validated per call
9817 [12.7] as at `run!`.
9818 [12.7] 
9819 [12.7] The dispositions, by trace content:
9820 [12.7] 
9821 [12.7] | trace content | disposition |
9822 [12.7] |---|---|
9823 [12.7] | the checkpoint's fingerprint: store layout, root-input faces, and what a copy by position relies on | compared against the `Build` |
9824 [12.7] | the recorded `Deployment`, carrying `Δt_base`, `h`, `N_base`, the algorithm, `localization_tol`, `localization_budget`, `firing_budget` | compared as a value against the deployment the target simulation's `Model` carries |
9825 [12.7] | each writer's face-name → position schema (on the trace's `schemas` list) | validated against the target model's root-input faces: disagreement is a replay error |
9826 [12.7] | the checkpoint's state | restored under the default form; no boundary zero |
9827 [12.7] | the clock | restored under the default form, `replay!` takes no `t0`; under `restore = false` the simulation's own clock runs, its origin equal to the recording's |
9828 [12.7] | `t_end`, `ignore_stop_requests` | absent from the checkpoint; each `replay!` call declares its own ([§13.5][s13-5]) |
9829 [12.7] 
9830 [12.7] #### What-if replay and `restore = false`
9831 [12.7] 
9832 [12.7] Replaying against the same structure with changed parameters is the what-if
9833 [12.7] replay. It is spelled as `init!` of the modified model under the authored
9834 [12.7] condition, then `replay!(sim, trc; restore = false)`. The trace is not
9835 [12.7] self-sufficient for it. Its checkpoint holds the recording model's state after
9836 [12.7] boundary zero, so the authored condition comes from outside ([D-274][d-274]).
9837 [12.7] Bit-identity is promised only against the identical build ([§2.4][s2-4]). What-if replay
9838 [12.7] promises determinism, never reproduction.
9839 [12.7] 
9840 [12.7] `replay!(sim, trc; restore = false)` attaches the feed to the simulation as it
9841 [12.7] stands and restores nothing. The simulation must be `initialized`, at the
9842 [12.7] recording's `t₀`, and at a frame from the header's to one short of the
9843 [12.7] recording's last. A simulation that is not `initialized` meets the lifecycle
9844 [12.7] gate before any write, with `MissingInit` when it is `built` and
9845 [12.7] `ServiceLifecycle` in any other state. One whose origin or frame is off the
9846 [12.7] recording's is refused before any write as `CheckpointMismatch`, its clock arm.
9847 [12.7] One grid is thus an invariant of every replay, and `to_time` has one meaning.
9848 [12.7] The drain applies the records from the frame after the simulation's own.
9849 [12.7] 
9850 [12.7] This form is a door like the default one, an entry point that builds a run
9851 [12.7] ([§12.6][s12-6]). It builds a fresh run under the recording keywords, takes
9852 [12.7] the simulation's own checkpoint as the new trace's header, and publishes one
9853 [12.7] snapshot. That form serves the what-if of a modified model initialized under
9854 [12.7] the authored condition (above). It also serves a seek, `restore!` of a
9855 [12.7] checkpoint the recording passed through followed by the feed from the next
9856 [12.7] frame ([D-274][d-274]).
9857 [12.7] 
9858 [12.7] #### Rejected shapes
9859 [12.7] 
9860 [12.7] Three shapes are rejected for the record ([D-101][d-101]). They are a
9861 [12.7] `run!(sim; replay = trc)` flag, a synthetic playback device staging the
9862 [12.7] recorded batches, and replay ending `stopped`. The input mode clarifies that
9863 [12.7] decision's drain substitution rather than replacing it, and carries its own
9864 [12.7] rejected shapes ([D-218][d-218]).

9868 [12.7] # Part IV — Failure and services
9869 [12.7] 
9870 [12.7] Part IV covers what happens when things go wrong, and what a stopped simulation
9871 [12.7] can be asked to do. [§13][s13] is the error discipline. It fixes which failures
