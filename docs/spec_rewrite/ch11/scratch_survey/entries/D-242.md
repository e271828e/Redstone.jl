9184	### D-242 — Name the resolution cases wiring, service and inspection, and retire "register" and "load-bearing" as spec vocabulary
9185	
9186	**Status.** ratified
9187	
9188	**Position.** [§13.3][s13-3]'s three resolution cases are named by their client, and
9189	two words leave the design documents' vocabulary, the log included. Every
9190	position stands as ratified; only the words for unchanged concepts move, under
9191	rule 2's word-substitution exception and rule 7's audit. This overturns
9192	[D-122][d-122]'s reservation of "register" for the mode sense.
9193	- *Wiring* replaces [D-130][d-130]'s *structural*: connection declarations, resolved
9194	  in Stratum A under [§6.1][s6-1]'s one-level rule.
9195	- *Service* replaces *load-bearing*: condition entries, trim `reads` and
9196	  taps, checked at the authoring or mount level. "Service" keeps its [§14][s14]
9197	  meaning; the paths are the ones a stopped-sim service compiles.
9198	- *Inspection* replaces *diagnostic*: device read bindings, GUI panels,
9199	  snapshot and log inspection, resolved by the instance walk. It is [D-083][d-083]'s
9200	  own word for that side, so the two vocabularies for one line collapse into
9201	  one. The paired side is *integration reads*.
9202	- "Register" in the sense "a mode or idiom in which something is done" (the
9203	  retired [§D.10][sD-10] entry) is gone. Its compounds are renamed by family: the
9204	  *harness writer* and *what-if replay* (glossary anchors renamed), the run
9205	  *modes* (unattended, test-harness, REPL), usage *idioms* (`step!`,
9206	  test-rig, parallel sweep), the *didactic style* of errors, the
9207	  *by-allocation convention*, the sample time's two *forms*, the naming
9208	  convention's four *classes*, and [§14.4][s14-4]'s *two ways of applying one plan*
9209	  named as the specialized `apply!` and the dynamic walk. The word survives
9210	  only in its storage sense: a slot holding a value.
9211	- "Load-bearing" is dropped outright, its plain-intensifier uses replaced by
9212	  ordinary words.
9213	
9214	**Spec.** [§8.1][s8-1], [§8.2][s8-2], [§10.5][s10-5], [§11.2][s11-2], [§11.3][s11-3], [§11.5][s11-5], [§12.6][s12-6], [§12.7][s12-7], [§13.2][s13-2], [§13.3][s13-3], [§13.5][s13-5],
9215	[§14.4][s14-4], [§14.8][s14-8], [§3.4][s3-4], [§8.6][s8-6], [Appendix A][sA], [§D.10][sD-10]
9216	
9217	**Rationale.** Four terms failed the cold reader at once. "Register" asked
9218	for a linguistics sense no technical reader reaches for; a heading such as
9219	"the duty is register-scoped" was decodable only from the table beneath it,
9220	and the same word also named storage slots elsewhere in the spec, so a reader
9221	could not tell which sense applied. "Load-bearing" named the right case at a
9222	distance but three quarters of its occurrences were plain intensifier, so the
9223	term of art had no room to be one; it also named the stakes axis where the
9224	spec's own argument turns on where a violation's cost lands. "Diagnostic"
9225	already meant warnings and errors throughout, with an appendix named for
9226	them, and sat a few characters from "didactic". "Structural" was
9227	serviceable but said nothing about wiring. Naming each case by its client
9228	lets the table's row name carry who resolves and, through the chapter the
9229	client belongs to, what is at stake.
9230	
9231	**Rejected.**
9232	- *"Task", "analysis" or "operation" in place of "service":* "task" collides
9233	  with Julia's `Task`; "analysis" excludes initialization and `capture`;
9234	  "operation" is accurate but buys little over the compounded "stopped-sim
9235	  service" and costs a chapter-wide rename.
9236	- *Naming the middle case by its stakes ("portable" or "relocatable"
9237	  paths):* self-explaining, but the other two rows name clients, and a mixed
9238	  scheme is harder to hold.
9239	- *Keeping "register" in the mode sense with an inline gloss at each first
9240	  use:* the gloss fights the reader's prior at every occurrence instead of
9241	  once.
9242	- *Leaving the log in its old vocabulary:* rule 2's first reading; rejected
9243	  because the substitution moves no position and the old words would mislead
9244	  every later reader of the log, so the exception was written into rule 2.
