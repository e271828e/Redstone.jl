7327	### D-199 — The `reads` enumeration returns a labeled NamedTuple of selectors
7328	
7329	**Status.** ratified
7330	
7331	**Position.** `reads(b)` returns a NamedTuple of [§14.4][s14-4] selectors,
7332	`(; label = get_output(…), …)`: the labels are the binding's own naming, they
7333	ride through the compiled gather in declaration order, and they key the
7334	NamedTuple `map_output` receives ([§11.2][s11-2], [§11.6][s11-6]).
7335	
7336	**Spec.** [§11.2][s11-2], [§11.6][s11-6]
7337	
7338	**Rationale.** [§11.6][s11-6] fixed what `map_output` receives — a labeled NamedTuple —
7339	without fixing where the labels come from, and the gather cannot be compiled
7340	without that answer. Making the enumeration itself the labeled value closes
7341	the gap with what the interface already has: one returned value carries
7342	names, order and reads together, the enumeration stays self-describing under
7343	the same attach-time validation as `claims`, and the binding author names
7344	their own wire vocabulary instead of inheriting path-derived names that would
7345	change under substitution.
7346	
7347	**Rejected.**
7348	- *Selectors carrying their own labels (a label field per selector):* the
7349	  label is the binding's naming of its wire datum, not a property of the
7350	  read; attaching it to the selector puts two facts in one value and makes
7351	  the same selector unshareable across bindings.
7352	- *A parallel label list beside a selector tuple:* two sequences pairing by
7353	  position is exactly the drift a single labeled value refuses — names and
7354	  reads can disagree in length or order with no check to catch it.
