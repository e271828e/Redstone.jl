13280	### D-314 — Rename `inner_wires` to `local_wires`
13281	
13282	**Status.** ratified
13283	
13284	**Position.** The class marker is `local_wires`, and the child-to-child kind
13285	of wire is a "local wire" in prose. `input_wires` and `output_wires` stand.
13286	Signatures, entry spellings and semantics are unchanged, and `Group`'s field
13287	and keyword follow the declaration. No aliases and no deprecation shims, on
13288	[D-220][d-220]'s terms.
13289	
13290	**Spec.** [§3.3][s3-3], [§6.1][s6-1], [§8.2][s8-2], [§8.5][s8-5], [§8.6][s8-6], [§8.8][s8-8], [§11.7][s11-7], [§14.2][s14-2], [Appendix B][sB],
13291	[Appendix C][sC], [Appendix D][sD]
13292	
13293	**Rationale.** [D-303][d-303] accepted the `inner_`/`input_` near-collision as a
13294	reading nuisance. The nuisance is in the common case: every assembly writes
13295	the two declarations on adjacent lines, and `inner` and `input` share their
13296	first two letters and their length, so the eye tells them apart by the
13297	third letter alone. `local` keeps the length, so the trio still aligns, and
13298	shares no letter run with `input`.
13299	
13300	`local` also names the distinction more exactly than `inner`. A local wire
13301	has both ends inside the assembly's boundary, and an input or output wire
13302	has one end on it. "Local" says "confined within, crossing no boundary",
13303	which is the contrast; "inner" says it only by opposition to an "outer"
13304	that nothing is called. [D-303][d-303] rejected `wires_local` because `local` names
13305	a scope beside two directions, but the three words sort the wires by one
13306	question, whether the wire crosses the boundary and in which direction, and
13307	a scope word is the right answer to its first half.
13308	
13309	`local` is a Julia keyword, and `local_wires` is an ordinary identifier the
13310	parser never confuses with it. `implementation.md`'s naming rules use
13311	"a local" for a local variable, and "local wire" is always two words, so the
13312	two senses never meet in one phrase.
13313	
13314	The rename does not change what kind of thing the three declarations hold.
13315	[D-303][d-303]'s rule that "wire" covers an entry of any of the three stands, and
13316	the [§8.6][s8-6] rule keeps its three verbs. That a boundary entry aliases a face
13317	rather than creating an endpoint is a mechanism the spec states where
13318	faces are defined, not a difference the names expose.
13319	
13320	**Rejected.**
13321	- *Keeping `inner_wires`:* the near-collision sits in every assembly's most
13322	  common two lines, and the build-time failure [D-303][d-303] relied on catches a
13323	  misfiled entry, not a misread one.
13324	- *`wires` alone:* the strongest contrast, and the inner set is the
13325	  assembly's wires proper. But `wires` is a local in `build.jl`'s cluster
13326	  code, which the naming rules forbid beside a package function of that
13327	  name, and the bare noun contradicts [D-303][d-303]'s rule that an entry of any of
13328	  the three is a wire.
13329	- *`peer_wires`, `child_wires`:* both precise about joining siblings.
13330	  "Peer" has no footing in the spec, and "child wires" reads as the
13331	  children's own wires.
13332	- *`internal_wires`, `intra_wires`:* keep the `in` prefix, so they keep the
13333	  collision.
13334	- *`input_aliases`/`output_aliases` beside the rename:* exposes the
13335	  virtual nature of the boundary entries in the API, at the price of the
13336	  uniform "wire" story, [D-303][d-303]'s rule, the glossary and every diagnostic
13337	  that pairs a local wire with an `input_wires` chain. One rename suffices
13338	  for readability, and the aliasing fact already has a home in the prose.
