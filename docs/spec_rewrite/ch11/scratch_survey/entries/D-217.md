8065	### D-217 — Conform the trace and replay sections to the prototype's record
8066	
8067	**Status.** ratified
8068	
8069	**Position.** The first increment to record a trace and replay it is [§11.5][s11-5]
8070	and [§12.7][s12-7]'s first test, and the sections move where the mechanism showed them
8071	short.
8072	
8073	- The trace carries its length beside the header and the batches: the number
8074	  of drains since the capture. A recording whose last frames drained nothing
8075	  still ran them, and replay's frame budget is that count.
8076	- The header's schema list only grows. Every capture and every roster change
8077	  appends the current writer set, earlier records keep their index, and a
8078	  record resolves only through its own schema entry.
8079	- `ReplayUnknownFace` carries the bare position where the writer's schema has
8080	  no name for it.
8081	- The entry pass validates each batch's frame ordinal against the recording's
8082	  length, as a `ReplayHeaderMismatch` discriminator, and the replay drain keys
8083	  records to the frame exactly.
8084	- `ReplayHeaderMismatch` and `ReplaySchemaMismatch` are collected.
8085	- The header records the `t_end`/`stop_on` pair bound at construction. A run's
8086	  effective bound is reported by its termination record when it fires.
8087	
8088	**Spec.** [§11.5][s11-5], [§12.7][s12-7], [§13.5][s13-5], [Appendix B][sB], [Appendix C][sC]
8089	
8090	**Rationale.** Positional records are meaningless without a schema, and the
8091	header outlives a run: `attach!` and `detach!` are legal at every stopped
8092	point between `init!` and the next advance ([§12.6][s12-6]), and a roster change
8093	recompiles the harness writer against a new complement, so the same face
8094	moves position. A schema list that only grows keeps every recorded index
8095	valid without rewriting a record. The same positional shape is why an
8096	unresolvable entry has no name to report. A frame ordinal the entry pass does
8097	not check lets a hand-edited record apply at a frame not its own, silently,
8098	and re-record as if it were; [§12.7][s12-7] promises exact ordinal keying, so the check
8099	belongs with the other up-front ones. The two header kinds collect for [D-216][d-216]'s
8100	reason: a check that runs over a list is collecting by its shape. The
8101	termination pair is the one place the sections contradicted each other. [§11.5][s11-5]
8102	captured "the effective pair" at `init!`, while [§13.5][s13-5] binds a `run!` override
8103	for that run only, after `init!` and possibly several times per trace. The
8104	header can only hold what `init!` knows, and the termination record already
8105	names what fired: `EndTimeReached` carries the final `t`, `ModelRequestedStop`
8106	the holding face. Replay neither compares nor applies the pair, so nothing
8107	rides on it.
8108	
8109	**Rejected.**
8110	- *A mutable termination slot in the header, rewritten at every advance:* one
8111	  slot holds one run's pair, and the header stops being a single-instant
8112	  capture, which [§11.5][s11-5] relies on.
8113	- *Moving the pair out of the header into the termination record:* the next
8114	  run overwrites the record, so the pair would leave the one persistent
8115	  artifact.
8116	- *Rewriting recorded positions at a roster change:* the values are recorded
8117	  and the positions are the index into a schema; a rewrite is a second record
8118	  format.
8119	- *A feed cursor that applies any record at or before the current frame:* it
8120	  masks a stale ordinal by applying it late, and under a `to_boundary`
8121	  truncation it would sweep every record past the cut into the last replayed
8122	  frame.
