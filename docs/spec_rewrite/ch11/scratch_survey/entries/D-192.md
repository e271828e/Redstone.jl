7035	### D-192 — Let the greedy claim empty the harness remainder
7036	
7037	**Status.** ratified
7038	
7039	**Position.** A rostered greedy claimant empties the harness writer's derived
7040	surface: the greedy claim wins and the harness surface genuinely goes empty, so
7041	every `stage!` in such a session is rejected at staging with `ClaimedFaceEntry`
7042	naming the incumbent — already-specified behavior, not a new diagnostic.
7043	
7044	**Spec.** [§11.3][s11-3]
7045	
7046	**Rationale.** The precedence is an *implication* of the claimed-versus-derived
7047	distinction [§11.3][s11-3] already draws, not a new rule: the greedy claim is a **rostered
7048	claim** whose computed source is exhausted at the attach point, while the
7049	harness writer's surface is the **derived** complement of the rostered claims
7050	— the faces no rostered device speaks for. A rostered greedy claimant therefore
7051	leaves that complement empty by construction. The harness writer exists for
7052	script and test sessions without an interactive claimant, and a `stage!`
7053	rejected in a GUI session with `ClaimedFaceEntry` is informative, not broken;
7054	[§11.6][s11-6]'s `EmptyGreedyClaim` covers greedy-vs-greedy only and wants no companion
7055	here. Escape hatch on the record: a user who wants a non-empty harness surface
7056	alongside the GUI attaches the GUI under a non-greedy (returned) binding — the
7057	design already allows it, the same GUI device type being equally attachable
7058	under a binding that returns explicit claims ([§11.6][s11-6]). Greedy-last attach is the
7059	idiom, computed claims staying exhausted at attach: attachment order is
7060	decisive by design.
7061	
7062	**Rejected.**
7063	- *Harness-first subtraction — subtract the harness remainder before computing
7064	  the greedy complement:* it would make the derived surface behave like a claim,
7065	  and it breaks greedy's purpose — everything unclaimed, without configuration.
7066	- *Recomputing computed claims at roster changes:* contradicts
7067	  exhaustion-at-attach — past the attach point nothing downstream can tell a
7068	  computed claim from a returned one — and the frozen-surface doctrine.
