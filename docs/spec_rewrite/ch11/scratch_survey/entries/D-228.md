8603	### D-228 — Attribute runtime diagnostics by cell, never by payload
8604	
8605	**Status.** ratified
8606	
8607	**Position.** A rate-limited diagnostic kind names its subjects in the payload
8608	and never its writer. The writer is the identity of the cell it was written
8609	to, and the published status record's `who` and the tail residue carry it.
8610	
8611	**Spec.** [§11.8][s11-8], [Appendix C][sC]
8612	
8613	**Rationale.** [§11.8][s11-8] gives every writer its own cell: one per rostered
8614	device, one for the harness writer, one for the loop. A device reports
8615	through its handle, the handle owns the cell, and no call anywhere passes a
8616	device id. At the drain each cell becomes one record of the published
8617	framework status, keyed by the writer's name, with the ring, the suppressed
8618	counts and the cumulative per-writer × per-kind totals beside it; the run's-end
8619	residue is built the same way. The writer is therefore a structural fact of
8620	the channel, and a payload field would restate what the value's container
8621	already says, at every emission site, with room for the two to disagree.
8622	[Appendix C][sC]'s rows had named a device or writer id in five payloads, and the
8623	2026-09-04 audit read them as missing fields. The rows now name subjects
8624	only. `DeviceJoinTimeout` keeps its device id: it names the abandoned device,
8625	and the loop writes it. The one cost is a value shown apart from its record,
8626	which is a renderer's concern: a renderer presents each value under its
8627	record's writer.
8628	
8629	**Rejected.**
8630	- *A `writer` field on the five kinds:* the wrapper, the staging shim, the
8631	  drain and the author's own loop body would each spell a name the channel
8632	  already guarantees, and a wrong name would contradict the cell.
