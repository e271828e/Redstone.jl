11479	### D-274 — Checkpoints: the executor's state as one value, restored without boundary zero
11480	
11481	**Status.** ratified
11482	
11483	**Position.** A `Checkpoint{T}` is the executor's state at a frame top,
11484	taken by `checkpoint(sim)` and by `init!`, and put back by `restore!`. It
11485	is the trace's header, and replay is a restore followed by the feed.
11486	
11487	- A checkpoint holds the flat buffer `x`, the `s` and `m` stores, the whole
11488	  signal table, the guard priors, the one event register that crosses a
11489	  boundary, the clock in full (`t`, the frame index, the boundary ordinal,
11490	  `t₀`), and the fingerprint, the deployment and the structural layout the
11491	  trace header carries today. The derivative buffer, the arrival pair, the
11492	  localization samples, which every frame rewrites before reading, the
11493	  cursor and the periphery stay out. The stepper seam gets a checkpoint
11494	  hook, empty for a single-step method.
11495	- `checkpoint(sim)` is a stopped-sim service ([§13.7][s13-7]): legal in
11496	  `initialized` and `stopped`, refused while running, and refused when the
11497	  executor sits mid-frame after a `t*` stop, the clock being past the frame
11498	  top with the remainder abandoned.
11499	- `restore!(sim, cp)` is a door beside `init!` and `replay!`: it checks the
11500	  fingerprint as replay checks the header, copies the state back, opens a
11501	  fresh run, publishes one snapshot, and runs no boundary zero. No sweep, no
11502	  guard evaluation, no update, no prior reset. The next frame is the
11503	  original lattice's next frame, since the clock came with the checkpoint;
11504	  the boundary ordinal continues, being the trajectory's ([§12.3][s12-3], [D-230][d-230]).
11505	- The trace header is the checkpoint `init!` takes at the end of boundary
11506	  zero, after the first publication. `TraceHeader` retires into it; the
11507	  header's resolved stores and root inputs are values still ([D-038][d-038]), the
11508	  checkpoint's. Replay restores it and feeds the batches from frame 1, so
11509	  [D-101][d-101]'s two substitutions become one, and `init!` is the only door that
11510	  runs boundary zero.
11511	- The parametric what-if of [§12.7][s12-7] is spelled as `init!` of the modified
11512	  model under the authored condition, followed by the trace's batches with
11513	  its header set aside. The trace is no longer self-sufficient for it: the
11514	  authored condition comes from outside. The edited-inputs what-if of
11515	  [§11.5][s11-5] is unchanged.
11516	- `linearize`'s default operating point is the checkpoint of the
11517	  simulation as it stands ([D-272][d-272]), restored into the scratch world; the
11518	  frozen tier's cells are the checkpoint's held cells, which is what [D-213][d-213]'s
11519	  establishment round approximated. `about = <condition>` stays for an
11520	  authored point.
11521	- A restore into a simulation of another activation is refused by
11522	  dispatch, as a reader or a trace is.
11523	- A throw inside boundary zero leaves no trace and no checkpoint, the
11524	  simulation returning to `built` ([D-223][d-223]). The run `init!` opened
11525	  stays behind, empty. Its reproduction is `init!` under the same
11526	  condition. Frame one shares pointer 0, and the `StepError`'s `host` tells
11527	  the two apart. The general recipe, restore to the pointer then `step!`,
11528	  holds from pointer 0 for a frame's failure. (Amended 2026-09-30: the
11529	  bullet started the general recipe at pointer 1, from the time one
11530	  rendered text served both failures at pointer 0. The halt at pointer 0
11531	  restores the header and runs no boundary zero, so frame one's record is
11532	  still ahead of it and `step!` re-executes that frame.)
11533	
11534	**Spec.** [§10.2][s10-2], [§10.6][s10-6], [§11.5][s11-5], [§12.6][s12-6], [§12.7][s12-7], [§13.7][s13-7], [§14.8][s14-8], [§14.10][s14-10], [Appendix B][sB]
11535	
11536	**Rationale.** Once the state past the initial instant is a value of its
11537	own, restoring it is a copy, and nothing can be one tick ahead: the held
11538	cells, the priors and the clock are simply there. The identity of a
11539	resume holds on any model by construction, multi-rate included, and no
11540	component owes anything at a restore. Unifying the trace header with the
11541	checkpoint follows from the shape: the header already held the stores, the
11542	root inputs, the deployment and the layout, five of the checkpoint's
11543	fields, and lacked only what boundary zero would derive. Taking it after
11544	boundary zero instead of before hands replay a state to copy rather than a
11545	condition to establish. The price is the parametric what-if's start: a
11546	post-sequence header carries the recording model's held cells and priors
11547	until each component's next tick and the next boundary, where the
11548	pre-sequence header re-derived them under the modified model. Spelling
11549	that what-if around `init!` under the authored condition is the more honest
11550	form, [§12.7][s12-7] promising determinism and never reproduction, and it costs the
11551	user the condition they authored anyway. [§14.5][s14-5]'s line that boundary zero's
11552	firings are recomputed and never recorded loses its force without harm: the
11553	checkpoint records the post-firing state faithfully and suppresses nothing,
11554	which is all [D-026][d-026]'s rule against masking insurance asks. The checkpoint is
11555	also the on-disk unit the persistence deferral of [§11.5][s11-5] waited for, and a
11556	long run becomes seekable, a checkpoint at boundary `k` plus the batches
11557	from frame `k+1`.
11558	
11559	Annotation (2026-10-09): amended by [D-319][d-319]. The state the first bullet lists
11560	is now a `ModelState`, the clock's `t` and `t₀` among its fields, and the
11561	frame index and the boundary ordinal are the run's counters, which a
11562	`Checkpoint` carries beside it. A `Checkpoint` is nominal by type, so the
11563	refusal by dispatch of a state from another activation sits on the two
11564	`restore!` methods that take a bare state.
11565	
11566	**Rejected.**
11567	- *Two header kinds, the pre-sequence resolved condition for an authored
11568	  opening and the checkpoint for a restored one:* one fingerprint, but a
11569	  union type, two replay dispositions and two openings to keep in step,
11570	  bought only for the parametric what-if's exactness at `t₀`.
11571	- *A checkpoint that can be overridden like a condition:* the danger lives
11572	  in the discrete tier, where a store and its held output move together;
11573	  a narrow override of `x` and the root inputs is safe and is the one
11574	  extension a mid-run trim would need, deferred until wanted.
11575	- *A partial first frame after a `t*` checkpoint:* the loop integrates
11576	  whole frames; refusing the mid-frame checkpoint keeps the lattice, and the
11577	  extension waits for a use.
