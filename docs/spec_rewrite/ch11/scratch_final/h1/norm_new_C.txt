### 11.3 Inbound: root inputs, claims and the frozen roster

The periphery (everything outside the loop that exchanges data with it) writes into a running model through its root inputs (the root component's own input faces) alone. The periphery's writers include devices (any attached participants in the periphery). Each root input has at most one writer at any time, and the assignment of root inputs to writers is fixed for a whole run, because the roster (the list of attached device entries) is frozen per run. The section's parts, in order, cover root inputs, exclusivity and claims, write surfaces and claim sources, the harness writer and the layout, root-input initial values, the frozen roster, device identity and admission, device death, and mid-run readers.

#### Root inputs

The write surface is the set of faces the entries of a writer's batch (its staged set of writes, applied whole at the next drain) may reach. Its members are root inputs. A root input is the root component's own input face, an assembly's `input_wires` key or a primitive's `u_types` key (§8.2, §8.6, D-208). A component is the unit of modeling, and a face is the name a port wears on its component's boundary. A root input is routed inward to consumers and produced by no component. At every non-root level an input face is fed by the parent's wire, and at the root there is no parent. No dedicated vocabulary is needed.

A root input is usefully read as the output face of the one producer the build never sees, the periphery and the services. On that reading, root-input exclusivity (below) is that producer's one-writer right, and the totality (§14.6) is its completeness obligation.

Root inputs are sources to the build-time ordering, and they are constants within a frame (one iteration of the loop). They are the only thing the periphery may write (D-031). The GUI reaches them through port resolution (§11.7), and control commands are not writes (§12.1). Devices, mappings, the trace (the primary record of a session) and the GUI write path address root inputs by face name (§8.6). Structural slash paths never reach the periphery's writes. The write side speaks the names of the root's contract, the interface the root component declares, and no others (D-129). The read side chooses per binding (the value passed at `attach!` that makes a device framework-legible). It uses slash paths in inspection reads, and face names in integration reads and in service reads (§11.2/§13.5/§14.4).

#### Exclusivity and claims

Root-input exclusivity permits at most one writer per root input at any time (D-044). A device claims its root inputs (stakes them as faces it may write) at attach, and claiming an already-claimed root input is an attach-time error. Detaching releases the claims. A released root input's GUI widgets are live again from the next run (§11.7). Exclusivity replaces any cross-device conflict policy (D-044), such as attachment-order precedence at the drain (the frame-top swap that applies staged device writes to the root inputs). Per-device staging cells (where each device's pending batch waits between drains), the compare-and-swap (CAS) merge and the atomicswap drain all stay. They serve atomicity and coalescing (keeping one pending batch per device), not arbitration.

Exclusivity holds because a second live writer on one root input makes the applied value a timing artifact, as the worked example in §11.4 shows. The usual pair is a stream and its mirror, a joystick axis and the GUI slider showing it. Claiming the stream settles that pair with no concurrent writing.

A claim is what a device may write, not what it will. Data-dependent write-sets are ordinary. A UDP/JSON peer writes whichever subset of faces the incoming message names, and `map_input` is arbitrary user code the framework never inspects. Such a device therefore claims the binding's enumerated allowed set, the faces the binding table lists, whether or not any given batch touches them. The claim is registered at attach exactly as a joystick's is. A broad claim costs liveness. Every enumerated face is claimed for the device's whole attachment, so the derived-liveness rule (GUI widget liveness, baked once at run start, §11.7) renders the GUI's widgets read-only even on faces the peer never writes. Narrow the binding to narrow the claim. The enumeration is the interface.

#### Write surfaces and claim sources

Every writer has a write surface, and the periphery enforces it. A batch entry reaches a root input iff the named face is inside the writer's surface (D-044). Anything else is discarded with a runtime warning (§13.2). Because surfaces are static per run under the roster freeze (attach and detach only while stopped, below), enforcement runs entirely at staging, the earliest site, on the writer's own task. The drain performs no checks at all.

Every device's surface is its claim set, and a claim set has two sources (D-174):

- A returned claim is enumerated by the binding. The binding declares `is_input(b) = true`, `claims(b)` (§11.6) is called once at attach, and what it names is staked. Such a claim is static for the attachment and exclusively its own, since the attach-time exclusivity check keeps claims disjoint. It is binding-bounded even where no one else is involved. A mapping that has drifted onto an unenumerated face is a diagnosable anomaly (`OutOfClaimEntry`), never a silent write, claimed or not. An enumeration naming a face outside the root's input faces is refused at attach (`AttachUnknownFace`, D-216).

- A computed claim is computed by the framework at attach, when the binding declares `is_greedy(b) = true` (§11.6). It is all root-input faces minus the union of the rostered claims, the unclaimed complement at that instant. This is the standard GUI binding's claim (§11.6), everything unclaimed, without configuration. It is disjoint from every incumbent claim by construction, so exclusivity validates trivially and nothing downstream can tell the two sources apart. A complement that comes out empty is legal and useless. `attach!` records an `EmptyGreedyClaim` warning for it (§11.6).

There is one claim mechanism with two claim sources. The source is exhausted at the attach point. Past it, validation, roster-entry storage, the compilation of shape, shim, merge and scatter (§11.4), the drain, the trace (§11.5) and detach-releases-claims treat a computed claim exactly as a returned one (D-174). The GUI is therefore not an exception but an ordinary enumerated writer whose enumeration the framework performed. Attaching the greedy claimant (the device holding the computed claim) last is the idiom (D-192). Its computed claim is taken at the attach point and never recomputed, so attachment order is decisive by design.

Opportunistic writing by autonomous devices does not exist (D-044). A device that wants a face enumerates it, and greediness is an explicit declaration, never a default. Cross-writer races on one root input therefore cannot arise structurally, because every claim is exclusive, whatever its source. That is what keeps drain order a diagnostic fact (§11.4) and lets a drained GUI value simply stay (§11.7).

#### The harness writer and the layout

One framework-owned remainder sits beside the roster, the harness writer (the framework-owned write path `stage!(sim, …)`). It is an entry point with no task of its own, the harness/REPL write path (§12.6, D-096), called as follows.

`stage!(sim, "face" => value, …)`

It stages a batch from any task, the calling task (the task that invoked `run!`) included (D-200). Its always-present staging cell, the harness cell, is drained, traced and surface-checked exactly as any device's.

The harness writer's surface is the one thing in the design that is derived rather than claimed (D-174). It is the unclaimed complement, the faces no rostered device speaks for. That surface is recomputed at every stopped-sim roster change, and is therefore as fixed within a run as any claim set. A `stage!` write to a claimed face is rejected at staging (`ClaimedFaceEntry`, naming the incumbent, D-174). A rostered greedy claimant empties that surface outright. The greedy claim is itself a rostered claim, so the complement it leaves is empty and every `stage!` in such a session is rejected that way (D-192). There is one edge case, a batch staged while stopped whose face a subsequent `attach!` claims, and the attach itself renormalizes it away (§11.4).

The harness cell drains last, by convention (D-096, D-174). With every surface disjoint the order is unobservable, so the rule exists to make the trace read the same way every time, not to arbitrate anything.

The periphery resolves root faces against the activation's cell layout alone (D-261). An activation is the build's typed products at a scalar type. The layout carries the root faces in structure order with their probe values (the real values the build evaluates each user function with), beside the address table, so both of the build's lookup families, schema and layout, read it there, and writers, claims and the harness compile against it. The services take their schema from the `Build` itself (§14.3). The periphery never needs an authored fact the layout does not hold.

#### Root-input initial values

The init/trim services own root-input initial values (D-047). Input declarations are bare types (§8.2) and carry no defaults, yet a root input unfed by any device must hold a defined value from the first frame. No declaration constant could own that value, because the trim service writes root-input values it solved for, such as throttle and elevator. Export-entry defaults were rejected (D-047). `init!` establishes every root input, and the trace header (the trace's fixed preamble, a checkpoint `init!` takes) captures the result. Totality is enforced pre-write at every complete-world application, namely `init!`, trim setup and trim commit (§14.6, D-149).

#### The frozen roster

The roster is frozen per run (D-106). Attach and detach are stopped-sim operations. `attach!`/`detach!` are legal in the `built`, `initialized` and `stopped` states (§12.6) and an error while `running`. That error is `ServiceLifecycle` (Appendix C, D-108), the same kind that gates the §14 services. The list is exhaustive. An `errored` simulation refuses both with the same kind. A roster change configures the next run, and an errored simulation has none (§13.6, D-232). The prohibition includes pause. Pause is a control-plane state inside a run (§12.1), and a surface that could move while paused would move mid-run.

The roster (roster entries, claims, attachment order) is therefore a plain immutable value. The loop reads it once at `run!`, and nothing republishes it (D-087). The partition of the root face set into per-writer surfaces plus the harness remainder is a static, inspectable fact of the run (D-106). It is printable before the run starts and valid until it ends (printable like the face routes, §13.7). Attachment order is the roster's own order. The trace still tags each record with a stable device id (described below), never a roster index, because ids read across runs, where the roster does change (D-087).

Attach validation, claim registration and the staging-shape compilation (§11.4) all run at the attach point. That makes `attach!`/`detach!` stopped-sim configuration operations beside `init!` and trim (§14). While a simulation runs, its configuration (build, roster, claims, surfaces) is immutable. The mid-run mutation doctrine (§12.5) extends to its final form. The running periphery stages writes and issues control commands, and nothing else changes (D-031).

#### Device identity and admission

Identity is the device instance. The same object (`===`) may occupy at most one roster entry. Two instances of the same type, two joysticks say, are two devices. The stable device id the trace, heartbeat and diagnostics speak is assigned at `attach!`, monotonic per `Simulation` and never reused. It lives exactly as long as its roster entry, across runs (roster persistence, §12.6) and until `detach!`.

Admission is a two-part check at the attach point, in order:

``` # each line: the condition that rejects the attach → the diagnostic it raises identity this instance is already rostered → AlreadyAttached (names the entry and its binding) claims face exclusivity: this device's claim set meets a rostered claim → ClaimConflict (names two distinct devices) ```

An already-rostered instance is rejected rather than silently absorbed, because rebinding has an explicit spelling, `detach!` then `attach!`, both legal at any stopped-sim point. Either a silent no-op or a silent rebind would discard a binding the caller handed over. Running the claims check after the identity check is what makes `ClaimConflict` always name two distinct devices, never a device colliding with its own earlier attachment. Detaching a device the roster does not hold is an error (`NotAttached`, D-215).

#### Device death and orphaned claims

Device death does not detach (D-106). A mid-run crash, voluntary exit or unplug (§11.6, §12.4) ends the device's task. The staging cell stops filling, the heartbeat (§12.2) shows the death by name, and the roster entry, claims included, persists to the end of the run. The orphaned claims (the claims of a device whose task died mid-run) are the accepted cost of the freeze. The device's root inputs hold their last-drained values and no other writer inherits them. The read-only widgets render the orphan visibly, never mysteriously (§11.7). Recovery is between runs. It takes a stop, a `detach!`, and either `init!` (fresh trajectory) or `replay!`-to-end then `run!`, which continues from the interrupted boundary, a published consistency point (§12.7). The death is an anomaly, not a surface event.

#### Mid-run readers

One deliberate asymmetry is on record as a guarded addition (a capability the design admits but does not build). A pure reader claims nothing. Examples are a binding declaring `is_output` alone (§11.6), a visualizer or a telemetry tap. Attaching one mid-run would therefore move no writer's surface (D-106). A dynamic reader list would touch only §12.3 wakeups, the heartbeat and the shutdown join, never the drain. The addition is cleanly severable from the freeze should the join-a-running-session workflow find a customer. The §12.2 thread-budget warning runs once per run, at `run!` or `replay!`, against the frozen population.
