Attach validation, claim registration and the staging-shape compilation (§11.4) all run at the attach 
point. That makes attach!/detach! stopped-sim configuration operations beside init! and trim (§14). 
While a simulation runs, its configuration (build, roster, claims, surfaces) is immutable. The mid-
run mutation doctrine ( §12.5) extends to its final form. The running periphery stages writes and 
issues control commands, and nothing else changes (D-031).
Device identity and admission
Identity is the device instance. The same object ( ===) may occupy at most one roster entry. Two 
instances of the same type, two joysticks say, are two devices. The stable device id the trace, 
heartbeat and diagnostics speak is assigned at attach!, monotonic per Simulation and never reused. 
It lives exactly as long as its roster entry, across runs (roster persistence, §12.6) and until detach!.
Admission is a two-part check at the attach point, in order:
# each line: the condition that rejects the attach → the diagnostic it raises
identity   this instance is already rostered
               → AlreadyAttached      (names the entry and its binding)
claims     face exclusivity: this device's claim set meets a rostered claim
               → ClaimConflict        (names two distinct devices)
An already-rostered instance is rejected rather than silently absorbed, because rebinding has an 
explicit spelling, detach! then attach!, both legal at any stopped-sim point. Either a silent no-op 
or a silent rebind would discard a binding the caller handed over. Running the claims check after 
the identity check is what makes ClaimConflict always name two distinct devices, never a device 
colliding with its own earlier attachment. Detaching a device the roster does not hold is an error 
(NotAttached, D-215).
Device death and orphaned claims
Device death does not detach  (D-106). A mid-run crash, voluntary exit or unplug ( §11.6, §12.4) 
ends the device's task. The staging cell stops filling, the heartbeat (§12.2) shows the death by name, 
and the roster entry, claims included, persists to the end of the run. The orphaned claims (the claims 
of a device whose task died mid-run) are the accepted cost of the freeze. The device's root inputs 
hold their last-drained values and no other writer inherits them. The read-only widgets render the 
orphan visibly, never mysteriously (§11.7). Recovery is between runs. It takes a stop, a detach!, and 
either init! (fresh trajectory) or replay!-to-end then run!, which continues from the interrupted 
boundary (§12.7), a published consistency point. The death is an anomaly, not a surface event.
