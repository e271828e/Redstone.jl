• If the port is root-driven within the GUI's claim (the set of faces a device may write), its widget 
is live. It peeks (the GUI display rule, specified below) and stages the resolved root input through 
the GUI's own staging cell (the per-device atomic holding place for pending writes).
• If the port is component-driven, or root-driven under the claim of another device (any attached 
participant in the periphery), its widget is a read-only rendering. It displays the driven value from 
the snapshot (the immutable per-boundary publication), visually distinct, with its source ("driven 
by avionics/throttle_cmd", the canonical slash form of §8.6).
This retires FlightCore's dead-slider convention and replaces it with checked structure. A widget is 
live exactly when the underlying input is yours to command in this configuration (D-045). 
The dead slider is the throttle of Cessna172Xv1, the first variant of FlightApps' Cessna 172X model. 
The engine panel's slider is visually live and the avionics silently overwrite it every cycle, so who 
commands what lives in the user's head. User-commandability is a wiring decision made where 
configurations are made. Command-plus-manual-override is a mux component with a root-wired 
select. That is explicit structure, not two writers racing (the same race as the drag phase in the 
worked example of §11.4, ruled out the same way). This places one obligation on the GUI. Read-
only rendering is first-class, not an error state, because the author of input_slider! (an illustrative 
widget function) cannot know at authoring time whether it will be live (D-026).
Liveness is a derived property (the rule computing a widget's liveness from wiring and claims), and 
resolution is transitive (D-045). A widget is live iff two things hold. Its port's feed chain terminates 
in a root input, and that root input lies inside the GUI's own claim ( D-174) in the run's frozen 
surface partition (root-input exclusivity, §11.3). The feed chain is walked through local wires (wires 
with both ends inside one assembly) and interface connections across all levels, not just the local 
assembly. The claim may have been computed from the unclaimed complement under is_greedy. It 
may instead have been enumerated by a partial-claims binding (the value passed at attach!, §11.6). 
Such a binding enumerates the claim one face (the name a port wears on its component's boundary) 
at a time. Either way, "live" reads as "inside the surface I declared for".
The roster (the list of attached devices, with their claims) is frozen for a run, since attach! and 
detach! are stopped-sim operations (§11.3). Under that freeze, liveness is a static fact of the run. It 
is baked once, with the port resolution, when the run starts, and never consulted against mutable 
claim state at render (D-270).
There is no per-port "GUI-controlled" marking anywhere. The export chain is the marking, written 
by the one author entitled to write it. A component's ports become GUI-commandable exactly when 
the assemblies above surface them. The switch between "driven by its own panel" and "driven by 
an external provider" is therefore automatic. World is the example assembly of pure composition 
that §8.8 introduces. At build time the switch follows the wiring archetype, where a scripted World 
wires a scenario component into the same faces the interactive World exports to root. A scenario 
component is the home of a sim-time script. At run start the switch follows roster claim state. 
Nominally-connected ports with a GUI override channel are rejected (D-045). The honest cost stands. 
Unexported ports are unpokeable (D-045). FlightCore's poke-any-u workflow deliberately does 
not survive the visibility rule of the contract (the declared interface of a component, §8.3).
