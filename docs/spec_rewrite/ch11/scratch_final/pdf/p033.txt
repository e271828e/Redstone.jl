Write surfaces and claim sources
Every writer has a write surface, and the periphery enforces it. A batch entry reaches a root 
input iff the named face is inside the writer's surface (D-044). Anything else is discarded with 
a runtime warning ( §13.2). Because surfaces are static per run under the roster freeze (attach and 
detach only while stopped, below), enforcement runs entirely at staging, the earliest site, on the 
writer's own task. The drain performs no checks at all.
Every device's surface is its claim set, and a claim set has two sources (D-174):
• A returned claim is enumerated by the binding. The binding declares is_input(b) = true, claims(b) 
(§11.6) is called once at attach, and what it names is staked. Such a claim is static for the attachment 
and exclusively its own, since the attach-time exclusivity check keeps claims disjoint. It is binding-
bounded even where no one else is involved. A mapping that has drifted onto an unenumerated 
face is a diagnosable anomaly (OutOfClaimEntry), never a silent write, claimed or not. An enumer-
ation naming a face outside the root's input faces is refused at attach (AttachUnknownFace, D-216).
• A computed claim is computed by the framework at attach, when the binding declares is_greedy(b) 
= true (§11.6). It is all root-input faces minus the union of the rostered claims, the unclaimed com-
plement at that instant. This is the standard GUI binding's claim ( §11.6), everything unclaimed, 
without configuration. It is disjoint from every incumbent claim by construction, so exclusivity 
validates trivially and nothing downstream can tell the two sources apart. A complement that 
comes out empty is legal and useless. attach! records an EmptyGreedyClaim warning for it (§11.6).
There is one claim mechanism with two claim sources. The source is exhausted at the attach 
point. Past it, validation, roster-entry storage, the compilation of shape, shim, merge and scatter 
(§11.4), the drain, the trace ( §11.5) and detach-releases-claims treat a computed claim exactly as a 
returned one ( D-174). The GUI is therefore not an exception but an ordinary enumerated writer 
whose enumeration the framework performed. Attaching the greedy claimant (the device holding 
the computed claim) last is the idiom ( D-192). Its computed claim is taken at the attach point and 
never recomputed, so attachment order is decisive by design.
Opportunistic writing by autonomous devices does not exist ( D-044). A device that wants a face 
enumerates it, and greediness is an explicit declaration, never a default. Cross-writer races on one 
root input therefore cannot arise structurally, because every claim is exclusive, whatever its source. 
That is what keeps drain order a diagnostic fact ( §11.4) and lets a drained GUI value simply stay 
(§11.7).
