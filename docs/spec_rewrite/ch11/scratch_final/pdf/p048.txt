Every violation in that list reports BindingContractMismatch (Appendix C). The report names the 
binding type, the trait, the method at fault and the direction (declared-but-missing, or defined-but-
undeclared). gather against a binding with no output side reports DeviceContractMismatch, the device

Declared sides, checked both ways, close the shadowing hole. Under the rejected alternative, 
detection by method presence ( D-177), a bidirectional binding whose claims was written without 
extending the framework's generic presented as output-only and degraded silently. That omission 
is the using-without-import trap (§8.1), one level down. With the side declared, the absent method 
has something to contradict. An abstract binding-type taxonomy encoding the sides, optional roots 
left unenforced, and a declared sides(b) trait returning the side set are rejected (D-177).
Greedy claims
is_greedy is a claim source, not a device class (D-174). A binding declares that source in exactly one 
line, in the open, and past the attach point nothing tells a greedy claim from a returned one (§11.3). 
The standing rejection of opportunistic writing to unclaimed faces "for any device" is untouched 
(D-044).
Greediness stays orthogonal to reads. A greedy front end may also drive a compiled output gather. 
That combination is legal and currently uninstantiated. Its plausible customer is a narrow-wire 
interactive surface, a motorized control board whose detents must be driven back out. The binding 
stays an attach! argument, never a device field. The same T16000M binds differently per aircraft, and 
narrowing the binding narrows the claim (§11.3).
A second greedy attach stakes the empty remainder  (D-174). The complement is computed 
against the roster as it stands. A greedy binding attached after another has already swallowed 
everything therefore gets the empty claim. That claim is legal, being the honest may-write-nothing 
degenerate below, and it is useless, which is worth saying out loud. The attach succeeds and records 
EmptyGreedyClaim (Appendix C, a warning naming the device and its binding) in the roster entry's 
diagnostic cell. attach! mutates the roster, so the warning lives in that state's status and surfaces 
through the status record (§11.8, D-250). The log line at return is presentation. That is the one honest 
reading of "you asked for what is left and nothing was left".
Several interactive front ends may be rostered at once  (D-174). A web console can claim the 
autopilot faces beside a local GUI claiming the stick faces. With explicit claims they are simply two 
enumerated devices, partitioning the surface rather than sharing it.
The standard GUI binding is a greedy one. It declares is_input and is_greedy, stakes the computed 
claim (everything unclaimed at the moment it attaches), and defines no claims of its own. It declares 
no reads either, because its read path is the handle's primitive read. The compiled output gather 
therefore has nothing to do for it. The same GUI device type is equally attachable under a binding 
that returns explicit claims. Greediness is the binding's declaration, not the device's nature. Every
