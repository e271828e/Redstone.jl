## 6. Composition: connections, aggregation and hierarchy [Components](#g-component) (the units of modeling, leaf or assembly) become a system through wiring. Connections route signals across the hierarchy of [assemblies](#g-assembly) (components of pure composition, with no dynamics of their own). Ordinary junction components sit wherever several signals must combine into one. [§6.1][s6-1] gives the connection and hierarchy rules. [§6.2][s6-2] gives the aggregation idiom they force. ### 6.1 Connections and hierarchy A wire names its two endpoints by path, and those paths run down the hierarchy of children. How far a path may reach decides whether a parent addresses a grandchild's [port](#g-port) (one declared input or output) directly or every intermediate level must re-export it. That question comes first here. Next comes what type-checks a wire once both endpoints resolve. The last part, on connections per port, covers how many connections each side may take and what becomes of the ports left unconnected. #### How far a path may reach An [assembly](#g-assembly) (a component of pure composition) has three wiring declarations ([§8.6][s8-6]). A `local_wires` pair wires one child's [face](#g-face) (the name a port wears on its component's boundary) to another's. An `input_wires` entry routes a face to an immediate child's face. An `output_wires` entry sources a face from an immediate child's face. An entry of any of the three is a wire, and an entry of the first is a [local wire](#g-local-wire) ([D-303][d-303], [D-314][d-314]). **Every connection endpoint names an immediate child and one of its faces** ([D-207][d-207]). An endpoint naming no port of the child it resolves to is an `UnknownPort` error, at either end of the wire ([D-113][d-113], [Appendix C][sC]). The one-level rule covers all three wiring declarations. [Container children](#g-container-children) (the elements of a `Tuple` or `NamedTuple` field holding only components, [§8.5][s8-5]) keep their key segment ([D-085][d-085]), so `"aircraft/2/face"` is one level and not two ([D-207][d-207]). A container declared name-transparent ([§8.5][s8-5]) is the exception. Its elements go by bare key, so `"ctl/face"` reads exactly as a plain child's endpoint does, the same one level ([D-211][d-211]). Routing across several levels is therefore declared level by level, with each assembly speaking only of its own children. This restriction is named the locality law ([D-065][d-065]), and fragments of the condition algebra follow the same law ([§14.2][s14-2]). The sketch below shows such routing in an aircraft. The root `Cessna172` holds a systems assembly `Systems`, which holds the landing gear `Ldg`, whose three legs are `left`, `right` and `nose`. The root's input `trn` is a terrain field handle ([§4.4][s4-4]). `Cessna172` hands its `trn` input to `systems`, `Systems` hands it to `ldg`, and `Ldg` fans it out to its three legs. The fan-out is declared at the level where the paths diverge. ```julia input_wires(::Cessna172) = ("trn" => "systems/trn", …) input_wires(::Systems) = ("trn" => "ldg/trn", …) input_wires(::Ldg) = ("trn" => ("left/trn_field", "right/trn_field", "nose/trn_field"), …) ``` Two consequences explain the rule. The first is that faces become the only currency crossing an assembly boundary, so substitutability holds at *every* boundary rather than only at generic ones. This is the contract-is-the-interface principle of [§8.3][s8-3] with its one leak closed. The second consequence is that the face graph is total ([D-207][d-207]). Every signal crossing a boundary bears a declared face there, at every level it crosses. Totality is what the condition algebra addresses against. An `at` prefix stopping at a child's face has a name to resolve through ([§14.2][s14-2]). The ceremony the rule costs is the re-export entry per level. It is the ceremony the design already pays almost everywhere, because every level of a realistic tree is a generic [seam](#g-seam) (a narrow, named interface kept deliberately thin), where one-level routing was mandatory in any case ([§8.8][s8-8]). Where the entries multiply, they are computed rather than typed, through `input_passthrough`/`output_passthrough` over a single authored feed list ([§8.8][s8-8]). Parallel routes threading many boundaries are the signal to gather them into a component of their own ([D-207][d-207]). Enforcement lives in the path-resolution primitive itself, `resolve` ([§13.3][s13-3], [D-130][d-130]). It walks declared field types alongside instances ([D-061][d-061]). An endpoint reaching past the immediate child is a `PathResolution` error ([Appendix C][sC]). Paths are validated at build time, and renames break loudly. #### Type-checking a wire Once both endpoints of a wire resolve, two clauses type-check it ([§8.2][s8-2]). The first is the [nominal](#g-nominal) bound check. Nominal means the `Float64` [activation](#g-activation) (the build's typed products at a given scalar type), and a declaration's `Float64` face. The bound check is stated over declaration evaluations. The producer's declaration at `Float64` must be `<:` the consumer's input entry at `Float64` ([D-078][d-078]). It is one uniform rule, and it degenerates to exact equality for a concrete entry. A violation is `WireTypeMismatch`. Beside it stands the walk-compatibility clause, for a continuous consumer only. A [walking](#g-walked) producer leaf, one the producer left unpinned, requires an unpinned entry. A pinned producer leaf, one declared `Pinned`, satisfies either entry, because frozen values embed upward under any activation. A discrete consumer takes the bound check alone, so a continuous producer feeding a discrete consumer is unconditionally legal, for the reason [§8.2][s8-2] gives ([D-295][d-295], [D-263][d-263]). The producer's declaration and the entry are plain declarations the walk retypes, so the walk clause is decided in the structure step by retyping them at a marker scalar. That is declaration reading, and no user stage code runs ([§9.1][s9-1]). An [abstract entry](#g-abstract-entry) is an input entry whose declared type is abstract. **The walk clause decides an abstract entry on the whole declaration**, an entry whose leaves cannot be enumerated ([D-236][d-236]). The producer's declaration at the marker, or the same with every pinned leaf lifted to the marker, must be `<:` the entry at the marker ([D-236][d-236]). The walk clause admits an opaque leaf on the same terms as a pinned or walking leaf. An opaque leaf ([§4.3][s4-3]), a handle stored whole, has no scalar position to embed through, and the walk clause does not need one. **A frozen handle arriving at an unpinned entry is the producer's [cell](#g-cell)** (its output's entry in the signal table), gathered as stored ([D-264][d-264]). The consumer's own arithmetic promotes whatever it reads from it ([D-264][d-264]). A discrete producer of a handle, which pins wholesale, is admitted the same way. **The identity rule for an opaque leaf governs a store, never a wire** ([§9.5][s9-5], [D-264][d-264]). One consequence follows from that rule. A component that re-emits a frozen handle pins that output too, so a handle's pin is a property of its chain ([D-264][d-264]). A violation of the walk clause is `WalkingFaceAtFrozenEntry`, naming both endpoints, the leaf and both declared leaf types. The message carries both remedies, "remove the entry's `Pinned` if the consumer promotes; feed it from a non-walking source if the freeze is genuine". The `Freeze` block is the one-line spelling of the second remedy ([§13.7][s13-7], [D-266][d-266]). The walk clause also gives the two [contract](#g-contract) sides their failure asymmetry. A pin written at an entry whose consumer really promotes fails at the *first nominal build*, at the wire, with both endpoints named ([D-263][d-263]). It fails there because an input has a build-time counterparty. The output side has none, so a pin written at a leaf that really participates lurks until the first `Dual` activation. It lurks loudly, never silently ([§8.2][s8-2], [D-286][d-286]). #### Connections per port Fan-out is free. One producer may feed many consumers. The converse is strict. **Every input port takes exactly one connection**, with no exceptions ([D-170][d-170]). Aggregation is done by junctions ([§6.2][s6-2]). The rule spans levels ([D-170][d-170]). A child input fed by a local wire at its own level *and* handed up through its parent's `input_wires` is a two-producers build error, `TwoProducers`. Routing a face upward cannot silently double-feed. There is no auto-bubbling of unconnected inputs ([D-043][d-043]). Unconnected input ports are a build error, with no silent defaults. The check is a whole-tree property, not a per-declaration one ([D-048][d-048]). Both this check and the two-producers check are decided at the root, since neither is decidable below it ([D-048][d-048]). Within a single assembly declaration an unfed child input is simply *awaiting a wire from above*, either a local wire or an `input_wires` entry handing the obligation up one level ([§8.6][s8-6]). The error, `UnconnectedInput`, fires at the root build for any input whose obligation chain never terminates. The one legitimate terminus fed by no [component](#g-component) is the root component's own input face, a [root input](#g-root-input) ([§11.3][s11-3], [D-208][d-208]). **Unconnected output ports are legal, silently, with no build-time warning** ([D-084][d-084]). ### 6.2 Aggregation: explicit summing junctions Several physical quantities are totals over many contributors. Total wrench, total mass properties and total internal angular momentum are the examples. In Flight.jl these totals come from tree walks. FlightPhysics' generated functions `get_wr_b`, `get_mp_b` and `get_hr_b` sum each quantity over a model tree. This section states the rule and the junction that carries it. It then covers what the explicit form buys, the hierarchical aggregation idiom, zero contributors and the cost. **N-to-1 physical aggregation is expressed by ordinary junction [components](#g-component) and explicit wires** ([D-037][d-037]). There is no framework aggregation mechanism. There are no multi-connection [ports](#g-port), no declared fold ops and no identity-element opt-outs. Every input port takes exactly one connection, everywhere ([§6.1][s6-1]). ```julia struct Junction{In, Out, N, F} <: AbstractComponent # library-provided (§13.7) f::F # the fold, N-ary; instance data end const SumJunction{V, N} = Junction{V, V, N, typeof(+)} x_init(::Junction) = (;) # stateless, continuous: the empty store is the tier marker (§8.2) u_types(::Junction{In, Out, N}) where {In, Out, N} = NamedTuple{ntuple(i -> Symbol(:in, i), N)}(ntuple(_ -> In, N)) y_types(::Junction{In, Out}) where {In, Out} = (out = Out,) y_direct(j::Junction, (; u)) = (out = j.f(u...),) ``` **The parameters are the nominal port types and the arity** ([D-311][d-311]), as in `SumJunction{Wrench{Float64}, 3}`, and the fold is instance data. `Wrench` is FlightPhysics' force-and-torque type, here parametrized by its scalar type as a walked type of [§7.2][s7-2]. `SumJunction{V, N}` is the `Junction` at `+`. A custom fold, such as a weighted blend or a mass-properties composition, is written at the site as `Junction{In, Out, N}(f)`, which infers `F`. The junction is a continuous leaf, so its [contracts](#g-contract) (a component's declared interface) are written at nominal `Float64`. On a continuous leaf the [leaf walk](#g-leaf-walk) (the framework's derivation of per-activation types) retypes them ([§8.2][s8-2]). An input entry at `Wrench{Float64}` is the tolerant form a promoting consumer writes. Walking, frozen and [root-input](#g-root-input) contributors are all admissible behind it. A root input is the root component's own input face. The Bool gates of [§13.7][s13-7] are the same leaf at `|` and `&` ([D-311][d-311]). The junctions themselves, [summing junctions](#g-summing-junction) and Bool gates, are the seed of the standard component library committed in [§13.7][s13-7] ([D-062][d-062]). They are ordinary components with no framework privileges. The inventory grows by judgement against the three guidelines [§13.7][s13-7] states ([D-313][d-313]). Wired at an ownership boundary, inside the [assembly](#g-assembly) that owns the contributors, the junction is ordinary structure. An assembly is a component of pure composition, with no dynamics of its own. In the block below, `Systems` is the systems assembly. Its children include the aerodynamics `aero`, the powerplant `pwp` and the landing gear `ldg`, and a child `dynamics` that consumes the total. `wr_sum::SumJunction{Wrench{Float64}, 3}` is a field of `Systems` like any other child ([§8.5][s8-5]). ```julia local_wires(::Systems) = ( "aero/wrench" => "wr_sum/in1", "pwp/wrench" => "wr_sum/in2", "ldg/wrench" => "wr_sum/in3", "wr_sum/out" => "dynamics/wr_ext", ) ``` #### What the explicit form buys - Every bookkeeping mistake at the junction is loud under the declaration layer. A contributor the junction counts but nobody wires is an unconnected-input error naming `in4`. A double-wired slot violates single-connection. A stale arity surfaces as one or the other. The bookkeeping is ceremony, never silence. A contributor's output that nobody wires stays legal and silent, as any unconnected output does ([D-084][d-084]). - The aggregate is a first-class signal. `"wr_sum/out"` is an ordinary port. It is loggable, GUI-visible, and fanned out to a second consumer (a component monitoring structural loads) for one wire. - Aggregation logic is arbitrary stage-2 code, such as mass-properties composition with its transport terms, or weighted blends. It is not restricted to a declared commutative-associative binary op. - **Fold order is author-visible** ([D-037][d-037]). It is the positional order of the junction's inputs. Reassigning contributors to different slots changes summation order, hence bits, because float addition is not associative. The ordering is deterministic per configuration and under author control, which is strictly more explicit than a framework-canonical order. - For the handful of real sites, a named site-specific junction documents the contributor set better than generated slots, at the price of hard-coding it into a type. An example is `u_types(::VehicleWrenchSum) = (aero = …, ldg = …, pwp = …)`. The generic positional form remains the tool for configuration-variable sites. Both are plain components, and the framework is not involved. #### The hierarchical aggregation idiom **Each assembly that owns contributors aggregates them with an internal junction and exports the total** ([D-037][d-037]). The junction is a component inside the assembly, and the assembly exports its `out` port ([§8.6][s8-6]). The [§6.1][s6-1] connection rules force this shape. A child is opaque to wiring at every boundary, so every assembly that owns contributors must export its aggregate. For example, the landing gear `Ldg` sums its three struts and exports `wrench`. The systems assembly sums `aero + ldg + pwp`. The vehicle wires the systems totals into the rigid-body equations of motion. Junctions placed this way replace the tree walk. Each recursion step of FlightPhysics' tree walk becomes one visible junction at the level that owns the contributors. For Flight.jl's Cessna 172 model that is about four junctions and fifteen wires, written once. They read as a manifest of what weighs, what pushes and what spins. Only physical contributors publish the ports a junction sums. A strut publishes `wrench`, and avionics publishes nothing. Frame responsibility is as in Flight.jl. Contributors publish in the common body frame, applying their own mounting transforms at source. Do not bundle the three quantities into one contribution struct. Contributors are ragged, each having only some of the three quantities. A bundled contribution struct forces zero-filled identity noise through every port, which is the "silently sum nothing" hazard in a new coat ([D-037][d-037]). A `sum_ports!`-style helper would instantiate, wire and export in one call. **Such a helper is [guarded-addition](#g-guarded-addition) sugar** (a capability the design admits but does not build), added when real models show the pattern repeated ([D-037][d-037]). #### Zero contributors Ragged contributors bottom out at none. Some configuration has a consumer whose required aggregate input has *no* physical contributors at all. FlightPhysics' bare-propagation vehicle with no systems, `Vehicle{NoVehicleSystems}`, is one. It has zero contributors to external wrench and to internal angular momentum, while its rigid-body dynamics component, `VehicleDynamics`, requires both unconditionally. There is no junction to write and no producer to wire. [§6.1][s6-1] bans unconnected inputs and silent defaults. The identity element a zero-arity junction would need is deliberately absent ([D-037][d-037]). **The zero-contributor spelling is a library [`Constant` source](#g-constant-source) ([§13.7][s13-7]) wired straight to the consumer's input** ([D-143][d-143]), as in `Constant(Wrench())` → `dynamics/wr_ext`. A constant source has no inputs and no state, and publishes a value its instance holds. The zero total thus becomes declared structure. The configuration states "external wrench ≡ 0" as a visible wire and an observable port, rather than as an identity method the framework supplies behind the author's back. **The `Constant` wire is not the banned default ([§6.1][s6-1]) in component clothing but its opposite** ([D-143][d-143]). That default is silent and consumer-declared. This one is loud and assembly-declared, with the author writing the child and the wire, both inspectable. #### The cost Adding a contributor from another subtree edits the assembly levels between its producer and the junction ([§6.1][s6-1]) instead of none. In exchange, explicit wiring buys per-contributor values and intermediate totals as observable ports, with every silence in the aggregation's bookkeeping inverted into a warning or error ([D-037][d-037]). Consumer-declared folds with multi-connection legality are closed ([D-037][d-037]). ---
