9089	### D-242 — Name the resolution cases wiring, service and inspection, and retire "register" and "load-bearing" as spec vocabulary
9090	
9091	**Status.** ratified
9092	
9093	**Position.** [§13.3][s13-3]'s three resolution cases are named by their client, and
9094	two words leave the design documents' vocabulary, the log included. Every
9095	position stands as ratified; only the words for unchanged concepts move, under
9096	rule 2's word-substitution exception and rule 7's audit. This overturns
9097	[D-122][d-122]'s reservation of "register" for the mode sense.
9098	- *Wiring* replaces [D-130][d-130]'s *structural*: connection declarations, resolved
9099	  in Stratum A under [§6.1][s6-1]'s one-level rule.
9100	- *Service* replaces *load-bearing*: condition entries, trim `reads` and
9101	  taps, checked at the authoring or mount level. "Service" keeps its [§14][s14]
9102	  meaning; the paths are the ones a stopped-sim service compiles.
9103	- *Inspection* replaces *diagnostic*: device read bindings, GUI panels,
9104	  snapshot and log inspection, resolved by the instance walk. It is [D-083][d-083]'s
9105	  own word for that side, so the two vocabularies for one line collapse into
9106	  one. The paired side is *integration reads*.
9107	- "Register" in the sense "a mode or idiom in which something is done" (the
9108	  retired [§D.10][sD-10] entry) is gone. Its compounds are renamed by family: the
9109	  *harness writer* and *what-if replay* (glossary anchors renamed), the run
9110	  *modes* (unattended, test-harness, REPL), usage *idioms* (`step!`,
9111	  test-rig, parallel sweep), the *didactic style* of errors, the
9112	  *by-allocation convention*, the sample time's two *forms*, the naming
9113	  convention's four *classes*, and [§14.4][s14-4]'s *two ways of applying one plan*
9114	  named as the specialized `apply!` and the dynamic walk. The word survives
9115	  only in its storage sense: a slot holding a value.
9116	- "Load-bearing" is dropped outright, its plain-intensifier uses replaced by
9117	  ordinary words.
9118	
9119	**Spec.** [§8.1][s8-1], [§8.2][s8-2], [§10.5][s10-5], [§11.2][s11-2], [§11.3][s11-3], [§11.5][s11-5], [§12.6][s12-6], [§12.7][s12-7], [§13.2][s13-2], [§13.3][s13-3], [§13.5][s13-5],
9120	[§14.4][s14-4], [§14.8][s14-8], [§3.4][s3-4], [§8.6][s8-6], [Appendix A][sA], [§D.10][sD-10]
9121	
9122	**Rationale.** Four terms failed the cold reader at once. "Register" asked
9123	for a linguistics sense no technical reader reaches for; a heading such as
9124	"the duty is register-scoped" was decodable only from the table beneath it,
9125	and the same word also named storage slots elsewhere in the spec, so a reader
9126	could not tell which sense applied. "Load-bearing" named the right case at a
9127	distance but three quarters of its occurrences were plain intensifier, so the
9128	term of art had no room to be one; it also named the stakes axis where the
9129	spec's own argument turns on where a violation's cost lands. "Diagnostic"
9130	already meant warnings and errors throughout, with an appendix named for
9131	them, and sat a few characters from "didactic". "Structural" was
9132	serviceable but said nothing about wiring. Naming each case by its client
9133	lets the table's row name carry who resolves and, through the chapter the
9134	client belongs to, what is at stake.
9135	
9136	**Rejected.**
9137	- *"Task", "analysis" or "operation" in place of "service":* "task" collides
9138	  with Julia's `Task`; "analysis" excludes initialization and `capture`;
9139	  "operation" is accurate but buys little over the compounded "stopped-sim
9140	  service" and costs a chapter-wide rename.
9141	- *Naming the middle case by its stakes ("portable" or "relocatable"
9142	  paths):* self-explaining, but the other two rows name clients, and a mixed
9143	  scheme is harder to hold.
9144	- *Keeping "register" in the mode sense with an inline gloss at each first
9145	  use:* the gloss fights the reader's prior at every occurrence instead of
9146	  once.
9147	- *Leaving the log in its old vocabulary:* rule 2's first reading; rejected
9148	  because the substitution moves no position and the old words would mislead
9149	  every later reader of the log, so the exception was written into rule 2.
