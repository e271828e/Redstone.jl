13180	### D-314 — Rename `inner_wires` to `local_wires`
13181	
13182	**Status.** ratified
13183	
13184	**Position.** The class marker is `local_wires`, and the child-to-child kind
13185	of wire is a "local wire" in prose. `input_wires` and `output_wires` stand.
13186	Signatures, entry spellings and semantics are unchanged, and `Group`'s field
13187	and keyword follow the declaration. No aliases and no deprecation shims, on
13188	[D-220][d-220]'s terms.
13189	
13190	**Spec.** [§3.3][s3-3], [§6.1][s6-1], [§8.2][s8-2], [§8.5][s8-5], [§8.6][s8-6], [§8.8][s8-8], [§11.7][s11-7], [§14.2][s14-2], [Appendix B][sB],
13191	[Appendix C][sC], [Appendix D][sD]
13192	
13193	**Rationale.** [D-303][d-303] accepted the `inner_`/`input_` near-collision as a
13194	reading nuisance. The nuisance is in the common case: every assembly writes
13195	the two declarations on adjacent lines, and `inner` and `input` share their
13196	first two letters and their length, so the eye tells them apart by the
13197	third letter alone. `local` keeps the length, so the trio still aligns, and
13198	shares no letter run with `input`.
13199	
13200	`local` also names the distinction more exactly than `inner`. A local wire
13201	has both ends inside the assembly's boundary, and an input or output wire
13202	has one end on it. "Local" says "confined within, crossing no boundary",
13203	which is the contrast; "inner" says it only by opposition to an "outer"
13204	that nothing is called. [D-303][d-303] rejected `wires_local` because `local` names
13205	a scope beside two directions, but the three words sort the wires by one
13206	question, whether the wire crosses the boundary and in which direction, and
13207	a scope word is the right answer to its first half.
13208	
13209	`local` is a Julia keyword, and `local_wires` is an ordinary identifier the
13210	parser never confuses with it. `implementation.md`'s naming rules use
13211	"a local" for a local variable, and "local wire" is always two words, so the
13212	two senses never meet in one phrase.
13213	
13214	The rename does not change what kind of thing the three declarations hold.
13215	[D-303][d-303]'s rule that "wire" covers an entry of any of the three stands, and
13216	the [§8.6][s8-6] rule keeps its three verbs. That a boundary entry aliases a face
13217	rather than creating an endpoint is a mechanism the spec states where
13218	faces are defined, not a difference the names expose.
13219	
13220	**Rejected.**
13221	- *Keeping `inner_wires`:* the near-collision sits in every assembly's most
13222	  common two lines, and the build-time failure [D-303][d-303] relied on catches a
13223	  misfiled entry, not a misread one.
13224	- *`wires` alone:* the strongest contrast, and the inner set is the
13225	  assembly's wires proper. But `wires` is a local in `build.jl`'s cluster
13226	  code, which the naming rules forbid beside a package function of that
13227	  name, and the bare noun contradicts [D-303][d-303]'s rule that an entry of any of
13228	  the three is a wire.
13229	- *`peer_wires`, `child_wires`:* both precise about joining siblings.
13230	  "Peer" has no footing in the spec, and "child wires" reads as the
13231	  children's own wires.
13232	- *`internal_wires`, `intra_wires`:* keep the `in` prefix, so they keep the
13233	  collision.
13234	- *`input_aliases`/`output_aliases` beside the rename:* exposes the
13235	  virtual nature of the boundary entries in the API, at the price of the
13236	  uniform "wire" story, [D-303][d-303]'s rule, the glossary and every diagnostic
13237	  that pairs a local wire with an `input_wires` chain. One rename suffices
13238	  for readability, and the aliasing fact already has a home in the prose.
