9981	### D-258 — "Schedule" is the tick timing and "execution order" the stage sequence
9982	
9983	**Status.** ratified
9984	
9985	**Position.** The spec uses "schedule" for the per-component tick timing,
9986	matching `Schedule`, and "execution order" for the order in which the stage
9987	functions run, carried by the `Dataflow` artifact. "Ordering" is the
9988	activity. "Bound schedule" is retired. [§5.1][s5-1] is "The ordering problem",
9989	Stratum B's heading is "execution order", and the glossary's "sweep" is one
9990	pass through the execution order.
9991	
9992	**Spec.** [§5.1][s5-1], [§5.3][s5-3], [§9.1][s9-1], [§9.2][s9-2], [§10.5][s10-5], [Appendix D][sD]
9993	
9994	**Rationale.** One word carried two senses: the topological order Stratum B
9995	computes and the `(D, Φ, Δt)` table deployment binds. With both becoming
9996	types ([D-253][d-253], [D-254][d-254]) the prose had to pick. The tick table keeps "schedule"
9997	because that is what the word means in the multi-rate literature and in
9998	[§10.5][s10-5]. The stage sequence is an order, so it is called one. A first sweep
9999	used "dataflow" for it and read wrongly at most sites: a dataflow is a
10000	graph, and the spec speaks of positions in the order, subsets of it, and
10001	re-running it, none of which a graph has. The type keeps the name because it
10002	carries the graph. The log keeps its vocabulary of the day under
10003	`decisions_style.md` rule 2; the sweep is the spec's and its companions'.
10004	
10005	**Rejected.**
10006	- *"Dataflow" for the order:* a graph has no positions.
10007	- *Renaming `Dataflow` to match the prose:* the artifact is more than the
10008	  order.
10009	- *Keeping "bound schedule":* with the anchor-relative triples in
10010	  `Structure` and the bound table in `Schedule`, "bound" no longer
10011	  distinguishes anything.
