8513	### D-228 — Attribute runtime diagnostics by cell, never by payload
8514	
8515	**Status.** ratified
8516	
8517	**Position.** A rate-limited diagnostic kind names its subjects in the payload
8518	and never its writer. The writer is the identity of the cell it was written
8519	to, and the published status record's `who` and the tail residue carry it.
8520	
8521	**Spec.** [§11.8][s11-8], [Appendix C][sC]
8522	
8523	**Rationale.** [§11.8][s11-8] gives every writer its own cell: one per rostered
8524	device, one for the harness writer, one for the loop. A device reports
8525	through its handle, the handle owns the cell, and no call anywhere passes a
8526	device id. At the drain each cell becomes one record of the published
8527	framework status, keyed by the writer's name, with the ring, the suppressed
8528	counts and the cumulative per-writer × per-kind totals beside it; the run's-end
8529	residue is built the same way. The writer is therefore a structural fact of
8530	the channel, and a payload field would restate what the value's container
8531	already says, at every emission site, with room for the two to disagree.
8532	[Appendix C][sC]'s rows had named a device or writer id in five payloads, and the
8533	2026-09-04 audit read them as missing fields. The rows now name subjects
8534	only. `DeviceJoinTimeout` keeps its device id: it names the abandoned device,
8535	and the loop writes it. The one cost is a value shown apart from its record,
8536	which is a renderer's concern: a renderer presents each value under its
8537	record's writer.
8538	
8539	**Rejected.**
8540	- *A `writer` field on the five kinds:* the wrapper, the staging shim, the
8541	  drain and the author's own loop body would each spell a name the channel
8542	  already guarantees, and a wrong name would contradict the cell.
