726: ### 14.7 The trim problem: NamedTuple decisions, declared reads, named residuals
727: 
728: The aircraft author ships one value. It says what the solver may vary, what
729: those decisions make of the model, what to read back after each evaluation,
730: and which equations the readings must satisfy. That value is a
731: [`TrimProblem`](#g-trimproblem). This section gives its fields, then its
732: decisions and parameters, its reads, its residuals and checks, the
733: formulation it encodes and its recorded extensions.
734: 
735: #### The fields
736: 
737: **The field set is normative and closed** ([D-118][d-118], [D-262][d-262]).
738: It is closed because the lift ([§14.9][s14-9]) is field-by-field.
739: 
740: - **`guess`, `lower` and `upper` are same-named all-`Float64` NamedTuples**
741:   ([D-069][d-069]).
742: - `condition` is the function over decisions whose value is a
743:   [condition](#g-condition) (the path-addressed sparse overlay that sets a
744:   build's state).
745: - `reads` is the declared read set.
746: - `residuals` is the residual function.
747: - `tolerances` is an all-`Float64` NamedTuple, same-named as the residual
748:   function's return.
749: - `checks` is the check function, and `check_tolerances` an all-`Float64`
750:   NamedTuple same-named as its return. Both default to empty.
751: 
752: `tolerances` is carried *in the problem* because a relocated problem must carry
753: its own convergence test. [`at`](#g-at) (the scoping combinator storing a path
754: prefix beside a node) passes it through untouched ([D-118][d-118]).
755: 
756: [Worked](#g-worked), the cruise case of Flight.jl's Cessna 172 model
757: (`c172.jl`) reduces to its three-equation core. The real problem is the same
758: shape with the full 7-variable search, and the θ-constraint elimination
759: survives inside its condition function, which the worked problem calls
760: `trim_condition`.
761: 
762: ```julia
763: cruise = TrimProblem(
764:     guess      = (throttle = 0.6, elevator = -0.05, α = 0.06),
765:     lower      = (throttle = 0.0, elevator = -1.0,  α = -0.1),
766:     upper      = (throttle = 1.0, elevator =  1.0,  α =  0.3),
767:     condition  = d -> trim_condition(ac, params, d),    #params closed over (§14.2)
768:     reads      = reads(v̇_b = get_deriv("vehicle/dynamics", :v_eb_b),
769:                        ω̇_b = get_deriv("vehicle/dynamics", :ω_eb_b),
770:                        EAS = get_output("vehicle/airdata", :EAS)),
771:     residuals  = (r, d) -> (axial_force  = r.v̇_b[1],
772:                             normal_force = r.v̇_b[3],
773:                             pitch_moment = r.ω̇_b[2]),
774:     tolerances = (axial_force = 1e-3, normal_force = 1e-3, pitch_moment = 1e-4),
775:     checks           = (r, d) -> (EAS = r.EAS - params.EAS,),  # read back after the commit
776:     check_tolerances = (EAS = 0.1,))
777: ```
778: 
779: The rest of the section takes what the author ships one piece at a time,
780: against Flight.jl's `c172.jl`.
781: 
782: #### Decisions and parameters
783: 
784: Decision variables, initial guess and box bounds are plain, same-*named*,
785: all-`Float64` NamedTuples. FlightPhysics' decision-variable supertype,
786: `AbstractTrimState{N}`, is dropped. It is an alias of StaticArrays'
787: `FieldVector{N, Float64}`. Its only job was vectorization, and vectorization
788: is the service's ([D-069][d-069]). The service packs and unpacks by field order, and
789: that order is the `guess` NamedTuple's own. This [seam](#g-seam) runs on the
790: rule [§9.5][s9-5] states, "the names are the pairing, order carries no
791: semantics" ([D-151][d-151]). `lower` and `upper` are checked at setup for
792: key-set equality with `guess` and for `Float64` fields. They are then
793: canonicalized to `guess`'s field order by the same type-level reorder
794: (`NamedTuple{keys(guess)}(lower)`). A permuted bound spelling is therefore a
795: non-event, rather than `α`'s bound silently applied to `throttle`. A key-set or
796: field-type mismatch is `TrimProblemInvalid`. Guess, bounds and the returned
797: solution share one spelling, and `Base.merge(guess, (throttle = 0.3,))` is free
798: warm-start tweaking. An author who wants a documented `@kwdef` struct keeps it
799: privately and converts.
800: 
801: `TrimParameters` is the struct in Flight.jl's `c172.jl` that holds a trim's
802: targets and settings. **`TrimParameters` stays a plain user struct** the
803: framework never sees ([D-069][d-069], [D-118][d-118]). The assignment is the
804: pure `trim_condition(ac, params, d)` [fragment](#g-fragment)-tree function
805: ([§14.2][s14-2]), applied per iteration by the compiled plan ([§14.4][s14-4]).
806: 
807: #### Reads
808: 
809: **The read side is declared, then compiled** ([D-069][d-069]). **The spelling
810: is `reads(name = get_state(path, leaf) | get_deriv(path, leaf) |
811: get_output(path, leaf) | get_input(leaf) | get_face(leaf), ...)`**, the
812: service read set ([§14.4][s14-4], [D-124][d-124]). Each `leaf` is a
813: [leaf address](#g-leaf-address) (the field name with dotted and bracketed
814: steps below it). `get_state` and `get_deriv` address a declared state field
815: and its derivative (validated against `x_init`/`s_init`). `get_output`
816: addresses a declared output [port](#g-port) (validated against `y_types`).
817: `get_input` and `get_face` address a [root input](#g-root-input)
818: [face](#g-face) and an output face (validated against the faces of the level
819: the read set is mounted at, the root's when unmounted) ([D-277][d-277]).
820: 
821: The path [selectors](#g-selector) (the closed family of deferred reads
822: resolving against a source) reach any port the structure has
823: ([§13.3][s13-3], [D-414][d-414]). As authoring guidance, an equilibrium
824: equation meant to survive substitution at a generic seam reads a face
825: ([D-414][d-414], [D-125][d-125]). A trim evaluation needing a value a
826: [component](#g-component) computes without declaring it finds no selector for
827: it ([§14.4][s14-4]). That is a signal that the component should export it. A
828: derivative wanted across a [contract](#g-contract) boundary takes the same
829: remedy. Publish it as an ordinary output port computed in `y_direct`. That is
830: the one-line binding of the fused idiom ([§5.3][s5-3]), made contract. By
831: convention, then, `get_deriv` stays inside subtrees the problem's author owns
832: ([D-414][d-414]). The compiled reader fills a stack-only NamedTuple per
833: evaluation. It is the gather twin ([§14.4][s14-4]).
834: 
835: #### Residuals and checks
836: 
837: **The user supplies a residual *system*, not a scalar cost**
838: ([D-069][d-069]). It is authored as a NamedTuple of named equations. The
839: residual function that returns it has this signature.
840: 
841: ```julia
842: residuals(reads::NamedTuple, d::NamedTuple) → NamedTuple
843: ```
844: 
845: **What the solver varies is passed, and what is fixed per problem is closed
846: over** ([D-118][d-118]).
847: 
848: The gathered reads and the decision NamedTuple arrive as arguments. `d` is the
849: one value that *cannot* be closed over. `TrimParameters` stays behind the
850: closure, exactly as `condition` already holds it, and the framework never sees
851: it. Being user-shaped, that record is also where any environment handles the
852: condition math needs conventionally ride. [§8.2][s8-2] gives the
853: [value-level constructor](#g-value-level-constructor) (the plain exported
854: function building a field handle from the component and input values) that
855: builds such a handle, and [§14.1][s14-1] the pre-sweep doctrine. The problem
856: *receives* the environment, and never writes it ([§14.9][s14-9]).
857: 
858: **The returned NamedTuple's names are the equation names** the report and the
859: failure messages use ([D-118][d-118]). The service packs residuals and
860: tolerances to the solver's vector by field order. The order is `tolerances`'
861: field order, the declared side again, canonical for the r-side. Each residual
862: return is reordered to it (`NamedTuple{keys(tolerances)}(r)`) before packing
863: ([D-151][d-151]). An equation list spelled in a different order inside the
864: lambda therefore pairs correctly and costs nothing. The decisions rule holds
865: symmetrically on both ends of the seam. Names pair, and order never does.
866: Names and types are checked at setup, because the guess evaluation the service
867: performs anyway observes the residual key set. A `tolerances` key-set
868: mismatch, or any field-type disagreement on either side of the seam, is
869: `TrimProblemInvalid` ([Appendix C][sC]), with the offending field and the
870: names or types in hand. Order is never a mismatch.
871: 
872: **Checks are equations the service reports once, at the committed state, and
873: never solves** ([D-262][d-262]). `checks` has the residual function's
874: signature, and `check_tolerances` pairs with its return as `tolerances` pairs
875: with the residuals'. The service gathers the checks after the commit, from the
876: [sweep](#g-sweep) of [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the initialization
877: boundary, the ordinary macro-sequence with an empty integrate), and reports
878: them ([§14.8][s14-8]). Setup observes their return's shape at the guess
879: evaluation, as it observes the residuals' ([§14.8][s14-8]). Their reads join
880: the problem's one read set.
881: 
882: The reason for the checks lies in analytic elimination. Under it, the
883: condition math pins the airspeed and the flight-path angle from the targets
884: and the atmosphere the user's `params` record holds, while the world measures
885: against the atmosphere the [baseline](#g-baseline) wrote. If the two disagree,
886: the residuals vanish at a true equilibrium that is not the requested point,
887: and nothing in the residual system can tell ([D-139][d-139]). A check reading
888: EAS (equivalent airspeed) from the world's airdata at the committed state and
889: subtracting the requested value is where a world measurement meets the
890: request, so any mismatch moves it. The checks stay out of the solve because
891: they carry no information for it ([D-262][d-262]).
892: 
893: #### The formulation
894: 
895: **The FlightPhysics formulation's core is correct and survives verbatim as
896: user math** ([D-069][d-069]). That core is analytic elimination, namely
897: FlightPhysics' `θ_constraint` substituting the pitch constraint, filter and
898: actuator equilibria imposed by construction, and the minimal 7-variable
899: search over `c172.jl`'s `TrimState`.
900: 
901: What changes is the numerics. Trim is a square root-find. The Cessna 172
902: model's derivative-free scalar minimization over $\|r\|^2$ against a hand-scaled
903: absolute `stopval` was the rational choice only because Jacobians through
904: FlightCore's mutating `f_ode!` chain and the assignment math were out of reach
905: ([D-069][d-069]).
906: 
907: The *default* is nonlinear least squares on $r(d)$ with exact AD Jacobians, in
908: the trust-region/Levenberg–Marquardt family ([§14.8][s14-8], [D-069][d-069]).
909: The `Dual` [activation](#g-activation) (the build's typed products at a given
910: scalar type) seeds the decision variables through the `T`-generic assignment,
911: sweep and `x_deriv`. The seeds survive the condition write because a
912: decision-descended leaf is `Dual`-typed in the shape and keeps its partials
913: ([§14.3][s14-3], [D-148][d-148]). Convergence is quadratic (~5–15
914: evaluations), the tolerances are per-residual and physical, and failure
915: reports name the unbalanced equations with magnitudes. The convergence verdict
916: itself is service-owned and backend-independent ([§14.8][s14-8]).
917: 
918: **Non-squareness degrades gracefully** ([D-069][d-069]). Redundant actuation
919: becomes weighted or minimum-norm least squares. Infeasible demands converge to
920: a nonzero residual identifying the impossible balance. At the solution,
921: $\partial r / \partial d$ is free flight-physics data (control effectiveness)
922: that cross-checks linearization.
923: 
924: **The derivative-free scalar path would survive as the fallback**, with
925: Flight.jl's algorithm as its degenerate case ([D-069][d-069]). [§14.8][s14-8]
926: gives the normalization of the residuals against the tolerances that would
927: repair the hand-scaled absolute threshold.
928: 
929: #### Recorded extensions
930: 
931: **Two problems are [recorded, not built](#g-recorded-not-built)** (a
932: worked-out extension deliberately left unimplemented, its seams named)
933: ([D-069][d-069]). They are closed-loop sampled-data trim and on-ground static
934: equilibrium, each simply another problem value over the same service.
935: Closed-loop trim appends `s_update(s) - s = 0` residuals via a nondestructive
936: [scratch](#g-scratch) evaluation of `s_update`, which was structurally
937: impossible under FlightCore's mutating `f_periodic!`. On-ground static equilibrium
938: solves strut compressions and attitude against gear forces.
939: 
940: ### 14.8 The trim service: solver seam, scratch stores, commit and report
941: 
942: A [`TrimProblem`](#g-trimproblem) is an inert value until a service runs it.
943: `trim!` is that service. It drives a solver it holds behind one method, works
944: on its own [scratch](#g-scratch) stores (mutable working storage whose contents
945: are never authoritative), commits a converged solution the way `init!` would,
946: and returns a report.
947: 
948: #### The backend seam
949: 
950: The signature is
951: `trim!(model, problem; baseline, t0 = 0.0, backend = LevenbergMarquardt())`,
952: where `model` is a [`Model`](#g-model) (a deployment materialized at one
953: scalar type) at `Float64` ([D-319][d-319]). A `Model` at another scalar is
954: refused as `ArgumentInvalid` with reason `:non_nominal` ([D-319][d-319]). A
955: model that a simulation has claimed is refused before any check, as
956: `ArgumentInvalid` with reason `:claimed`, since the call commits
957: ([D-318][d-318], [D-319][d-319]). The `baseline` keyword takes a [baseline](#g-baseline) (an
958: aircraft-shipped, full-coverage condition function). `trim!(sim, problem; …)`
959: gates on the simulation's lifecycle and commits through the simulation's
960: `init!`.
961: 
962: **The default backend is an in-house dense Levenberg–Marquardt**
963: ([D-070][d-070]). For decision dimensions ~10 with exact Jacobians, the core is
964: ~100 lines, namely a damping loop, a small linear solve and a convergence
965: test. That is the [§10.2][s10-2] stepper precedent exactly, a tiny needed core
966: against a heavy dependency. The per-residual physical tolerances sharpen the
967: case ([§14.7][s14-7]). They are a convergence test no external package spells
968: natively. That is precisely why the *service*, not the backend, applies it.
969: 
970: **The backend [seam](#g-seam) is a pinned signature**, value-passed, with one
971: required method per backend ([D-158][d-158]). That one method is the seam.
972: 
973: ```julia
974: solve(backend, eval!, d0, lower, upper, tol) -> (; d, status, n_evaluations, n_iterations)
975: ```
976: 
977: `eval!(r, J, d)` is in-place and always fills `r`, the residual vector packed
978: in `tolerances`' field order. It fills `J` iff `J !== nothing`. The Jacobian is
979: requested by argument, so a Jacobian-free backend simply always passes
980: `nothing`, and there is still exactly one evaluation method to implement.
981: 
982: `d0`, `lower` and `upper` are packed `Vector{Float64}` in `guess`'s field
983: order, with `±Inf` meaning unbounded. The declared side is canonical on both
984: ends, and the service has canonicalized before packing ([§14.7][s14-7]). A
985: backend that ignores bounds therefore ignores two vectors, not a missing
986: argument.
987: 
988: `tol` is a `Vector{Float64}` in `tolerances`' field order. It is data the
989: backend *may* stop on, under the service's per-backend-family translation
990: below, and it is decisive of nothing.
991: 
992: The return holds `d`, the solution, `status::Symbol`, and the diagnostic counts
993: `n_evaluations` and `n_iterations`. **The status set is deliberately open**,
994: and the status is recorded verbatim in the report because the verdict is the
995: service's ([D-158][d-158]). The name `solve` is subject to the exported-name
996: audit (`pending.md`) like every other API spelling. The backend sees vectors
997: and never names, so the solution it returns unpacks by the same order it was
998: given ([D-151][d-151]).
999: 
1000: #### The convergence verdict
1001: 
1002: **The convergence verdict is the service's, uniformly** ([D-150][d-150]).
1003: `converged` means `all(abs.(rᵢ) .≤ tolᵢ)`. That is the per-residual box test
1004: ([§14.7][s14-7]) in its own physical units, evaluated by the service at the
1005: backend's returned point. It is one residual evaluation, noise against the
1006: solve that produced it. That verdict, and nothing else, gates the commit and
1007: fills `TrimReport.converged`. The backend's returned `status` is recorded in
1008: the report as diagnostic data, and it is authoritative over nothing.
1009: 
1010: #### The tolerance translation
1011: 
1012: The tolerance translation is the service's too, per backend family
1013: ([D-150][d-150]). In least-squares backends the tolerances *are* the stopping
1014: criterion. They feed the per-residual test directly, so LM's damping loop tests
1015: exactly what the service will re-test.
1016: 
1017: The derivative-free fallback is designed and not yet built, and `pending.md`
1018: lists it ([D-070][d-070]). In the design it is `NLoptBackend(:LN_BOBYQA)` in a
1019: package extension. It would pass `nothing` for the Jacobian, keep Flight.jl's
1020: algorithm one keyword away, and leave the framework core carrying zero
1021: optimizer dependencies. For that fallback the service would square *and
1022: normalize*. The objective minimized would be $\sum_i (r_i/\mathit{tol}_i)^2$
1023: with `stopval = 1`. That objective is dimensionless where the threshold
1024: in Flight.jl's Cessna 172 model (`c172.jl`) was hand-scaled and absolute ([§14.7][s14-7]). It is a well-scaled
1025: valley where a raw $\|r\|^2$ sums forces against moments.
1026: 
1027: The two criteria cannot disagree in the dangerous direction, because of this
1028: implication ([D-150][d-150]).
1029: 
1030: $$\sum_i (r_i/\mathit{tol}_i)^2 \le 1
1031: \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (r_i/\mathit{tol}_i)^2 \le 1 \;\text{ for every } i$$
1032: 
1033: The `stopval` sphere is therefore *inscribed* in the tolerance box, and a
1034: fallback stopping at `stopval` necessarily passes the service's box test. The
1035: converse disagreement, a backend stopping early and reporting an optimistic
1036: status, is caught by the re-check, which remains the single authority. What is
1037: *not* claimed is point identity. Different backends may land on different
1038: solutions, which is an algorithmic difference and a legitimate one. What is
1039: eliminated is per-backend meanings of `converged`.
1040: 
1041: #### Box bounds and saturated decisions
1042: 
1043: **Box bounds are honored by step projection** ([D-070][d-070]). A decision
1044: variable saturated *at the solution* is listed in the report's saturated list,
1045: as `(:elevator, :upper)`. That is the classic CG-limit diagnostic (CG is the
1046: center of gravity), which Flight.jl's trim leaves inferable only from mysterious residuals.
1047: 
1048: #### Scratch stores
1049: 
1050: Every `trim!` invocation instantiates a fresh working store set. Each half of
1051: it (below) is a scratch `Model` of its own, materialized from the deployment
1052: the service was called on ([D-317][d-317], [D-319][d-319]). The set holds the
1053: `x` backing, the `s` and `m` stores, the [root input](#g-root-input) and
1054: [signal tables](#g-signal-table), and the derivative [buffer](#g-buffer). The
1055: set is built from the [activation](#g-activation)'s *layout* (the build's typed
1056: products at a given scalar type). The layout is the reusable compiled
1057: artifact. The buffers are per-invocation and die with the call (stopped-sim
1058: allocation, [§9.8][s9-8]). Each set also calls every
1059: [workspace](#g-workspace) (component-declared mutable scratch, arriving as
1060: `ws`) allocator once, at the activation's scalar ([D-077][d-077]).
1061: 
1062: The `Dual` backend's buffers being un-aliasable by type is defense in depth,
1063: not the mechanism. A `Float64` backend, such as the designed NLopt fallback,
1064: would get equally fresh buffers. The invariant is backend-independent.
1065: **The simulation's authoritative stores have exactly one writer**, the commit
1066: through [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the initialization boundary, the
1067: ordinary macro-sequence with an empty integrate) ([D-070][d-070]).
1068: 
1069: Setup applies `override(baseline, condition(guess))`, the problem's
1070: [condition](#g-condition) at the guess layered over the baseline by
1071: [override](#g-override), to the scratch set once. That application's full
1072: coverage is *checked here*, as [§14.6][s14-6] requires ([D-149][d-149]).
1073: [Sweeps](#g-sweep) therefore see a complete world. Raw instantiation is sound
1074: exactly because of that check, since every root input is written before any
1075: read. An incomplete `baseline` is one declaration-ordered
1076: `UninitializedInputs` at setup rather than a whole solve against undefined
1077: [cells](#g-cell).
1078: 
1079: **The frozen cells are established, not probe-seeded** ([D-213][d-213]). At the
1080: seeded activation the discrete [tier](#g-tier) is frozen ([§9.4][s9-4]). Its
1081: stages never run there, so nothing at that activation can derive a discrete
1082: output cell from the authored `s`. Setup therefore instantiates the scratch set
1083: in two halves. A [nominal](#g-nominal) set (one at the `Float64` activation)
1084: takes the composite first, by the dynamic walk ([§14.4][s14-4]), and runs one
1085: establishment round. That round is boundary zero's sweep with every discrete
1086: output stage admitted, due or not ([D-205][d-205]), with no
1087: [projection](#g-projection), no [guards](#g-guard) and no `s_update`. The
1088: seeded set is then written by the specialized `apply!`. Its frozen cells are
1089: copied from the nominal set, `Float64` values that a continuous consumer reads
1090: as zero-partial constants ([§9.5][s9-5]). Every cell the iterations read is
1091: thus derived from the authored world. A frozen cell holds what the authored
1092: discrete state publishes, which makes "held at the operating point" literal.
1093: No scratch cell holds the [probe](#g-probe)'s synthesized values. That is the
1094: [§14.6][s14-6] barrier reaching the scratch world, as [D-205][d-205] made it
1095: reach the published one. The iterations are untouched. They are raw write →
1096: sweep → read cycles at the seeded activation, over the continuous chain and
1097: `x_deriv` alone ([§14.5][s14-5]). The zero-decision problem (below) is the
1098: nominal half alone ([D-213][d-213]). Its one evaluation is that establishment
1099: round, followed by one derivative evaluation for the reads.
1100: 
1101: The commit applies the same composite over the same `baseline`
1102: (`override(baseline, condition(d*))`, [§14.9][s14-9]), so its coverage is
1103: setup's. Commit's totality check is therefore structurally unfailable through
1104: the trim path, and it stands as the shared `init!` defense ([D-149][d-149]). A
1105: converged solve is always committable. `TrimReport` therefore carries no
1106: committed flag, and the no-throw doctrine needs no exception.
1107: 
1108: Iterations rewrite the composite's write-set via the compiled plan. The
1109: write-set is `override(baseline, condition(d))`, the same tree setup resolved,
1110: so a store both layers touch merges exactly as it did at setup. An LM
1111: evaluation is one Dual-seeded sweep yielding `r` from the value parts and `J`
1112: from the partials, together ([D-069][d-069]). No convergence means no commit.
1113: Non-convergence is the service's box test failing at the returned point,
1114: whatever status the backend attached to it ([D-150][d-150]). No commit means
1115: the sim is bit-for-bit untouched, including "never initialized". Flight.jl's
1116: warn-but-assign is structurally impossible.
1117: 
1118: The same structure covers an interrupt. Ctrl-C during a long solve unwinds an
1119: ordinary Julia call operating on per-invocation scratch stores. No commit has
1120: happened, and the simulation is bit-for-bit untouched, exactly as a
1121: non-converged solve leaves it. The services need no counterpart to the loop's
1122: boundary masking ([§12.4][s12-4]). An interrupt inside the commit's boundary
1123: zero moves the simulation to `built` and propagates raw ([§13.4][s13-4],
1124: [D-223][d-223]).
1125: 
1126: 
1127: #### The commit, in full
1128: 
1129: The committed solution is applied as an `init!` in every respect. It is
1130: `override(baseline, condition(d*))` through boundary zero, with the pre-write
1131: [root-input totality](#g-root-input-totality) check ([§14.6][s14-6]), the
1132: sequence ([§14.5][s14-5]) and guards at commit. That check requires the
1133: application to cover every root input.
1134: 
1135: The `t0` argument that anchors the grid, and its default, are the same for
1136: both init-service entry points ([§14.5][s14-5], [D-110][d-110]). The
1137: [recorders](#g-recorders) (the trace and the log jointly) restart exactly as
1138: [§12.6][s12-6] states for `init!` ([D-110][d-110]). The [trace](#g-trace)
1139: (the primary record of a session) and the log are new objects. Any
1140: [batches](#g-batch) still in [staging cells](#g-staging-cell) (where a
1141: device's pending write batch waits between drains) are cleared.
1142: 
1143: A fresh recording that starts at its own anchor is the natural shape of
1144: unattended mode. Trim is not offered at a point reached by flying. It takes
1145: an authored baseline. Fly-then-retrim is spelled as a `checkpoint` of the
1146: flight, then a trim baseline authored from what the
1147: [checkpoint](#g-checkpoint) (the `Model`'s state at a frame top as one value)
1148: shows ([D-273][d-273]).
1149: 
1150: #### The report
1151: 
1152: `trim!` returns a structured `TrimReport` ([D-070][d-070]). The sketch names
1153: its eleven fields in order.
1154: 
1155: ```julia
1156: # what trim! returns: a value to read, never an exception to catch
1157: struct TrimReport
1158:     converged            # the service's box test at the backend's returned point
1159:     solution             # the solution NamedTuple, guess-shaped
1160:     residuals            # the solved-point residuals the verdict is read off
1161:     tolerances           # the tolerances they come with
1162:     committed_residuals  # the same residuals, re-gathered after the commit
1163:     committed_checks     # the check function's return at the committed state
1164:     status               # the backend's returned status, diagnostic only
1165:     n_evaluations        # the backend's evaluation count
1166:     n_iterations         # the backend's iteration count
1167:     saturated            # the saturated-bounds list
1168:     fired_events         # the events the commit fired
1169: end
1170: ```
1171: 
1172: The list below takes the fields in turn.
1173: 
1174: - `converged` is the service's own box test at the returned point, never a
1175:   backend's opinion.
1176: - `solution` is the solution NamedTuple. It is guess-shaped, hence
1177:   warm-startable.
1178: - `residuals` are the solved-point residuals, and `tolerances` the
1179:   tolerances they come with ([§14.5][s14-5], [D-155][d-155]). They are the
1180:   very numbers the verdict is read off, gathered at the backend's returned
1181:   point.
1182: - `committed_residuals` are the committed-state residuals, the same
1183:   residuals re-gathered from the boundary-zero world after the commit.
1184: - `committed_checks` holds the committed-state checks, the check function's
1185:   return at the committed state. They are gathered beside the
1186:   committed-state residuals and empty when the problem declares none
1187:   ([§14.7][s14-7]).
1188: - `status` is the backend's returned status, and `n_evaluations` and
1189:   `n_iterations` are its evaluation and iteration counts. These are
1190:   diagnostic throughout, informative about *how* the solve went and decisive
1191:   about nothing.
1192: - `saturated` is the saturated-bounds list.
1193: - `fired_events` holds the commit's fired events, as component paths and
1194:   event names. The list is empty when boundary zero ran quiet
1195:   ([§14.5][s14-5], [D-126][d-126]).
1196: 
1197: The committed-state residuals are nearly free ([D-155][d-155]). Boundary
1198: zero's sweep has already run, so the residuals' declared reads need one
1199: derivative evaluation and a gather from it. There is no offset caveat to
1200: carry. Every output stage publishes at boundary zero ([D-205][d-205],
1201: [§14.5][s14-5]). A residual reading the [port](#g-port) (one declared name,
1202: one cell) of an offset [component](#g-component) therefore reads a
1203: commit-refreshed cell like any other. Those committed-state residuals are the
1204: numbers describing the state the simulation is actually *in*, which is the
1205: point a `linearize` at its default operating point reads.
1206: 
1207: A non-empty fired-event set also raises `TrimCommitEvents` ([Appendix C][sC],
1208: [D-126][d-126]). The committed stores then sit at the post-handler point, not
1209: the reported solution, and a `linearize` at its default operating point
1210: ([§14.10][s14-10]) reads that point.
1211: 
1212: The two residual sets are what make the moved point auditable. **A converged
1213: solve whose committed-state residuals violate the box test raises
1214: `TrimCommitResiduals`**, naming the offending residuals with their committed
1215: values and tolerances ([Appendix C][sC], [D-155][d-155]). The move, whether
1216: `x_projection` or a commit-fired handler ([§14.5][s14-5]), is surfaced rather
1217: than left silent. The verdict itself is not revisited. It gated the commit,
1218: at the solved point, and the numbers ([D-150][d-150]) stand as reported.
1219: 
1220: The committed-state checks audit the point rather than the move. **A
1221: converged solve whose committed-state checks leave their tolerances raises
1222: `TrimCommitChecks`**, naming the offending checks with their committed values
1223: and tolerances ([Appendix C][sC], [D-262][d-262]). The equilibrium is real.
1224: The operating point is not the one the problem asked for, which is what a
1225: params-vs-world atmosphere mismatch produces under elimination
1226: ([§14.7][s14-7], [D-139][d-139]). The checks are gathered from the same
1227: boundary-zero sweep, so they cost no evaluation of their own. No commit means
1228: no checks, exactly as for the committed-state residuals.
1229: 
1230: **Non-convergence never throws** ([D-070][d-070]). It is an expected
1231: *outcome*, not broken machinery ([§13.5][s13-5]). In an envelope of trims,
1232: hitting the infeasible edge is information.
1233: 
1234: A malformed problem is a different case. It fails at setup with a
1235: `DiagnosticError` carrying `TrimProblemInvalid` ([Appendix C][sC]). The
1236: malformed cases are these.
1237: 
1238: - A field that is not a NamedTuple, or a `reads` that is no read set.
1239: - A guess/bounds key-set or field-type disagreement.
1240: - A box with `lower` above `upper`.
1241: - A tolerance or check tolerance that is not finite and positive.
1242: - A `tolerances`/residual key-set mismatch observed at the setup guess
1243:   evaluation, and a `check_tolerances`/check key-set mismatch observed at the
1244:   same evaluation.
1245: - A residual or check return whose fields are not real scalars, observed at
1246:   that evaluation too.
1247: 
1248: The error carries the offending field with the names or types in hand,
1249: collected. The read set's own refusals ride beside it, `ReadResolution` and
1250: the path walk's `PathResolution` ([§14.4][s14-4], [D-406][d-406]). An unknown
1251: `reads` [selector](#g-selector) (one of the closed family of deferred reads)
1252: is refused that way. A permuted spelling is none of these ([§14.7][s14-7]).
1253: 
1254: A throw inside the commit's boundary zero is a third case, and neither of
1255: those. It is model code failing. **The throw propagates out of `trim!` as the
1256: commit's `StepError`**, and no report is returned for that solve
1257: ([D-224][d-224]). The simulation is left `built` ([§13.4][s13-4],
1258: [D-223][d-223]).
1259: 
1260: An *empty* problem is none of them either. **`TrimProblem(guess = (;), …)` is
1261: legal**, not `TrimProblemInvalid` ([D-156][d-156]). With zero decision
1262: variables the solver is bypassed outright. There is nothing to pack, no
1263: seeded activation and no backend call. The service simply evaluates the
1264: residuals once at `override(baseline, condition((;)))`, the baseline with
1265: whatever the problem's condition sets. The ordinary box test decides
1266: `converged`, and the commit runs as usual. The degenerate problem is the "is
1267: this operating point an equilibrium?" probe. It evaluates this condition's
1268: equations and reports, which is useful in its own right and free.
1269: 
1270: #### The AD obligation
1271: 
1272: **The default formulation requires `Dual` genericity of exactly the
1273: continuous output-stage chains and `x_deriv`**, plus the user's assignment
1274: and residual math ([D-070][d-070]). The discrete tier's stages and
1275: `s_update`, and the event system's guards and handlers, never see a `Dual`.
1276: They are frozen constants with zero partials, semantically exact
1277: ([§8.2][s8-2]).
1278: 
1279: This is *not a new obligation*. It is the same activation linearization is
1280: defined on. AD-readiness is also a build-checked property. A
1281: [pinned](#g-walked) intermediate (a value held at `Float64` inside authored
1282: continuous math) makes the Dual probe fail with a culprit-naming
1283: `MethodError` or `InexactError` ([D-307][d-307]). `build(world; activations)`
1284: puts the probe in CI ([D-280][d-280]). The robustness comes from enforcement,
1285: not hope.
1286: 
1287: A migration audit of Flight.jl's Cessna 172 model (`c172.jl`) against this
1288: obligation took one afternoon. Its findings on tables and saturations follow
1289: ([D-070][d-070]), and its two Flight.jl-only findings, on the landing gear and the
1290: `norm`-at-zero guards, are in the `migration_outline.md` companion.
1291: 
1292: - `Interpolations.jl` tables (propeller coefficient maps, engine maps) must
1293:   accept generic scalars. They do. A call `itp(x::Dual)` works through the
1294:   `BSpline`/`scale`/`extrapolate` compositions in Flight.jl's tables. Prefer
1295:   cubic knots over linear where partials matter, though, since linear knots
1296:   make Jacobian entries piecewise-constant. That is no regression against
1297:   finite differences ([D-307][d-307]). A manual chain rule via
1298:   `Interpolations.gradient` is the escape hatch for anything exotic, and the
1299:   pattern for wrapping non-Julia black boxes ([§14.10][s14-10],
1300:   [D-266][d-266]).
1301: - In-model saturations (actuator limits, idle/FRC clamps) zero Jacobian
1302:   columns when active. FRC is the friction constraint compensator of
1303:   FlightPhysics' piston engine (`piston.jl`). LM damping tolerates the rank
1304:   deficiency, and the report names the saturated variable ([D-070][d-070]).
1305:   Cruise trim leaves those saturations inactive.
1306: 
1307: The derivative-free fallback would be chosen per call, through the one
1308: `backend =` keyword.
1309: 
1310: ### 14.9 Mounting: problems as relocatable values
1311: 
1312: This section treats a [`TrimProblem`](#g-trimproblem) (the closed nine-field
1313: value a trim solves) as a relocatable value. [Mounting](#g-mounting)
1314: (relocating a whole problem or tap set to a prefix) is the work of
1315: [`at`](#g-at), the scoping combinator. The section says what a problem is,
1316: then how `at` lifts one, how a mounted read set and mounted input entries
1317: resolve, why a problem never authors the environment, and how worlds, thin
1318: rigs and swarms use the lift.
1319: 
1320: **A `TrimProblem` is not a [condition](#g-condition) but an implicitly
1321: specified condition** ([D-071][d-071]). A condition is the datum that says
1322: "set this build to this state". `condition` is a condition-*valued function*
1323: over the decision space. `reads`/`residuals` are the equations that pin the
1324: free variables down. `guess`, `lower` and `upper` say where to search.
1325: Solving makes the implicit condition explicit. The commit is then literally
1326: an init, `override(baseline, condition(d*))` through
1327: [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the initialization boundary, the ordinary
1328: macro-sequence with an empty integrate). [`override`](#g-override) is the
1329: ordered, asymmetric layering combinator, in which the patch wins on a shared
1330: leaf. The services unify as clients of one
1331: condition algebra. `init!` applies an explicit condition, and `trim!`
1332: searches a family for the member satisfying its equations.
1333: 
1334: **`at` lifts a problem field by field**, and each field falls in one of three
1335: cases ([D-071][d-071], [D-277][d-277]). The `condition` field is
1336: condition-producing (path-relative), so the lift post-composes it. The
1337: `reads` field is a read set, and the lift prepends the prefix to the set's
1338: mount chain (the prefixes it is mounted under, root first). Every other field
1339: is path-free and passes through. The rule that residual math sees only the
1340: gathered NamedTuple ([§14.7][s14-7]) is what makes the residuals path-free.
1341: 
1342: ```julia
1343: at(prefix::String, p::TrimProblem) = TrimProblem(
1344:     guess      = p.guess,                      #path-free: pass through
1345:     lower      = p.lower,
1346:     upper      = p.upper,
1347:     condition  = d -> at(prefix, p.condition(d)),  #post-compose: wrap each returned tree
1348:     reads      = at(prefix, p.reads),              #inert selector data: the prefix joins the read set's mount chain
1349:     residuals  = p.residuals,                      #path-free: pass through
1350:     tolerances = p.tolerances,
1351:     checks     = p.checks,
1352:     check_tolerances = p.check_tolerances)
1353: ```
1354: 
1355: The condition side needs nothing new at resolution. The flattening
1356: accumulator of [§14.3][s14-3] enters each `Scoped` wrapper and prefixes every
1357: condition entry below it.
1358: 
1359: **A read set carries its mount chain as data** ([D-277][d-277]).
1360: `at(prefix, read_set)` prepends the prefix to that chain and joins nothing. **A
1361: `Reads` value is no condition node**, so `Scoped` never wraps one
1362: ([D-277][d-277]). **Resolution
1363: walks the chain from the root**, each prefix from the level the previous one
1364: reached ([§13.3][s13-3], [D-277][d-277]). It then rebases every
1365: [selector](#g-selector) (one of the closed family of deferred reads) to the
1366: root before the selector resolves. A path selector's path is joined to the
1367: mount (`"vehicle/dynamics"` → `"wing/vehicle/dynamics"`). `get_input` names
1368: an input [face](#g-face) (the name a port wears on its
1369: [component](#g-component)'s boundary) of the mount level. It follows the
1370: export chain to the [root input](#g-root-input) it lands on (the root
1371: component's own input face). `get_face` names an output face of the mount
1372: level and reads its producer's port ([D-277][d-277]).
1373: 
1374: **A refusal spells the selector as authored and names the mount**
1375: ([D-277][d-277]). `at` refuses an empty prefix on a read set, as it does on a
1376: condition node ([§14.2][s14-2]), and raises `ReadSetMisuse`. A problem's lift
1377: and a tap set's lift reach that refusal through `at` on their read sets
1378: ([D-278][d-278]).
1379: 
1380: **Input entries authored in the aircraft's face vocabulary resolve through the
1381: export chain from the mount point** ([D-071][d-071]). For a wingman aircraft
1382: mounted at `"wing"`, the input entry `throttle` resolves to root input
1383: `"wing.throttle"`. An unexported face fails resolution by name, and correctly so
1384: ([§14.2][s14-2]). An internally wired input (a [scenario
1385: component](#g-scenario-component) driving the wingman's throttle) is untrimmable
1386: from outside. Resolution refuses it by name, with the producer that drives it in
1387: hand, as `ConditionResolution` for a condition entry and as `ReadResolution` for
1388: a `get_input`. The service compiles the scoped condition and reads, and runs the
1389: identical loop. It never knows where its paths are mounted.
1390: 
1391: **A problem never authors the environment** ([D-139][d-139]). The environment is
1392: the business of the world and of the [`baseline`](#g-baseline) (the
1393: full-coverage condition `trim!` takes as a keyword). In a full world it is a
1394: sibling component's root inputs. In a thin rig it is a root input holding an
1395: isbits [handle](#g-field-handle) (an immutable query object carried on an
1396: ordinary port). In a thin rig it can also be a [constant
1397: source](#g-constant-source) (a library component publishing a value its instance
1398: holds) holding a bulk-data handle ([D-364][d-364]). The problem *receives* its
1399: handles through the user parameter record ([§14.7][s14-7]), and only queries
1400: them.
1401: 
1402: The reason is the resolution rule stated above. A condition entry naming an
1403: internally wired input fails by name, correctly. A problem writing an
1404: environment face would therefore be applicable only to those rigs where that
1405: face happens to be unconnected. The relocatability this section exists to
1406: guarantee would be lost.
1407: 
1408: **The world wrapper dissolves** ([D-071][d-071]). Flight.jl's
1409: `f_init!(::Model{<:SimpleWorld})` (initialize environment, then call the
1410: aircraft's trim) has no successor method. The environment, the other aircraft
1411: and all root inputs are covered by the `baseline` condition ([§14.6][s14-6]),
1412: applied once at setup. The commit is
1413: `override(baseline, at(mount, condition(d*)))`. Method nesting became value
1414: layering.
1415: 
1416: "Aircraft as root" is a thin world. **Design tasks use a shipped rig,
1417: [`design_world(ac)`](#g-design_world)** ([D-071][d-071]). They need one because
1418: by default the aircraft is not literally the root. Its environment inputs are
1419: function-valued signals ([§4.4][s4-4]), wired from provider components. The rig
1420: is `design_world(ac)` = aircraft + `SimpleAtmosphere(wind = NoWind())` +
1421: `HorizontalTerrain`, where `SimpleAtmosphere` and `NoWind` are FlightPhysics'
1422: atmosphere types. `design_world` promotes the ad-hoc atmosphere and terrain
1423: FlightCore `Model`s inside Flight.jl's `linearize` to a named artifact. There is
1424: one mechanism. The "root" case is the shallowest world, and the trim problem
1425: mounts at `"aircraft"` like anywhere else.
1426: 
1427: Leaving an environment face *unconnected* is legal by construction, though, for
1428: an isbits handle ([D-364][d-364]). The face becomes an ordinary root input
1429: holding the handle *value*, written by the `baseline` like any other root input.
1430: That is the test-rig idiom. It is the function-valued sibling of a constant
1431: source, with zero ceremony for a frozen environment. **A handle that references
1432: bulk data is never a root input**, and the rig wires a constant source holding
1433: it ([D-364][d-364]). Root inputs are traced and checkpointed by value, and
1434: [§4.4][s4-4] states why such a handle cannot be one ([D-237][d-237]). For design
1435: tasks the shipped rig stays `design_world(ac)`. That keeps the environment's
1436: tunables in the root-input vocabulary that conditions, linearization's input
1437: surface and the [trace header](#g-trace-header) (the trace's fixed preamble)
1438: already speak.
1439: 
1440: The swarm doctrine covers trims of several aircraft. **The service solves
1441: one problem at a time** ([D-071][d-071]). Sequential independent trims (trim
1442: lead, commit, trim wing against the committed world) cover weak or one-way
1443: coupling. A joint trim is user-side value composition. Concatenate decision
1444: NamedTuples under prefixed names, combine the scoped condition trees, and
1445: stack the residuals. If joint trims become routine, **a
1446: `product(p₁ => "lead", p₂ => "wing")` helper is the extension recorded for
1447: them** ([D-071][d-071]). That helper is
1448: [recorded, not built](#g-recorded-not-built) (a worked-out extension
1449: deliberately left unimplemented, its seams named).
1450: 
1451: ### 14.10 Linearization: tap selectors, one seeded pass, a pure query
1452: 
1453: `linearize` computes the matrices `A`, `B`, `C` and `D` about an operating
1454: point in one seeded evaluation, and it commits nothing. What it seeds and
1455: reads is a [tap set](#g-taps) (three labeled lists declaring what
1456: linearization seeds and reports). This section covers, in order, the tap set,
1457: the operating point, the seeded pass, the leaves the pass seeds and the leaves
1458: it holds frozen, the returned value, the guidance on coordinates, the recorded
1459: sampled-data extension, and declared non-participation with the local
1460: derivative rule.
1461: 
1462: #### The tap set
1463: 
1464: Flight.jl's per-aircraft `XStateSpace`/`UStateSpace`/`YStateSpace` structs,
1465: plus the `get_*_ss`/`assign_*_ss!` shuttle methods, run to ~150 lines of
1466: bookkeeping per variant. All of it becomes three [selector](#g-selector)
1467: lists (the closed family of deferred reads resolving against a source).
1468: **The three lists are declared together as one tap set,
1469: `taps(x = …, u = …, y = …)`** ([D-072][d-072], [D-272][d-272]). Membership is
1470: closed per list. The `x` list takes `get_state`, the `u` list `get_input`,
1471: and the `y` list `get_output` and `get_face` ([§14.4][s14-4]). The NamedTuple
1472: key is the label control design slices by.
1473: 
1474: **A tap names one scalar** ([D-272][d-272]). Its
1475: [leaf address](#g-leaf-address) (the field name with dotted and bracketed
1476: steps below it) resolves to one scalar leaf ([§14.4][s14-4],
1477: [D-276][d-276]). So a vector leaf yields *named scalars*, one tap per
1478: element. A seeded tap, in the `x` or `u` list, reaches its scalar through one
1479: index step at most and never a `.name` step, because a seed is written into a
1480: whole state field or root input, or into one array element of it. A seeded
1481: tap's leaf is exactly `Float64`. A `y` tap is a read and takes any leaf
1482: address. A tap resolving to a vector or a struct
1483: is rejected at resolution as a `TapResolution`, and so is a member in the
1484: wrong list. A second tap resolving to a site already seeded is rejected
1485: naming the earlier label, since one site is one column and a second seed
1486: there would overwrite the first ([D-272][d-272]).
1487: 
1488: ```julia
1489: taps(x = (p = get_state("vehicle/dynamics", "ω_eb_b[1]"),
1490:           θ = get_state("vehicle/kinematics", :θ_nb), …),
1491:      u = (throttle_cmd = get_input(:throttle),
1492:           wind_n = get_input("wind[1]"), …),
1493:      y = (EAS = get_output("vehicle/airflow", :EAS), …))
1494: ```
1495: 
1496: The three lists are validated at resolution, with
1497: [did-you-mean](#g-did-you-mean) errors (the offending name plus the
1498: list-in-hand it should have matched). Those errors are the selectors' own
1499: `ReadResolution` ([D-406][d-406]). The `x` list is validated against the
1500: `x_init` [stores](#g-store) of the continuous [tier](#g-tier) (the continuous
1501: or discrete side of the hybrid formalism), the `u` list against
1502: [faces](#g-face), and the `y` list against `y_types`. **An `x` tap naming a
1503: discrete store is rejected at resolution** as a `TapResolution`, with the tap
1504: and its tier in hand ([D-197][d-197]). That is the no-silent-zeros rule again
1505: ([D-197][d-197]). The frozen tier's only possible partials are zeros, and the
1506: rejection's next-move guidance points at the recorded step-map extension
1507: below. The lists compile to offsets once, and relocate whole via
1508: `at(prefix, taps)` ([D-072][d-072]). The shuttle layer's successor is that
1509: compiled writer/reader pair, and **no hand-written state-space mapping
1510: remains** ([§7.1][s7-1], [D-072][d-072]).
1511: 
1512: #### The operating point
1513: 
1514: **Linearization is a pure query** ([D-072][d-072]). It commits nothing and
1515: runs no [boundary zero](#g-boundary-zero) (the initialization boundary, the
1516: ordinary macro-sequence with an empty integrate). It works only on
1517: [scratch](#g-scratch) buffers (mutable working storage whose contents are
1518: never authoritative), and nothing it computes becomes authoritative.
1519: Flight.jl's restore-the-trim dance, the re-`assign!` after the `FiniteDiff`
1520: package dirtied the model, has no successor.
1521: 
1522: **The default operating point is the [checkpoint](#g-checkpoint)** (the
1523: model's state at a frame top as one value) of the simulation or the model as
1524: it stands ([D-274][d-274], [D-319][d-319]). It is `checkpoint(sim)` on a
1525: simulation and `checkpoint(model)` on a [`Model`](#g-model) (a deployment
1526: materialized at one scalar type), each restored into the scratch world with
1527: no boundary zero ([§12.6][s12-6], [D-274][d-274], [D-319][d-319]). The frozen
1528: tier's [cells](#g-cell) (the typed entries of the signal table) are the
1529: checkpoint's held cells. After a `trim!` commit, `linearize(sim, taps)` or
1530: `linearize(model, taps)` is about the trim point with nothing re-specified.
1531: **`about = <condition>`, with `t0` beside it, linearizes anywhere else
1532: without touching the sim** ([D-272][d-272]). `t0` is admitted only there,
1533: and a `t0` passed without `about` is an `ArgumentInvalid`.
1534: 
1535: #### The seeded pass
1536: 
1537: Each invocation instantiates its own scratch store set, the trim service's
1538: mechanism verbatim ([§14.8][s14-8]), and establishes the operating point in it
1539: (above). **It then runs the seeded `Dual` evaluation**, one direction per `x`
1540: tap and per `u` tap, in groups of `width` directions per pass
1541: ([D-072][d-072]). **`width` is a keyword of `linearize`, default 8**, and the
1542: default width's scalar type is public, so a build can pre-materialize its
1543: [activation](#g-activation) (the build's typed products at a given scalar
1544: type) through `activations` ([§9.4][s9-4]) and a tap set of any size then
1545: linearizes with no compile at the keyboard ([D-272][d-272]). Value parts give
1546: `ẋ₀` and `y₀`. Partials give `A` and `B` against the `x`- and `u`-seeds, and
1547: `C` and `D` against the same seeds read at `y`. All four come out
1548: simultaneously, exact to machine precision. `linearization_walkthrough.md`
1549: runs the pass on a two-state model, seed by seed.
1550: 
1551: ```
1552:   x-taps ─┐                                         ┌─ value parts → ẋ₀, y₀
1553:           ├─ seed directions → one Dual evaluation ─┤
1554:   u-taps ─┘          (chunked internally)           └─ partials    → A, B, C, D
1555: ```
1556: 
1557: That seeded evaluation replaces Flight.jl's four `FiniteDiff` jacobians, their
1558: step-size heuristics and ~4n perturbed evaluations.
1559: 
1560: #### Seeded and frozen leaves
1561: 
1562: **Unseeded states sit constant at the operating point**, and so do unseeded
1563: [root inputs](#g-root-input) (the root's own input faces) ([D-072][d-072]).
1564: Establishing the operating point embeds their `Float64` values as
1565: zero-partial constants. The discrete tier is frozen with zero partials, which
1566: is precisely "linearize with the discrete state held" ([§8.2][s8-2]).
1567: Partials enter through per-invocation seeding, one rule for `x` and root
1568: inputs alike ([§7.2][s7-2], [D-297][d-297]). [§8.2][s8-2] states which root
1569: inputs the schema leaves seedable ([D-295][d-295]). A root input whose input
1570: entry is declared `Pinned` is declaredly unseedable, and its cell is frozen at
1571: every activation.
1572: 
1573: **Selecting such a root input as a `B`-matrix tap is rejected at tap
1574: resolution** as a `TapResolution`, with the offending input entry in hand,
1575: rather than silently yielding a zero column ([D-295][d-295],
1576: [D-296][d-296]). Under fan-out the rejection names the pinning consumer, not
1577: the face alone. A root input is unseedable whenever any one of its consumers
1578: demands frozen, which is the fan-out meet ([§8.2][s8-2]; [D-168][d-168]). The
1579: author's next move is to promote that leaf to a tolerant input entry, or to
1580: route the tap around it. Either move depends on knowing which leaf froze the
1581: root input. A discrete consumer pins its input entry by tier, not by
1582: declaration ([§8.2][s8-2]), so a root input it reads is unseedable at every
1583: activation with nothing to promote. The rejection then names the tier, and
1584: the next move is to tap the continuous cell that consumer drives, or the
1585: recorded step map below ([D-272][d-272]).
1586: 
1587: The table sets seeded and frozen leaves side by side.
1588: 
1589: | leaf | in the one pass | what fixes it |
1590: |---|---|---|
1591: | a state leaf named by an `x` tap | seeded, one direction | per-invocation seeding |
1592: | any other state leaf | sits constant at the operating point | per-invocation seeding |
1593: | a root input named by a `u` tap | seeded, one direction | per-invocation seeding |
1594: | any other seedable root input | constant, its `Float64` value embedded as a zero-partial constant | per-invocation seeding |
1595: | a root input whose entry is declared `Pinned` | frozen at every activation, and rejected as a `B`-matrix tap | the schema: a declaration |
1596: | a root input a discrete consumer reads | frozen at every activation, and rejected as a `B`-matrix tap | the tier |
1597: | any discrete-tier leaf | frozen, zero partials | the tier |
1598: 
1599: #### The returned value
1600: 
1601: **`linearize` returns a `Linearization` value** ([D-072][d-072],
1602: [D-272][d-272]). It holds `ẋ₀`, `x₀`, `u₀` and `y₀` as NamedTuples under the
1603: tap labels, `A`, `B`, `C` and `D` as matrices, and the three label sets of the
1604: taps ([D-272][d-272]). On that data, FlightPhysics' `subsystem`/`delete_vars`
1605: survive as pure label-indexed matrix slicing, with no model involvement.
1606: Flight.jl's `c172x_ctl` LQR pipeline consumes it with cosmetic changes.
1607: **FlightPhysics' `LinearizedSS` the [*component*](#g-component) survives separately, as an
1608: ordinary [continuous component](#g-continuous-component)** in the migrated
1609: library ([D-072][d-072]). Its `x_init` is the state vector, its faces are
1610: labeled, and the affine update lives in `y_direct`/`x_deriv`. It has no
1611: privileges, and its schema is everyone else's.
1612: 
1613: #### Coordinate guidance
1614: 
1615: The recorded guidance is that linearization taps should select
1616: minimal-coordinate mechanizations ([D-072][d-072]). Perturbing Euler-angle
1617: states is meaningful where seeding quaternion components steps off the
1618: unit-norm manifold. This is why Flight.jl's C172X design code linearizes the
1619: aircraft's `{NED}` variant, the one built on NED kinematics.
1620: [`design_world(ac)`](#g-design_world) (the thin world that mounts an aircraft
1621: for trim and linearization) rigs that variant, promoting implicit
1622: practice to stated rule. The coordinate choice belongs to the tap author, not
1623: the framework.
1624: 
1625: #### The sampled-data extension
1626: 
1627: **The sampled-data `Dual` activation is
1628: [recorded, not built](#g-recorded-not-built)**, a worked-out extension
1629: deliberately left unimplemented with its seams named ([D-073][d-073]). The
1630: frozen-exact doctrine is consumer-scoped, not a capability wall. Trim and
1631: linearization differentiate the continuous dynamics with the discrete state
1632: held, and for that a frozen discrete output (a ZOH constant with zero
1633: partials) is the exact answer, as [§7.2][s7-2] states ([D-072][d-072]). The frozen discrete cell is not an AD
1634: limitation on the signal path. It is the true zero of an instantaneous
1635: dependence that the hybrid semantics never had. The signal flow through a
1636: discrete component is temporal, not instantaneous, and AD
1637: follows the actual data movement. `frozen_discrete_walkthrough.md` works the
1638: three-component chain through.
1639: 
1640: Differentiating "through" the discrete side means differentiating a
1641: *different object*. That object is the sampled-data step map
1642: $\Phi : ((x_k, s_k), \mathrm{inputs}) \to (x_{k+1}, s_{k+1})$, taken over the
1643: model's *whole* state, both letters at once. One evaluation of $\Phi$
1644: integrates one period, then runs the [due](#g-due) [ticks](#g-tick). The
1645: extension is additive along existing [seams](#g-seam).
1646: 
1647: - [Walked](#g-walked)-leaf parametrization of the discrete tier's real-scalar
1648:   state leaves. Counters and enums stay pinned, like `m`.
1649: - Opt-in participation on discrete components, with frozen-exact staying the
1650:   default. A participating component opts in through an explicit trait, and
1651:   that trait turns the walk on for its [contracts](#g-contract) (its declared
1652:   interface). Its `Float64` leaves follow the scalar and `Pinned` keeps its
1653:   meaning, exactly as on the continuous tier ([§8.2][s8-2]). Participation
1654:   therefore stays authored per leaf on that tier too, and no declaration
1655:   changes shape, which gives graceful migration with no flag day.
1656: - One new activation, "continuous chain + `x_deriv` + the discrete tier's
1657:   output stages + `s_update`" ([§9.4][s9-4]).
1658: - Forward sensitivities through the in-house RK steppers, for free. That is a
1659:   payoff of owning the loop ([§10.1][s10-1]).
1660: - **A distinct `s`-tap list sits beside the `x` list**, labeling the step
1661:   map's state blocks $\partial(x^+, s^+)/\partial(x, s)$ ([D-197][d-197]). The
1662:   `x` list keeps its continuous meaning unchanged.
1663: 
1664: The honest limit is that $\Phi$ is differentiable only where the event
1665: pattern is locally constant. Exactness across a firing needs saltation
1666: corrections. The scope is therefore event-quiescent operating points, which
1667: trim points already are, because [guards](#g-guard) at commit see to that
1668: ([§14.5][s14-5]). The scope comes with a loud diagnostic if an event fires
1669: inside a differentiated step. Two consumers wait. The first is the closed-loop
1670: trim extension ([§14.7][s14-7]), whose `s_update(s) - s = 0` residuals would
1671: currently need the derivative-free fallback, since a frozen `s_update` has no
1672: Jacobian columns. The second is exact discrete-time linearization of the full
1673: loop, which is digital design on the exact discretized plant instead of
1674: continuous linearization plus Tustin.
1675: 
1676: #### Declared non-participation
1677: 
1678: Both halves of non-participation have a spelling ([D-263][d-263]). The output
1679: half is the freeze door, which declares a leaf frozen under differentiation as
1680: [§8.2][s8-2] and [§9.5][s9-5] spell it ([D-286][d-286]). An opaque wrapper (an FMU, a C aerodynamic table)
1681: and a deliberately severed coupling can therefore both say so in the schema,
1682: instead of showing up in Jacobians as unexplained zero rows. The conformance
1683: check holds them to it at every activation ([D-286][d-286]). The input half
1684: is the FFI door ([§8.2][s8-2]). A consumer's `Pinned` input entry declares
1685: "never hand me partials", enforced at the wire ([§6.1][s6-1],
1686: [D-263][d-263]). At a root input, such an input entry *is* the forbid-seeding
1687: marker itself. That marker carries semantics rather than mere protection,
1688: because it types the root-input cell at every activation. An unseeded root
1689: input is therefore a *choice*, where a `Pinned`-entry root input is a
1690: *declaration* ([D-295][d-295]).
1691: 
1692: An implementation that cannot take `Dual`s has a third move. The marker
1693: records that a leaf carries no partials, not that an implementation cannot
1694: propagate them. **A component that must participate keeps its input entry
1695: tolerant and supplies its own local derivative inside the stage**
1696: ([D-266][d-266]). A C table or an FMU whose coupling matters to the Jacobian
1697: is such a component. It evaluates at the value, obtains a local Jacobian by
1698: whatever means it has, a finite difference at a step its own grid suggests or
1699: the table's analytic slope, and chains it onto the incoming partials
1700: ([D-266][d-266]).
1701: 
1702: ```julia
1703: lookup(tb, u::Float64) = c_lookup(tb, u)                      # nominal: straight to the table
1704: 
1705: function lookup(tb, u::ForwardDiff.Dual{Tag}) where {Tag}      # Dual: the local rule
1706:     u0 = ForwardDiff.value(u)
1707:     y0 = c_lookup(tb, u0)
1708:     h  = sqrt(eps(u0))                                         # or a step the table's grid suggests
1709:     dy = (c_lookup(tb, u0 + h) - y0) / h
1710:     return ForwardDiff.Dual{Tag}(y0, dy * ForwardDiff.partials(u))
1711: end
1712: ```
1713: 
1714: The stripping happens mid-expression, which [§9.5][s9-5] leaves legal at an
1715: unpinned leaf, and the seeded pass sees the component as an ordinary
1716: differentiable one. The derivative is finite-difference in quality on this
1717: one component and exact everywhere else. **Linearization keeps this one
1718: mechanism and no perturbation-based fallback** ([D-266][d-266]). The
1719: derivative-free trim fallback ([§14.8][s14-8]) would serve problems with
1720: no Jacobian columns, a case linearization does not have.
1721: `handle_walk_walkthrough.md` places the rule beside the `Freeze` block
1722: ([§13.7][s13-7]) and the pinned input entry.
1723: 
1724: What stays recorded is only the remaining tooling over that visibility. One
1725: piece is pinned-face validation by the tap declaration, where selecting a
1726: declared-frozen output is a warning. The other is a
1727: [feedthrough](#g-feedthrough)-graph lint, where a frozen output fed by
1728: participating inputs names the severed coupling. Both are additive when a
1729: consumer shows up, with no flag day. Until then the declared pins and the
1730: visible zero rows suffice.
