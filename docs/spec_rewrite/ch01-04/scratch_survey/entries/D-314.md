13227	### D-314 — Rename `inner_wires` to `local_wires`
13228	
13229	**Status.** ratified
13230	
13231	**Position.** The class marker is `local_wires`, and the child-to-child kind
13232	of wire is a "local wire" in prose. `input_wires` and `output_wires` stand.
13233	Signatures, entry spellings and semantics are unchanged, and `Group`'s field
13234	and keyword follow the declaration. No aliases and no deprecation shims, on
13235	[D-220][d-220]'s terms.
13236	
13237	**Spec.** [§3.3][s3-3], [§6.1][s6-1], [§8.2][s8-2], [§8.5][s8-5], [§8.6][s8-6], [§8.8][s8-8], [§11.7][s11-7], [§14.2][s14-2], [Appendix B][sB],
13238	[Appendix C][sC], [Appendix D][sD]
13239	
13240	**Rationale.** [D-303][d-303] accepted the `inner_`/`input_` near-collision as a
13241	reading nuisance. The nuisance is in the common case: every assembly writes
13242	the two declarations on adjacent lines, and `inner` and `input` share their
13243	first two letters and their length, so the eye tells them apart by the
13244	third letter alone. `local` keeps the length, so the trio still aligns, and
13245	shares no letter run with `input`.
13246	
13247	`local` also names the distinction more exactly than `inner`. A local wire
13248	has both ends inside the assembly's boundary, and an input or output wire
13249	has one end on it. "Local" says "confined within, crossing no boundary",
13250	which is the contrast; "inner" says it only by opposition to an "outer"
13251	that nothing is called. [D-303][d-303] rejected `wires_local` because `local` names
13252	a scope beside two directions, but the three words sort the wires by one
13253	question, whether the wire crosses the boundary and in which direction, and
13254	a scope word is the right answer to its first half.
13255	
13256	`local` is a Julia keyword, and `local_wires` is an ordinary identifier the
13257	parser never confuses with it. `implementation.md`'s naming rules use
13258	"a local" for a local variable, and "local wire" is always two words, so the
13259	two senses never meet in one phrase.
13260	
13261	The rename does not change what kind of thing the three declarations hold.
13262	[D-303][d-303]'s rule that "wire" covers an entry of any of the three stands, and
13263	the [§8.6][s8-6] rule keeps its three verbs. That a boundary entry aliases a face
13264	rather than creating an endpoint is a mechanism the spec states where
13265	faces are defined, not a difference the names expose.
13266	
13267	**Rejected.**
13268	- *Keeping `inner_wires`:* the near-collision sits in every assembly's most
13269	  common two lines, and the build-time failure [D-303][d-303] relied on catches a
13270	  misfiled entry, not a misread one.
13271	- *`wires` alone:* the strongest contrast, and the inner set is the
13272	  assembly's wires proper. But `wires` is a local in `build.jl`'s cluster
13273	  code, which the naming rules forbid beside a package function of that
13274	  name, and the bare noun contradicts [D-303][d-303]'s rule that an entry of any of
13275	  the three is a wire.
13276	- *`peer_wires`, `child_wires`:* both precise about joining siblings.
13277	  "Peer" has no footing in the spec, and "child wires" reads as the
13278	  children's own wires.
13279	- *`internal_wires`, `intra_wires`:* keep the `in` prefix, so they keep the
13280	  collision.
13281	- *`input_aliases`/`output_aliases` beside the rename:* exposes the
13282	  virtual nature of the boundary entries in the API, at the price of the
13283	  uniform "wire" story, [D-303][d-303]'s rule, the glossary and every diagnostic
13284	  that pairs a local wire with an `input_wires` chain. One rename suffices
13285	  for readability, and the aliasing fact already has a home in the prose.
