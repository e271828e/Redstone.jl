# The rendering spike: axis 4 of the inspector's stage 1

A throwaway prototype, run beside the descriptor increment, that settles the
inspector's canvas choice and tells the schema what the drawing needs before
version one freezes. It is retired when the inspector repo's generated
fixtures land; its report and its fixture are what survive.

## What it decides

1. **The canvas: hand-rolled SVG or Svelte Flow**, both over ELK.js layout.
   `initial_design.md` answer 11 fixes the stack (TypeScript, Svelte, ELK.js)
   and leaves the canvas to this spike. Answer 12 fixes the layout rules:
   one level at a time, child assemblies as closed blocks, ties broken by
   declaration order, previous positions as hints on a new push, a drag
   that lasts until reload.
2. **Performance on the largest expected subsystem.** The longitudinal
   example's `closed_loop` is one flat level of about 30 blocks and 60
   wires. Measure layout and render time there, and on a synthetic level
   of about 150 blocks and 300 wires, since a real flight model's flat
   levels will be larger than the example.
3. **What the drawing needs that the schema lacks.** Every field the canvas
   wanted and had to derive, guess or omit is a finding. This is the
   spike's main output for the increment.

## What it reads

- `design/inspector/descriptor_design.md`, whole: the format's answers, the
  node entry (answer 4), the wires (answer 5), the kind marker (answer 6),
  the fixture plan (answer 16).
- `design/inspector/initial_design.md` lines 189 to 215, answers 11 and 12,
  and lines 357 to 362, axis 4.
- `design/prototypes/render_spike/schema.json`, a frozen copy of the
  schema draft the increment is building against. The fixture validates
  against this copy with ajv. The increment's copy may move while the
  spike runs; the report names any divergence it learns of, and nothing
  else.
- `examples/src/longitudinal.jl`: `closed_loop`, `aircraft` and `descent`,
  the models the fixtures transcribe.

## The fixture

A descriptor of kind `build` for `closed_loop`, written by hand against the
schema. Transcribe it from the model's source and from the `Build`'s own
rendering, printed read-only from the repository root:

    julia --project=examples design/prototypes/render_spike/print_builds.jl

which writes `build_closed_loop.txt`, `build_descent.txt` and
`build_aircraft.txt` beside it (they may already be there). The wires list
in the source is the level's wires verbatim. Kind markers, types and signatures may be
approximate where the source does not show them, since the fixture's job
is to exercise the drawing, not the writer. Add a second, small fixture
for `descent`, two levels deep (the root `Group` holding `plant`, `gear`
and `stop`), to exercise a root with input wires.

Keep the fixtures under `fixtures/`, validate them with ajv in a script
(`npm run validate`), and keep the schema copy beside them.

## The prototypes

Two canvases over one ELK layout module, in one Svelte project under this
directory, selectable at the page:

- hand-rolled SVG: ELK's output drawn as `<g>`, `<rect>`, `<path>`;
- Svelte Flow (`@xyflow/svelte`) with ELK positions fed in.

Both draw the same thing, the minimum that exercises the layout rules: a
block per node with its last path segment and its type's last segment, a
port per face on the block's side, right-angle wires through ELK's edge
routing, a drag that moves a block and re-routes its wires, and one level
at a time with assemblies as closed blocks (the `descent` fixture gives one
level with three leaves; `closed_loop` one level with about 30). A click on
a block logs its node to the console; a detail pane is out of scope. Color
leaves by tier, continuous against discrete, and nothing more of axis 3's
grammar.

Measure, in the browser, ELK layout time and first render time for each
canvas on both fixtures and on the synthetic level, generated by a script
from the schema's node shape. Record the numbers in the report.

## The report

`report.md` in this directory, with:

1. the canvas choice and the reasons, with the measurements in a table;
2. the schema findings, one bullet each: the field wanted, what the canvas
   did instead, and the proposed addition or change, marked additive or
   breaking;
3. what the spike did not test.

## Rules

- Everything the spike creates lives under `design/prototypes/render_spike/`.
  Nothing under `src/`, `test/`, `schema/` or `examples/` changes.
- `node` and `npm` are Homebrew's, on the path. Dependencies install into
  this directory's `node_modules`, which the repo ignores.
- No commits: the coordinator commits the spike's files after reading the
  report.
- Run `julia` read-only, from the repository root, only to print tables.
  Never run the test suite.
- Use `/bin/ls` or `fd`, never `ls`, which is aliased to a tool that can
  hang a finished shell.
- The type checker runs clean (`npm run check`) before the report is
  written.
